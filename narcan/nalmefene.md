# Nalmefene (Opvee, Zurnai)

> If someone may be overdosing on an opioid, **call 911 and give naloxone or nalmefene.**

## What it is

Nalmefene is an opioid antagonist like naloxone, but it lasts much longer. **Opvee** is a nasal spray approved by the FDA in May 2023, and **Zurnai** is an auto-injector approved in 2024. Both are prescription products for emergency use.

## History

Nalmefene was developed in the 1970s and approved as an injection (Revex) in 1995; Revex was discontinued in 2008. In Europe, nalmefene tablets (Selincro) are used to reduce alcohol drinking. The fentanyl crisis renewed interest because a longer-acting reversal could help prevent re-overdose.

## How it works and how it differs from naloxone

Like naloxone, it pushes opioids off their receptors and restores breathing. Its effect lasts many hours (its half-life is about 11 hours, compared with about 1 hour for naloxone), so the overdose is less likely to return.

## What reversal feels like

The person wakes into withdrawal, as with naloxone. Because nalmefene lasts longer, the withdrawal can also last longer, sometimes many hours, with vomiting, sweats, agitation, and severe discomfort. Some experts worry this is harder on people than naloxone.

## What bystanders should expect

The same as naloxone: give it, support breathing, put them in the recovery position, call 911, and stay with them.

## Getting it

Opvee and Zurnai require a prescription. Naloxone is the easiest to get and works well.
