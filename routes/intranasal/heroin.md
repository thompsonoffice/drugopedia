# Heroin (diacetylmorphine): Intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## About this route

Absorbed through the nasal lining into the bloodstream, faster than swallowing. For Heroin (diacetylmorphine): Snorting, common with powder heroin; medical diamorphine nasal spray is used for children's pain in UK emergency departments.

## How this route feels different

A noticeably quicker and sharper onset than swallowing, with a mild rush or 'drop' of warmth, a bitter drip in the throat, and a shorter overall duration.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (5-15 min)

Smoked, heroin acts within seconds; injected, within seconds to a minute; snorted, a few minutes. The first feeling is a rush of warmth from the stomach up through the chest into the head.

### Come-up (10-20 min)

Injected, the rush is described as overwhelming: a hot flush, a sense of release, heavy limbs, and sometimes nausea or vomiting. Smoked or snorted, it is gentler and builds over minutes.

### Peak (15-45 min)

The peak is the deep heroin calm: complete safety and warmth, pain and worry gone, heavy eyelids, pinpoint pupils, dry mouth, and strong itch. Many describe it as the most comforting feeling of their lives.

### Plateau (1-2 h)

The plateau is classic nodding: drifting in and out, chin dropping, dreamy visions, then waking with a start, for hours.

### Comedown (2-4 h)

After 3 to 5 hours, the comedown starts: warmth fades, and in dependent people craving and restlessness begin.

### After-effects (4-12 h)

Constipation, dry mouth, and fatigue. With street supply now dominated by fentanyl, duration and intensity are unpredictable.

### Next day

After regular use, withdrawal begins 6 to 12 hours after last use and peaks at 1 to 3 days: sweats, chills, goosebumps, aches, diarrhea, vomiting, restless legs, and insomnia.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Heroin (diacetylmorphine) page](../../drugs/heroin.md).

## Risks of this route

Faster, stronger peak than swallowing, raising overdose risk. Damage to nasal tissue, nosebleeds, and sinus infections. Shared straws can spread hepatitis C. Crushing pills meant to be swallowed releases extended-release reserves and binders that harm tissue.

## Overdose note

Since street heroin now usually contains fentanyl, overdose risk is unpredictable. Overdoses cluster after time away from use (jail, rehab, hospital), when tolerance is lower.

## Signs of an opioid overdose

- Breathing that is very slow, shallow, gurgling, or has stopped. Snoring-like sounds can be a warning sign, not sleep.
- Cannot be woken, or does not respond to a loud voice or a firm rub on the breastbone.
- Pinpoint pupils.
- Blue, gray, or ashen lips and fingertips; cold, clammy skin.
- Limp body.

## What to do

- Call 911. Many US states have Good Samaritan laws that protect people who call for help.
- Give naloxone (Narcan or another brand) if it is available. It is sold over the counter in US pharmacies. It can wear off before the opioid does, so overdose can return; stay with the person.
- If they are breathing, put them on their side (recovery position) so they do not choke.
- Never leave the person alone to "sleep it off."

Read more: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../../safety/opioid-overdose.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
