# Hydrocodone: Intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## About this route

Absorbed through the nasal lining into the bloodstream, faster than swallowing. For Hydrocodone: Misused by snorting; acetaminophen fillers make it painful and harmful to nasal tissue.

## How this route feels different

A noticeably quicker and sharper onset than swallowing, with a mild rush or 'drop' of warmth, a bitter drip in the throat, and a shorter overall duration.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (5-15 min)

Hydrocodone usually starts 15 to 30 minutes after swallowing with a pleasant warmth in the chest and a lift in mood, sometimes before any body heaviness. Many describe a sudden sense that the day has gotten easier.

### Come-up (10-20 min)

The come-up brings a talkative, friendly glow. People report wanting to chat, clean, or do small tasks, a 'productive buzz' lighter than oxycodone.

### Peak (15-45 min)

At the peak, around 1 to 1.5 hours, hydrocodone gives a warm, contented, sociable euphoria with a relaxed, comfortable body. Compared with oxycodone, people describe it as softer, dreamier, and less sharp.

### Plateau (1-2 h)

The plateau tilts toward sleepiness: the chatty phase gives way to quiet contentment and light nodding, especially when lying down.

### Comedown (2-4 h)

The comedown, around 4 to 6 hours, is gradual. People describe a slide into tiredness and mild irritability as the warmth leaves, and some feel the urge to take more, which is how use escalates.

### After-effects (4-12 h)

After-effects include constipation, dry mouth, and sometimes headache. Because many products contain acetaminophen, repeated use can damage the liver without warning signs.

### Next day

After a single use, the next day is usually normal. After regular use, withdrawal appears within a day: anxiety, sweats, yawning, aches, and strong cravings.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Hydrocodone page](../../drugs/hydrocodone.md).

## Risks of this route

Faster, stronger peak than swallowing, raising overdose risk. Damage to nasal tissue, nosebleeds, and sinus infections. Shared straws can spread hepatitis C. Crushing pills meant to be swallowed releases extended-release reserves and binders that harm tissue.

## Overdose note

Acetaminophen combination products can cause fatal liver failure. Extended-release forms (Zohydro, Hysingla) hold a large reserve that is released all at once if damaged.

## Signs of an opioid overdose

- Breathing that is very slow, shallow, gurgling, or has stopped. Snoring-like sounds can be a warning sign, not sleep.
- Cannot be woken, or does not respond to a loud voice or a firm rub on the breastbone.
- Pinpoint pupils.
- Blue, gray, or ashen lips and fingertips; cold, clammy skin.
- Limp body.

## What to do

- Call 911. Many US states have Good Samaritan laws that protect people who call for help.
- Give naloxone (Narcan or another brand) if it is available. It is sold over the counter in US pharmacies. It can wear off before the opioid does, so overdose can return; stay with the person.
- If they are breathing, put them on their side (recovery position) so they do not choke.
- Never leave the person alone to "sleep it off."

Read more: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../../safety/opioid-overdose.md). See also [naloxone (Narcan)](../../narcan/naloxone.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
