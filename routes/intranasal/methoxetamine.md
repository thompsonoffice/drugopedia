# Methoxetamine (MXE): Intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Absorbed through the nasal lining into the bloodstream, faster than swallowing. For Methoxetamine (MXE): Snorted powder.

## How this route feels different

Snorted: faster onset than swallowing; damages the nose and sinuses.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (10-30 min)

Onset is slower than ketamine, often 10 to 30 minutes snorted.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Gradual detachment.

### Peak (1-2 h)

Peak 1 to 2 hours.

### Plateau (2-3 h)

Long plateau.

### Comedown (3-5 h)

Fades over several hours.

### After-effects (5-12 h)

Low mood and fatigue.

### Next day

Next-day fog.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Methoxetamine (MXE) page](../../drugs/methoxetamine.md).

## Risks of this route

Nasal damage, nosebleeds, sinus infections, and shared straws can spread hepatitis C.

## Overdose note

Emergencies include agitation, high blood pressure, seizures and catatonia; deaths often involved other drugs.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Unresponsive, or breathing slowly or noisily.
- Vomiting while dazed.
- Very high temperature, seizures, or extreme agitation (especially PCP or DXM).
- Injury they cannot feel.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect callers.
- Put them in the recovery position and keep them away from water, stairs and roads.
- Give naloxone if opioids may be involved.

Read more: [Dissociative safety: K-holes, injuries, bladder damage, mixing](../../safety/dissociative-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
