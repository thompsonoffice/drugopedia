# Tramadol: Rectal (suppositories)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## About this route

Given as suppositories in medicine, mainly when a patient cannot swallow. Absorption is partly protected from first-pass metabolism and varies a lot. For Tramadol: Suppositories exist in some countries.

## How this route feels different

Onset sits between swallowing and snorting. It is mostly used medically; experiences vary because absorption is uneven.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (10-30 min)

Tramadol starts around 30 to 60 minutes after swallowing, often with a light, slightly stimulating lift in mood and subtle body warmth.

### Come-up (20-40 min)

The come-up feels energetic and talkative for many, more like an antidepressant than an opioid. Some feel restless, sweaty, or nauseous.

### Peak (45-90 min)

The peak, around 2 to 3 hours, is mild euphoria with gentle body warmth and brightened mood. Less sedating than most opioids.

### Plateau (1-3 h)

The plateau is steady and functional. Sedation is light; some feel wired.

### Comedown (3-6 h)

The comedown, after 6 to 8 hours, can bring irritability and low mood.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Sweating, dizziness, and constipation. Seizure risk is present throughout.

### Next day

Next day can bring a mood dip. Withdrawal includes typical opioid symptoms plus odd ones: tingling, panic, confusion, and sometimes hallucinations.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Tramadol page](../../drugs/tramadol.md).

## Risks of this route

Absorption is unpredictable, so the effect can be stronger or weaker than expected. Irritation and tissue injury are possible.

## Overdose note

Tramadol can cause seizures, even at ordinary amounts and more in overdose or with other drugs that lower the seizure threshold. Combined with antidepressants and other serotonergic drugs it can cause serotonin syndrome (agitation, high fever, muscle twitching, confusion). Naloxone reverses breathing suppression but not seizures.

## Signs of an opioid overdose

- Breathing that is very slow, shallow, gurgling, or has stopped. Snoring-like sounds can be a warning sign, not sleep.
- Cannot be woken, or does not respond to a loud voice or a firm rub on the breastbone.
- Pinpoint pupils.
- Blue, gray, or ashen lips and fingertips; cold, clammy skin.
- Limp body.

## What to do

- Call 911. Many US states have Good Samaritan laws that protect people who call for help.
- Give naloxone (Narcan or another brand) if it is available. It is sold over the counter in US pharmacies. It can wear off before the opioid does, so overdose can return; stay with the person.
- If they are breathing, put them on their side (recovery position) so they do not choke.
- Never leave the person alone to "sleep it off."

Read more: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../../safety/opioid-overdose.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
