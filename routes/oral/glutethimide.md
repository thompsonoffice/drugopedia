# Glutethimide (Doriden): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For Glutethimide (Doriden): Historic tablets or capsules.

## How this route feels different

Swallowing is the standard medical route. Effects build gradually; food can slow onset.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (about 30 min)

Glutethimide began within about 30 minutes, with heavy sedation and a very dry mouth.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Clumsiness and drowsiness.

### Peak (1-2 h)

The peak was barbiturate-like; with codeine ('loads') it greatly deepened opioid effects.

### Plateau (2-6 h)

Several hours.

### Comedown (many hours)

It faded over many hours, and its fat solubility made overdoses last days.

### After-effects (1-2 days)

Confusion, dry mouth.

### Next day (hangover)

Hangover.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Glutethimide (Doriden) page](../../drugs/glutethimide.md).

## Risks of this route

Slow onset tempts people to take more before the first amount peaks. Long-acting drugs build up over days. Mixing with alcohol, opioids, or benzodiazepines is the main cause of deaths.

## Overdose note

Overdose caused deep coma, low blood pressure, and slowed breathing that could last for days.

## Signs of dangerous sedation or overdose

- Cannot be woken, or very confused and slurring.
- Slow, shallow, or stopped breathing; snoring or gurgling.
- Blue or gray lips; cold, clammy skin.
- Seizures or vomiting while drowsy.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect people who call.
- Give naloxone if opioids might be involved; it will not reverse these drugs but can reverse an opioid part.
- Put them in the recovery position on their side and stay with them.

Read more: [Mixing depressants: why combinations kill and what to do](../../safety/depressant-mixing.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
