# Mephedrone (4-MMC): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For Mephedrone (4-MMC): Swallowed.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (15-45 min)

Snorted, effects start within 10 to 20 minutes.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

A fast rise of euphoria.

### Peak (1-2 h)

Peak within 30 to 60 minutes.

### Plateau (2-3 h)

Effects last 1 to 2 hours.

### Comedown (3-5 h)

Comedown with craving.

### After-effects (hours)

Anxiety and insomnia.

### Next day

Low mood for a day or two.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Mephedrone (4-MMC) page](../../drugs/mephedrone.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

Overdose risks include overheating, seizures, heart problems and psychosis.

## Signs of a stimulant emergency

- Chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or severe headache.
- Very high body temperature, hot dry skin, or confusion.
- Seizures, extreme agitation, or paranoia.
- Sudden weakness or trouble speaking (possible stroke).
- Drowsiness and slow breathing (possible fentanyl contamination).

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect callers.
- Move them somewhere cool, loosen clothing, and use cool water on the skin.
- Keep them calm; do not restrain them.
- Give naloxone if they are unresponsive or breathing slowly, since stimulants are often contaminated with fentanyl.

Read more: [Stimulant safety: overheating, heart risks, psychosis, fentanyl contamination](../../safety/stimulant-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
