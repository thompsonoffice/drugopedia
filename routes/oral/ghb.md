# GHB and sodium oxybate (Xyrem, Xywav, Lumryz): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For GHB and sodium oxybate (Xyrem, Xywav, Lumryz): Liquid medicine (sodium oxybate); illicit GHB is usually a salty or soapy-tasting clear liquid.

## How this route feels different

Swallowing is the standard medical route. Effects build gradually; food can slow onset.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (10-20 min)

Swallowed, effects begin within about 10 to 20 minutes.

### Come-up (15-30 min)

A fast, warm, loosening wave like several drinks at once.

### Peak (30-60 min)

Peak around 30 to 60 minutes: euphoria, sociability, sometimes dizziness and sudden sleepiness.

### Plateau (30-90 min)

A short plateau.

### Comedown (1.5-3 h total)

Effects fade within about 1.5 to 3 hours, sometimes with abrupt waking.

### After-effects (3-6 h)

Tiredness, sometimes a clear-headed 'reset' feeling, or nausea.

### Next day (usually little)

Little next-day effect unless used repeatedly.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general GHB and sodium oxybate (Xyrem, Xywav, Lumryz) page](../../drugs/ghb.md).

## Risks of this route

Slow onset tempts people to take more before the first amount peaks. Long-acting drugs build up over days. Mixing with alcohol, opioids, or benzodiazepines is the main cause of deaths.

## Overdose note

Overdose causes sudden unconsciousness, vomiting, slow breathing, slow heartbeat and low body temperature. With alcohol, the risk is much greater. Never leave an unconscious person on their back; use the recovery position and call 911.

## Signs of dangerous sedation or overdose

- Cannot be woken, or very confused and slurring.
- Slow, shallow, or stopped breathing; snoring or gurgling.
- Blue or gray lips; cold, clammy skin.
- Seizures or vomiting while drowsy.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect people who call.
- Give naloxone if opioids might be involved; it will not reverse these drugs but can reverse an opioid part.
- Put them in the recovery position on their side and stay with them.

Read more: [Mixing depressants: why combinations kill and what to do](../../safety/depressant-mixing.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
