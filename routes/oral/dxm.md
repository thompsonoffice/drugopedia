# DXM (dextromethorphan): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For DXM (dextromethorphan): Syrups, capsules, tablets.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30-60 min)

Effects begin 30 minutes to 1 hour after swallowing, often with nausea and warmth.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

A drunk, giddy feeling and heavy limbs build.

### Peak (2-4 h)

The peak, 2 to 4 hours in, brings the strongest dissociation and altered thinking.

### Plateau (3-6 h)

The plateau lasts several hours.

### Comedown (6-12 h)

Comedown is long, over 6 hours or more.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Fatigue, low mood and unsteadiness.

### Next day

A hangover-like fog often continues the next day.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general DXM (dextromethorphan) page](../../drugs/dxm.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

DXM poisoning causes agitation, high temperature, fast heart rate, seizures and breathing problems; combination products add acetaminophen liver failure and antihistamine toxicity.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Unresponsive, or breathing slowly or noisily.
- Vomiting while dazed.
- Very high temperature, seizures, or extreme agitation (especially PCP or DXM).
- Injury they cannot feel.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect callers.
- Put them in the recovery position and keep them away from water, stairs and roads.
- Give naloxone if opioids may be involved.

Read more: [Dissociative safety: K-holes, injuries, bladder damage, mixing](../../safety/dissociative-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
