# Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) as misused: Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) as misused: Tablets, capsules, liquids.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30-60 min)

Drowsiness begins within 30 to 60 minutes.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Dry mouth, fast heartbeat, and confusion at higher amounts.

### Peak (2-4 h)

Peak delirium at 2 to 4 hours.

### Plateau (4-8 h)

Delirium can last many hours.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

Long comedown with confusion.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Grogginess and memory gaps.

### Next day

Next-day fog.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) as misused page](../../drugs/diphenhydramine.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

Diphenhydramine overdose causes delirium, seizures, high temperature, dangerous heart rhythms and death.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Confusion, talking to people who are not there.
- Hot, dry, flushed skin; very fast heartbeat.
- Seizures or collapse.

## What to do

- Call 911 or Poison Control (1-800-222-1222).
- Keep them safe and calm; they may not know where they are.

Read more: [Deliriant safety](../../safety/deliriant-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
