# LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide): Blotter paper, liquid, or gel tabs held in the mouth and swallowed.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30-90 min)

Effects begin 30 to 90 minutes after taking it: restlessness, yawning, and a shift in how things look.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Over the next hour colors brighten and patterns appear; emotions intensify.

### Peak (3-5 h)

The peak, about 3 to 5 hours in, brings the strongest visual and mental changes and loss of the usual sense of time.

### Plateau (5-8 h)

Effects remain strong for several hours.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

From about 8 hours, effects taper slowly over several more hours.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Difficulty sleeping and a reflective afterglow are common.

### Next day

Tiredness the next day; tolerance lasts several days.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide) page](../../drugs/lsd.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

There is no known human death from LSD toxicity alone at typical levels; deaths result from accidents, suicide, or substances sold as LSD (especially NBOMes).

## Signs that someone needs help

- Seizures, very high temperature, or chest pain (especially with NBOMes or 2C drugs).
- Severe panic that does not calm with reassurance, or danger to self or others.
- Unresponsiveness.

## What to do

- Call 911 for physical symptoms. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect callers.
- Move to a quiet, safe place and speak calmly.
- Stay with them; do not leave them alone.

Read more: [Psychedelic safety](../../safety/psychedelic-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
