# MDMA (ecstasy, molly): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For MDMA (ecstasy, molly): Pills, capsules or crystals.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30-60 min)

Swallowed, effects begin 30 to 60 minutes later, often with nausea and a rush of anxiety.

### Come-up (1-1.5 h)

A wave of warmth and energy builds.

### Peak (1.5-2.5 h)

The peak at 1.5 to 2.5 hours brings maximal euphoria and empathy.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

Plateau about 1 to 2 hours.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

Comedown over 2 hours with fatigue.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Low mood and jaw ache.

### Next day (2-4 days)

Low mood for days in some people.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general MDMA (ecstasy, molly) page](../../drugs/mdma.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

MDMA deaths are mostly from overheating, low blood sodium (from drinking too much water plus MDMA's hormone effects), serotonin syndrome and heart problems.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Very high temperature, confusion, or stopping sweating.
- Severe headache, vomiting and confusion after drinking lots of water (low sodium).
- Muscle rigidity, jerking, or seizures.
- Collapse.

## What to do

- Call 911 and tell responders what was taken.
- Move to a cool place; cool the skin.
- Do not give large amounts of water.

Read more: [Empathogen (MDMA) safety](../../safety/empathogen-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
