# Chlordiazepoxide (Librium): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Never stop a daily benzodiazepine suddenly: withdrawal can cause seizures. Counterfeit pills often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For Chlordiazepoxide (Librium): Capsules.

## How this route feels different

Swallowing is the standard medical route. Effects build gradually, with no rush; food can slow onset.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30-60 min)

Begins 30 to 60 minutes after swallowing.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Slow build of relaxation.

### Peak (1-4 h)

Mild peak at 1 to 4 hours.

### Plateau (4-12 h)

Long plateau.

### Comedown (12-48 h)

Very slow fade.

### After-effects (1-3 days)

Next-day sleepiness.

### Next day (still active)

Long-lasting metabolites make withdrawal slow-onset.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Chlordiazepoxide (Librium) page](../../drugs/chlordiazepoxide.md).

## Risks of this route

Slow onset tempts people to take more before the first amount peaks. Long-acting drugs and their metabolites build up over days, especially in older adults, causing falls, confusion, and impaired driving. Counterfeit pills may contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines.

## Overdose note

Accumulation in older adults and those with liver disease. Dangerous with alcohol, which is especially relevant given its use in alcohol withdrawal.

## Signs of a benzodiazepine overdose or dangerous sedation

- Extreme drowsiness, cannot be woken, or slurred, confused speech.
- Stumbling, poor coordination, or collapse.
- Slow or shallow breathing, especially if opioids or alcohol are involved.
- Blue or gray lips and fingertips.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect people who call for help.
- If opioids may be involved (including counterfeit pills), give naloxone; it will not reverse the benzo but can reverse the opioid part.
- Put them on their side in the recovery position and stay with them.

Read more: [Benzodiazepine safety: overdose, mixing, blackouts, and withdrawal](../../safety/benzodiazepine-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
