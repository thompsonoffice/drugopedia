# Antidepressants as misused (bupropion, trazodone, amitriptyline): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For Antidepressants as misused (bupropion, trazodone, amitriptyline): Tablets.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30-60 min)

Effects begin within an hour.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Stimulation (bupropion) or sedation (trazodone, amitriptyline).

### Peak (2-4 h)

Peak at 2 to 4 hours.

### Plateau (4-8 h)

Several hours.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

Fade.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Lingering effects.

### Next day

Next-day effects.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Antidepressants as misused (bupropion, trazodone, amitriptyline) page](../../drugs/antidepressants-misused.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

Bupropion overdose causes seizures; tricyclic overdose causes seizures, coma and fatal arrhythmias.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Seizures, unresponsiveness, chest pain, or severe confusion.

## What to do

- Call 911 or Poison Control (1-800-222-1222).
- Recovery position if drowsy.

Read more: [Other psychoactives safety](../../safety/other-psychoactives-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
