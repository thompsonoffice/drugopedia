# Non-beverage alcohols (methanol, isopropyl, hand sanitizer, mouthwash): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For Non-beverage alcohols (methanol, isopropyl, hand sanitizer, mouthwash): Swallowed.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30-60 min)

Intoxication within 30 to 60 minutes.

### Come-up (1 h)

Drunkenness similar to ethanol.

### Peak (1-2 h)

Ethanol-like peak; methanol's real toxicity is delayed.

### Plateau (2-6 h)

Hours later, vision changes and breathing difficulty (methanol).

### Comedown (12-24 h)

Severe illness 12 to 24 hours after methanol.

### After-effects (days)

Permanent blindness or brain damage possible.

### Next day (permanent harm possible)

Requires hospital care.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Non-beverage alcohols (methanol, isopropyl, hand sanitizer, mouthwash) page](../../drugs/alcohol-nonbeverage.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

Methanol poisoning causes blindness, brain damage and death; anyone who may have drunk methanol needs emergency care even if they feel fine.

## Signs of alcohol poisoning

- Cannot be woken.
- Slow or irregular breathing.
- Vomiting while passed out.
- Cold, clammy, pale or bluish skin; seizures.

## What to do

- Call 911. Do not let them 'sleep it off'.
- Put them in the recovery position.
- Stay with them. Cold showers and coffee do not help.

Read more: [Alcohol safety](../../safety/alcohol-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
