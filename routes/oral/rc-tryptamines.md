# Research-chemical tryptamines (4-AcO-DMT, 4-HO-MET, AMT, 5-MeO-MiPT, DPT): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For Research-chemical tryptamines (4-AcO-DMT, 4-HO-MET, AMT, 5-MeO-MiPT, DPT): Capsules or powder.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (20 min-3 h)

Onset varies from 20 minutes to 3 hours by drug.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Body load and visuals build.

### Peak (2-6 h)

Peaks range from 2 to 6 hours.

### Plateau (2-8 h)

Plateau varies.

### Comedown (4-16 h)

Taper varies.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Fatigue.

### Next day

AMT may last into next day.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Research-chemical tryptamines (4-AcO-DMT, 4-HO-MET, AMT, 5-MeO-MiPT, DPT) page](../../drugs/rc-tryptamines.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

Risks include overheating, serotonin syndrome (especially AMT with other drugs), and accidents.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Seizures, very high temperature, or chest pain (especially with NBOMes or 2C drugs).
- Severe panic that does not calm with reassurance, or danger to self or others.
- Unresponsiveness.

## What to do

- Call 911 for physical symptoms. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect callers.
- Move to a quiet, safe place and speak calmly.
- Stay with them; do not leave them alone.

Read more: [Psychedelic safety](../../safety/psychedelic-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
