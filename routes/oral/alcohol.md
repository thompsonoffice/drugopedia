# Alcohol (ethanol): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For Alcohol (ethanol): Drunk.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (10-30 min)

Effects begin within 10 to 30 minutes, faster on an empty stomach: warmth and relaxation.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

As blood alcohol rises, talkativeness and confidence increase.

### Peak (30-90 min)

Peak at 30 to 90 minutes after drinking stops, with the greatest impairment.

### Plateau (depends on drinking)

Impairment continues as long as drinking continues.

### Comedown (hours)

Blood alcohol falls at a roughly fixed rate; sleepiness follows.

### After-effects (next morning)

Hangover: headache, nausea, dehydration and anxiety.

### Next day

Next-day impaired concentration; daily drinkers may have withdrawal tremor.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Alcohol (ethanol) page](../../drugs/alcohol.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

Alcohol poisoning causes vomiting, confusion, slow breathing, low temperature, seizures and death.

## Signs of alcohol poisoning

- Cannot be woken.
- Slow or irregular breathing.
- Vomiting while passed out.
- Cold, clammy, pale or bluish skin; seizures.

## What to do

- Call 911. Do not let them 'sleep it off'.
- Put them in the recovery position.
- Stay with them. Cold showers and coffee do not help.

Read more: [Alcohol safety](../../safety/alcohol-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
