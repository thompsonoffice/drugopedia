# HHC (hexahydrocannabinol): Oral (swallowed)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Swallowed as tablets, capsules, or liquids. The drug passes through the gut and liver first (first-pass metabolism), which lowers and slows what reaches the brain. For HHC (hexahydrocannabinol): Gummies.

## How this route feels different

Swallowed: slower onset and longer effects; harder to judge, which tempts people to take more.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30 min-2 h)

Inhaled, effects begin within minutes; edibles take 30 minutes to 2 hours.

### Come-up (1-3 h)

Relaxation builds.

### Peak (2-4 h)

Peak with typical cannabis effects.

### Plateau (4-6 h)

Plateau about an hour (vape) or several hours (edible).

### Comedown (6-10 h)

Gradual fade.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Dry mouth and sleepiness.

### Next day

Possible next-day fog.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general HHC (hexahydrocannabinol) page](../../drugs/hhc.md).

## Risks of this route

Delayed effects tempt redosing; edibles and long-acting pills are a common cause of accidental overdose and emergency visits.

## Overdose note

Too much causes anxiety, vomiting, confusion and fast heart rate.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Panic, chest pain, fainting, or a very fast heartbeat.
- Severe confusion, paranoia, or hallucinations.
- Repeated vomiting.
- A child who may have eaten an edible.
- With synthetic cannabinoids: seizures, unresponsiveness, or violent agitation.

## What to do

- Stay calm, move to a quiet place, and reassure them.
- Call 911 for chest pain, seizures, unresponsiveness, or a child. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.
- Use the recovery position if they are drowsy and vomiting.

Read more: [Cannabis safety: edibles, high-potency products, synthetic cannabinoids](../../safety/cannabis-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
