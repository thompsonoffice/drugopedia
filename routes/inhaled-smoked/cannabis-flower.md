# Cannabis flower (marijuana): Inhaled and smoked

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Vapor or smoke absorbed through the lungs, reaching the brain within seconds. For Cannabis flower (marijuana): Smoked in joints or pipes, or vaporized.

## How this route feels different

Smoked or vaped: effects in seconds to minutes, shorter duration, and strong reinforcement; damages the lungs and airways.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (seconds to minutes)

Inhaled, effects begin within minutes: a warm head rush, faster heartbeat, and lightness.

### Come-up (5-15 min)

Over 10 to 15 minutes relaxation and laughter build; senses sharpen.

### Peak (15-30 min)

The peak, at about 15 to 30 minutes, brings the strongest time distortion, memory gaps and appetite.

### Plateau (30-60 min)

The high plateaus for about an hour.

### Comedown (1-3 h)

It fades over 2 to 3 hours into sleepiness.

### After-effects (2-4 h)

Dry mouth, hunger, and a mild fog.

### Next day (next morning)

Some feel slow or foggy the next morning; daily users may feel irritable without it.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Cannabis flower (marijuana) page](../../drugs/cannabis-flower.md).

## Risks of this route

Lung and airway damage, burns, and very fast, compulsive use patterns.

## Overdose note

Cannabis alone is not known to cause fatal overdose, but it causes panic, fainting, vomiting, psychosis, and impaired-driving crashes.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Panic, chest pain, fainting, or a very fast heartbeat.
- Severe confusion, paranoia, or hallucinations.
- Repeated vomiting.
- A child who may have eaten an edible.
- With synthetic cannabinoids: seizures, unresponsiveness, or violent agitation.

## What to do

- Stay calm, move to a quiet place, and reassure them.
- Call 911 for chest pain, seizures, unresponsiveness, or a child. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.
- Use the recovery position if they are drowsy and vomiting.

Read more: [Cannabis safety: edibles, high-potency products, synthetic cannabinoids](../../safety/cannabis-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
