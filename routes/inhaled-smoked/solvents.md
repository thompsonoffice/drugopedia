# Solvents and gases (glue, gasoline, lighter fluid, paint thinner): Inhaled and smoked

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Vapor or smoke absorbed through the lungs, reaching the brain within seconds. For Solvents and gases (glue, gasoline, lighter fluid, paint thinner): Vapors inhaled.

## How this route feels different

Smoked or vaped: effects in seconds to minutes, shorter duration, and strong reinforcement; damages the lungs and airways.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (seconds)

Effects begin within seconds.

### Come-up (seconds-minutes)

Dizziness and giddiness rise quickly.

### Peak (1-5 min)

The peak lasts minutes.

### Plateau (minutes)

Users often repeat to keep effects going.

### Comedown (15-45 min)

Fades within minutes to an hour.

### After-effects (hours)

Headache, nausea and drowsiness.

### Next day

Hangover; long-term damage with repeated use.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Solvents and gases (glue, gasoline, lighter fluid, paint thinner) page](../../drugs/solvents.md).

## Risks of this route

Lung and airway damage, burns, and very fast, compulsive use patterns.

## Overdose note

Sudden sniffing death, suffocation, burns and choking are the main dangers; there is no safe way to inhale solvents.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Unconscious, not breathing normally, or no pulse.
- Chest pain, irregular heartbeat, or collapse.
- Seizures or severe confusion.

## What to do

- Call 911 and start CPR if they are not breathing.
- Do not chase or startle someone who is intoxicated on inhalants; a sudden adrenaline surge can stop the heart.
- Get them into fresh air and remove any bag or covering.

Read more: [Inhalant safety: sudden sniffing death, poppers, nitrous oxide](../../safety/inhalant-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
