# Midazolam (Versed): Sublingual and buccal (under the tongue or in the cheek)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Never stop a daily benzodiazepine suddenly: withdrawal can cause seizures. Counterfeit pills often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines.

## About this route

Absorbed through the lining of the mouth, partly skipping first-pass metabolism. For Midazolam (Versed): Buccal solution (Europe).

## How this route feels different

Under the tongue, some benzodiazepines start a little faster than when swallowed. Medically used for alprazolam, lorazepam, and others in some countries, and buccal midazolam for seizures.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (5-15 min)

IV: 1 to 5 minutes; nasal: about 5 to 10 minutes.

### Come-up (immediate)

Very quick heaviness.

### Peak (15-30 min)

Peak within minutes.

### Plateau (under 1 h)

Short: about 1 to 2 hours.

### Comedown (1-3 h total)

Fades fast.

### After-effects (3-8 h)

Amnesia, grogginess.

### Next day (usually none)

Usually no next-day effect.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Midazolam (Versed) page](../../drugs/midazolam.md).

## Risks of this route

Faster onset than swallowing for some drugs, with a sharper drop in alertness and memory.

## Overdose note

Dangerous with opioids and alcohol; can cause blackouts, falls, and stopped breathing in combination. Withdrawal seizures if stopped suddenly after daily use.

## Signs of a benzodiazepine overdose or dangerous sedation

- Extreme drowsiness, cannot be woken, or slurred, confused speech.
- Stumbling, poor coordination, or collapse.
- Slow or shallow breathing, especially if opioids or alcohol are involved.
- Blue or gray lips and fingertips.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect people who call for help.
- If opioids may be involved (including counterfeit pills), give naloxone; it will not reverse the benzo but can reverse the opioid part.
- Put them on their side in the recovery position and stay with them.

Read more: [Benzodiazepine safety: overdose, mixing, blackouts, and withdrawal](../../safety/benzodiazepine-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
