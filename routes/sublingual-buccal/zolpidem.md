# Zolpidem (Ambien): Sublingual and buccal (under the tongue or in the cheek)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## About this route

Absorbed through the lining of the mouth, partly skipping first-pass metabolism. For Zolpidem (Ambien): Sublingual tablets (Edluar, Intermezzo).

## How this route feels different

Under-the-tongue forms exist for a few sleep medicines and act a little faster than swallowed tablets.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (10-20 min)

Swallowed, zolpidem usually starts within about 15 to 30 minutes.

### Come-up (20-30 min)

A rapid heaviness and slowed thinking.

### Peak (about 1 h)

Peak around 1 to 2 hours: strong sleepiness; awake people may have visual distortions and amnesia.

### Plateau (2-3 h)

Sleep through most of the effect.

### Comedown (4-6 h total)

Effects fade over about 6 to 8 hours (immediate-release).

### After-effects (6-8 h)

Possible grogginess.

### Next day (little)

Next-morning driving impairment is possible, especially in women.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Zolpidem (Ambien) page](../../drugs/zolpidem.md).

## Risks of this route

Slightly faster onset, so take it only in bed.

## Overdose note

Overdose alone causes deep sleep and confusion; with alcohol, opioids, or benzodiazepines, breathing can slow and stop. Flumazenil can reverse it in hospitals.

## Signs of dangerous sedation or overdose

- Cannot be woken, or very confused and slurring.
- Slow, shallow, or stopped breathing; snoring or gurgling.
- Blue or gray lips; cold, clammy skin.
- Seizures or vomiting while drowsy.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect people who call.
- Give naloxone if opioids might be involved; it will not reverse these drugs but can reverse an opioid part.
- Put them in the recovery position on their side and stay with them.

Read more: [Mixing depressants: why combinations kill and what to do](../../safety/depressant-mixing.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
