# Buprenorphine (Subutex, Suboxone): Sublingual and buccal (under the tongue or in the cheek)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## About this route

Absorbed through the lining of the mouth, partly skipping first-pass metabolism. For Buprenorphine (Subutex, Suboxone): Subutex and Suboxone tablets and films under the tongue; Belbuca buccal film; Zubsolv. The main route.

## How this route feels different

Slower and steadier than injecting or snorting, faster than swallowing for some drugs. There is no rush; people describe a gradual settling.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (40-80 min)

Under the tongue, buprenorphine dissolves over several minutes with a bitter or flavored taste. Effects start around 40 to 80 minutes.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

In people without tolerance, the come-up is surprisingly strong: warm, floaty, nauseous, and sleepy. In people with tolerance, it is mostly the steady disappearance of craving and sickness.

### Peak (1.5-4 h)

The peak, roughly 1 to 4 hours, is a long, mellow high in non-tolerant people, often with heavy nausea. Because of the ceiling effect, more drug does not keep adding euphoria or breathing suppression the same way.

### Plateau (4-18 h)

The plateau lasts a very long time, often a full day. People describe being calm and functional, sometimes slightly energized, sometimes with headache.

### Comedown (18-48 h)

The effect fades over 24 to 72 hours, so there is usually no noticeable comedown between daily treatment doses.

### After-effects (24-72 h)

Headache, constipation, sweating, and insomnia are common. Precipitated withdrawal, if it is taken while a full agonist is still active, feels like sudden severe sickness: vomiting, diarrhea, sweats, and agitation.

### Next day (still active)

Still active the next day and beyond. Withdrawal after stopping is slow to start (often 2 to 4 days) and milder but longer than with full agonists.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Buprenorphine (Subutex, Suboxone) page](../../drugs/buprenorphine.md).

## Risks of this route

Effects build slowly and last long, so a second amount may overlap with the first. Dental problems have been linked to sublingual buprenorphine films and tablets.

## Overdose note

Its ceiling effect makes overdose from buprenorphine alone much less likely in adults, but combining it with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other sedatives has caused many deaths. Children who swallow films or tablets can die. Naloxone still helps, though higher amounts may be needed because buprenorphine binds so tightly. Precipitated withdrawal is not an overdose, but it is severe.

## Signs of an opioid overdose

- Breathing that is very slow, shallow, gurgling, or has stopped. Snoring-like sounds can be a warning sign, not sleep.
- Cannot be woken, or does not respond to a loud voice or a firm rub on the breastbone.
- Pinpoint pupils.
- Blue, gray, or ashen lips and fingertips; cold, clammy skin.
- Limp body.

## What to do

- Call 911. Many US states have Good Samaritan laws that protect people who call for help.
- Give naloxone (Narcan or another brand) if it is available. It is sold over the counter in US pharmacies. It can wear off before the opioid does, so overdose can return; stay with the person.
- If they are breathing, put them on their side (recovery position) so they do not choke.
- Never leave the person alone to "sleep it off."

Read more: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../../safety/opioid-overdose.md). See also [naloxone (Narcan)](../../narcan/naloxone.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
