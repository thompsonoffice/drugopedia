# Fentanyl (pharmaceutical): Sublingual and buccal (under the tongue or in the cheek)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## About this route

Absorbed through the lining of the mouth, partly skipping first-pass metabolism. For Fentanyl (pharmaceutical): Actiq, Fentora, Abstral, Subsys, Onsolis.

## How this route feels different

Slower and steadier than injecting or snorting, faster than swallowing for some drugs. There is no rush; people describe a gradual settling.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (5-15 min)

The first sign is often subtle: a faint warmth in the stomach or chest, a slight heaviness behind the eyes, and a loosening of tension in the shoulders. People describe it as the edge coming off whatever they were feeling.

### Come-up (15-30 min)

The warmth spreads outward to the arms, legs, and face. Pain, if present, fades into the background rather than disappearing outright; people describe still knowing it is there but no longer caring.

### Peak (20-45 min)

At the peak, people describe a deep, cozy contentment, as if wrapped in a warm blanket, with worries feeling far away and unimportant. Body feels heavy and relaxed; speech can slow.

### Plateau (45-90 min)

The effects level off into a steady, calm state. "Nodding" (heavy and sudden with Fentanyl) can occur: drifting in and out of a light sleep, head dropping, then jerking awake, sometimes mid-sentence or with a cigarette or drink in hand.

### Comedown (1-3 h)

The glow fades gradually. People describe the world getting "louder" again: light, noise, and small problems regain their weight.

### After-effects (3-8 h)

Effects fade quickly with fast routes (often within 1 to 2 hours), but fentanyl stored in fat can extend after-effects with repeated use.

### Next day

After a single medical exposure, most people feel close to normal, perhaps a little foggy or constipated. After repeated use, the next day can bring early withdrawal: anxiety, runny nose, goosebumps, aches, diarrhea, and strong craving.

For the full, detailed description of each phase and the character of the high, see the [general Fentanyl (pharmaceutical) page](../../drugs/fentanyl.md).

## Risks of this route

Effects build slowly and last long, so a second amount may overlap with the first. Dental problems have been linked to sublingual buprenorphine films and tablets.

## Overdose note

Fentanyl overdose can happen within seconds to minutes. It can cause 'wooden chest' (chest-wall rigidity), where the breathing muscles go stiff. More than one naloxone dose may be needed. Heat on a patch speeds release.

## Signs of an opioid overdose

- Breathing that is very slow, shallow, gurgling, or has stopped. Snoring-like sounds can be a warning sign, not sleep.
- Cannot be woken, or does not respond to a loud voice or a firm rub on the breastbone.
- Pinpoint pupils.
- Blue, gray, or ashen lips and fingertips; cold, clammy skin.
- Limp body.

## What to do

- Call 911. Many US states have Good Samaritan laws that protect people who call for help.
- Give naloxone (Narcan or another brand) if it is available. It is sold over the counter in US pharmacies. It can wear off before the opioid does, so overdose can return; stay with the person.
- If they are breathing, put them on their side (recovery position) so they do not choke.
- Never leave the person alone to "sleep it off."

Read more: [What an overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../../safety/opioid-overdose.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
