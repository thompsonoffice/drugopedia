# Scopolamine (hyoscine): Transdermal (skin patches)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Medical skin patches release drug slowly through the skin over days. For Scopolamine (hyoscine): Skin patch.

## How this route feels different

Through the skin, as with nicotine patches: slow and steady.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (4-6 h)

Patch effects build over hours; swallowed or injected forms act within minutes to an hour.

### Come-up (6-8 h)

Dry mouth and drowsiness.

### Peak (8-24 h)

Peak sedation and confusion.

### Plateau (up to 72 h)

Long plateau with patch.

### Comedown (after removal)

Gradual fade.

### After-effects (1-2 days)

Blurred vision.

### Next day (days)

Memory gaps.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Scopolamine (hyoscine) page](../../drugs/scopolamine.md).

## Risks of this route

Heat or damaged patches can release more drug; children are at risk from used patches.

## Overdose note

Overdose causes delirium, fast heart rate, high temperature and seizures.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Confusion, talking to people who are not there.
- Hot, dry, flushed skin; very fast heartbeat.
- Seizures or collapse.

## What to do

- Call 911 or Poison Control (1-800-222-1222).
- Keep them safe and calm; they may not know where they are.

Read more: [Deliriant safety](../../safety/deliriant-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
