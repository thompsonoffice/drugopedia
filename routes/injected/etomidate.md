# Etomidate: Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Delivered into a vein, muscle, or under the skin. Intravenous is the fastest and most dangerous route. For Etomidate: Medical IV.

## How this route feels different

Injected: the fastest and most intense route, with the highest risks of overdose, infection, and vein damage. Medical injections are given with monitoring.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (30-60 s)

IV etomidate causes unconsciousness within about a minute; vaped, effects come within seconds to minutes.

### Come-up (about 1 min)

Sedation and muscle jerks.

### Peak (1-5 min)

The peak lasts minutes.

### Plateau (3-10 min)

Short plateau.

### Comedown (under 1 h)

Recovery within minutes to an hour.

### After-effects (hours)

Confusion and fatigue.

### Next day (none)

With repeated use: adrenal suppression and weakness.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Etomidate page](../../drugs/etomidate.md).

## Risks of this route

Infection, abscesses, endocarditis, HIV and hepatitis C from shared equipment, and very fast overdose.

## Overdose note

Risks include stopped breathing, falls, and adrenal crisis with repeated use.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Unresponsive, or breathing slowly or noisily.
- Vomiting while dazed.
- Very high temperature, seizures, or extreme agitation (especially PCP or DXM).
- Injury they cannot feel.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect callers.
- Put them in the recovery position and keep them away from water, stairs and roads.
- Give naloxone if opioids may be involved.

Read more: [Dissociative safety: K-holes, injuries, bladder damage, mixing](../../safety/dissociative-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
