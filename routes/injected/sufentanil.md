# Sufentanil: Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## About this route

Delivered into a vein, muscle, or under the skin. Intravenous is the fastest and most dangerous route. For Sufentanil: IV in hospitals, with monitoring.

## How this route feels different

Intravenous use produces the classic 'rush': within seconds a hot, intense wave of pleasure from the stomach up through the chest and head, often compared to orgasm, sometimes with flushing, a heavy body, and a feeling of 'coming home'. It is shorter and the comedown is harder. Intramuscular and subcutaneous injection are slower, without much of a rush.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (under 1 min)

IV: about one minute; sublingual: around 15 to 30 minutes.

### Come-up (seconds)

Almost no come-up: drowsiness falls quickly.

### Peak (1-5 min)

Deep sedation; chest-wall stiffness can occur with fast IV use.

### Plateau (5-30 min (remifentanil ends within minutes of stopping))

Sedation for under an hour after a single IV bolus; longer with infusions.

### Comedown (under 1 h)

Wears off quickly once stopped, though it builds up in fat with long infusions.

### After-effects (1-4 h)

Grogginess, nausea, itching.

### Next day

Usually little lingering effect next day.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Sufentanil page](../../drugs/sufentanil.md).

## Risks of this route

The highest overdose risk of any route: the full effect hits within seconds to minutes, with no time to react. Infections (abscesses, endocarditis, sepsis), HIV and hepatitis C from shared equipment, collapsed veins, and damage from pill fillers, which are not made for veins. Syringe service programs offer sterile supplies, testing, and naloxone.

## Overdose note

Extremely potent; breathing can stop and chest-wall rigidity occur within a minute. Used only where airway support is ready.

## Signs of an opioid overdose

- Breathing that is very slow, shallow, gurgling, or has stopped. Snoring-like sounds can be a warning sign, not sleep.
- Cannot be woken, or does not respond to a loud voice or a firm rub on the breastbone.
- Pinpoint pupils.
- Blue, gray, or ashen lips and fingertips; cold, clammy skin.
- Limp body.

## What to do

- Call 911. Many US states have Good Samaritan laws that protect people who call for help.
- Give naloxone (Narcan or another brand) if it is available. It is sold over the counter in US pharmacies. It can wear off before the opioid does, so overdose can return; stay with the person.
- If they are breathing, put them on their side (recovery position) so they do not choke.
- Never leave the person alone to "sleep it off."

Read more: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../../safety/opioid-overdose.md). See also [naloxone (Narcan)](../../narcan/naloxone.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
