# Methamphetamine: Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Delivered into a vein, muscle, or under the skin. Intravenous is the fastest and most dangerous route. For Methamphetamine: Injected.

## How this route feels different

Injected: the fastest and most intense route, with the highest risks of overdose, infection, and vein damage. Medical injections are given with monitoring.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (seconds)

Smoked, the rush comes within seconds; swallowed, within 20 to 30 minutes.

### Come-up (minutes)

Intense euphoria and energy.

### Peak (15-30 min)

The peak lasts much longer than cocaine.

### Plateau (4-8 h)

Effects last 8 to 12 hours or more.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

The crash brings exhaustion and depression.

### After-effects (1-3 days)

Long sleep and extreme hunger.

### Next day (days)

Days of low mood and craving.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Methamphetamine page](../../drugs/methamphetamine.md).

## Risks of this route

Infection, abscesses, endocarditis, HIV and hepatitis C from shared equipment, and very fast overdose.

## Overdose note

Meth overdose causes overheating, heart attack, stroke, seizures and psychosis; fentanyl contamination adds breathing failure.

## Signs of a stimulant emergency

- Chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or severe headache.
- Very high body temperature, hot dry skin, or confusion.
- Seizures, extreme agitation, or paranoia.
- Sudden weakness or trouble speaking (possible stroke).
- Drowsiness and slow breathing (possible fentanyl contamination).

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect callers.
- Move them somewhere cool, loosen clothing, and use cool water on the skin.
- Keep them calm; do not restrain them.
- Give naloxone if they are unresponsive or breathing slowly, since stimulants are often contaminated with fentanyl.

Read more: [Stimulant safety: overheating, heart risks, psychosis, fentanyl contamination](../../safety/stimulant-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
