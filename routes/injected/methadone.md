# Methadone: Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## About this route

Delivered into a vein, muscle, or under the skin. Intravenous is the fastest and most dangerous route. For Methadone: Medical injection exists for patients who cannot swallow; misuse by injection is harmful because of fillers.

## How this route feels different

Intravenous use produces the classic 'rush': within seconds a hot, intense wave of pleasure from the stomach up through the chest and head, often compared to orgasm, sometimes with flushing, a heavy body, and a feeling of 'coming home'. It is shorter and the comedown is harder. Intramuscular and subcutaneous injection are slower, without much of a rush.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (IV under 1 min; IM 10-20 min)

Methadone starts slowly, 30 to 60 minutes after swallowing. The first sensations are quiet: a slight warmth, eased tension, and, in someone in withdrawal, the gradual fading of sickness.

### Come-up (IV a few minutes)

The come-up is gradual and underwhelming, which is part of the danger. People without tolerance describe a heavy, warm, sedated feeling building over hours, and may wrongly think it is weak.

### Peak (IV 10-30 min)

The felt peak is around 2 to 4 hours, but methadone keeps accumulating, and breathing suppression can deepen after the 'high' has leveled off. People describe a calm, heavy, dull contentment, sweating, and drowsiness.

### Plateau (1-8 h)

The plateau is very long, often the rest of the day. For people in treatment it feels like being normal: no craving, no sickness, clear enough to work.

### Comedown (8-36 h)

Methadone fades over 24 hours or more. There is no sharp comedown; it slowly thins out.

### After-effects (24-72 h)

Sweating, constipation, low libido, and sleepiness are common ongoing effects. The drug can also change heart rhythm.

### Next day (still active)

Methadone is still active the next day. Daily use builds blood levels for about 5 days before stabilizing, which is why the first week of treatment is the riskiest.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Methadone page](../../drugs/methadone.md).

## Risks of this route

The highest overdose risk of any route: the full effect hits within seconds to minutes, with no time to react. Infections (abscesses, endocarditis, sepsis), HIV and hepatitis C from shared equipment, collapsed veins, and damage from pill fillers, which are not made for veins. Syringe service programs offer sterile supplies, testing, and naloxone.

## Overdose note

Methadone overdose is often delayed and long-lasting. Peak breathing suppression can come hours after the peak 'feeling', and deaths often occur during sleep, the night of use or in the first days of starting or increasing. Naloxone wears off far sooner than methadone, so repeated doses and hospital monitoring are needed. Methadone can also cause a dangerous heart rhythm (QT prolongation). Combining with benzodiazepines or alcohol is especially dangerous.

## Signs of an opioid overdose

- Breathing that is very slow, shallow, gurgling, or has stopped. Snoring-like sounds can be a warning sign, not sleep.
- Cannot be woken, or does not respond to a loud voice or a firm rub on the breastbone.
- Pinpoint pupils.
- Blue, gray, or ashen lips and fingertips; cold, clammy skin.
- Limp body.

## What to do

- Call 911. Many US states have Good Samaritan laws that protect people who call for help.
- Give naloxone (Narcan or another brand) if it is available. It is sold over the counter in US pharmacies. It can wear off before the opioid does, so overdose can return; stay with the person.
- If they are breathing, put them on their side (recovery position) so they do not choke.
- Never leave the person alone to "sleep it off."

Read more: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../../safety/opioid-overdose.md). See also [naloxone (Narcan)](../../narcan/naloxone.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
