# Ketamine: Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## About this route

Delivered into a vein, muscle, or under the skin. Intravenous is the fastest and most dangerous route. For Ketamine: Medical IV or IM; also injected recreationally.

## How this route feels different

Injected: the fastest and most intense route, with the highest risks of overdose, infection, and vein damage. Medical injections are given with monitoring.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (seconds-5 min)

Snorted, ketamine takes effect within about 5 to 15 minutes: numbness, a light 'drunk' feeling, and a shifting visual field.

### Come-up (1-5 min)

Within minutes the body starts to feel distant; walking becomes unsteady.

### Peak (5-20 min)

The peak, about 15 to 30 minutes in, is the strongest dissociation; at high levels, the K-hole.

### Plateau (20-40 min)

Effects plateau for 30 to 60 minutes.

### Comedown (about 1 h)

People 'come back' over another hour, often confused and nauseated.

### After-effects (2-3 h)

Tiredness and a dreamy afterglow; some feel low.

### Next day

Next-day fatigue; with frequent use, bladder pain and urgency.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Ketamine page](../../drugs/ketamine.md).

## Risks of this route

Infection, abscesses, endocarditis, HIV and hepatitis C from shared equipment, and very fast overdose.

## Overdose note

Ketamine alone rarely stops breathing, but with alcohol, opioids or benzodiazepines it can; vomiting while unresponsive and accidents are major causes of death.

## Signs that someone needs help

- Unresponsive, or breathing slowly or noisily.
- Vomiting while dazed.
- Very high temperature, seizures, or extreme agitation (especially PCP or DXM).
- Injury they cannot feel.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect callers.
- Put them in the recovery position and keep them away from water, stairs and roads.
- Give naloxone if opioids may be involved.

Read more: [Dissociative safety: K-holes, injuries, bladder damage, mixing](../../safety/dissociative-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
