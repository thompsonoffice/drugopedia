# Methohexital (Brevital): Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## About this route

Delivered into a vein, muscle, or under the skin. Intravenous is the fastest and most dangerous route. For Methohexital (Brevital): Hospital IV only.

## How this route feels different

Injection is a hospital route for anesthesia, seizures, or emergencies, with monitoring of breathing and blood pressure.

## Timeline for this route

### Onset (IV under 1 min)

Effects begin within about IV under 1 min.

### Come-up (immediate)

Drowsiness and loosened coordination build.

### Peak (within 1 min)

The peak is the deepest sedation.

### Plateau (5-10 min)

Plateau of 5-10 min.

### Comedown (10-20 min)

Effects fade over 10-20 min.

### After-effects (under 1 h)

Grogginess and poor sleep quality.

### Next day (none)

Next-day effects: none.

For the full, detailed description of each phase, see the [general Methohexital (Brevital) page](../../drugs/methohexital.md).

## Risks of this route

A hospital route. Breathing and blood pressure can drop quickly. Injecting pills causes vein damage, infection, and blood clots.

## Overdose note

Overdose causes coma, slowed or stopped breathing, low blood pressure and death; there is no antidote.

## Signs of dangerous sedation or overdose

- Cannot be woken, or very confused and slurring.
- Slow, shallow, or stopped breathing; snoring or gurgling.
- Blue or gray lips; cold, clammy skin.
- Seizures or vomiting while drowsy.

## What to do

- Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect people who call.
- Give naloxone if opioids might be involved; it will not reverse these drugs but can reverse an opioid part.
- Put them in the recovery position on their side and stay with them.

Read more: [Mixing depressants: why combinations kill and what to do](../../safety/depressant-mixing.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
