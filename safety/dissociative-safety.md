# Dissociative and anesthetic safety

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice. No amounts, preparation, or sourcing. In an emergency in the US, call 911. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222. SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357.

## Why this page exists

Dissociatives (ketamine, PCP, DXM, nitrous oxide, methoxetamine and research chemicals) and anesthetics (propofol, etomidate) separate the mind from the body. The main dangers are accidents, choking, breathing problems when mixed, and long-term organ damage.

## The biggest dangers

### Being unable to move or respond

At high levels people cannot move, speak or protect themselves. Deaths happen from drowning, falls, traffic, cold exposure, and choking on vomit.

### Mixing with depressants

Alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, GHB and sleep drugs with dissociatives can stop breathing.

### No pain

People do not feel injuries, burns or broken bones until later.

### Agitation and overheating

PCP, DXM and some research chemicals cause agitation, high blood pressure, high temperature and seizures.

### Long-term harm

- Ketamine: bladder damage (pain, urgency, blood in urine) that can become permanent, and memory problems.
- Nitrous oxide: vitamin B12 depletion causing numbness, weakness and trouble walking.
- DXM products: liver failure from acetaminophen in combination products.
- Etomidate: adrenal suppression.

## Warning signs

- Unresponsive, or breathing slowly or noisily.
- Vomiting while dazed.
- Seizures, very high temperature, or severe agitation.

## What to do

1. Call 911.
2. Put them in the recovery position.
3. Keep them away from water, stairs, roads and heat.
4. Give naloxone if opioids could be involved.
