# Stimulant and cathinone safety

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice. No amounts, preparation, or sourcing. In an emergency in the US, call 911. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222. SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357.

## Why this page exists

Stimulants (amphetamines, methamphetamine, cocaine, crack, prescription ADHD medicines) and synthetic cathinones ("bath salts") push the heart, blood vessels and body temperature hard. They have no antidote like naloxone, and many stimulant deaths now also involve fentanyl.

## The biggest dangers

### Overheating

Stimulants raise body temperature, and heat, crowds, dancing and dehydration make it worse. Very high temperature can break down muscle and shut down the kidneys.

### Heart and brain

Heart attack, dangerous heart rhythms, and stroke can happen even in young people, especially with cocaine and with binges.

### Psychosis and excited delirium

Days without sleep and high levels cause paranoia, hallucinations, and severe agitation. Cathinones such as MDPV and alpha-PVP are especially linked to this.

### Contamination

Counterfeit Adderall can contain methamphetamine. Cocaine, meth and "molly" can contain fentanyl. Fentanyl test strips and drug-checking services exist in many areas, and carrying naloxone matters even for people who do not use opioids.

### Mixing

Stimulants hide the effects of alcohol and depressants, so people drink or use more. Cocaine with alcohol forms cocaethylene, which is harder on the heart. Stimulants with antidepressants or other serotonin drugs can cause serotonin syndrome.

## Warning signs

- Chest pain, pressure, or a racing or irregular heartbeat.
- Very hot skin, confusion, or stopping sweating.
- Seizures.
- Sudden severe headache, weakness, or trouble speaking.
- Extreme agitation, paranoia or hallucinations.
- Slow breathing or unresponsiveness (possible fentanyl).

## What to do

1. Call 911. Good Samaritan laws in most US states protect people who call for help.
2. Move the person somewhere cool and quiet; use cool water and fanning.
3. Speak calmly; do not restrain them.
4. If they are unresponsive or breathing slowly, give naloxone. It will not harm them if no opioid is present.
5. If they stop breathing, start CPR.

## After a binge

The crash can bring deep depression and suicidal thoughts. Call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) if that happens.
