# Xylazine ("tranq") safety

> Educational harm-reduction content. Call **911** in an emergency.

## What it is

Xylazine is a veterinary sedative used on horses and cattle. It is not an opioid and is not approved for people. Since about 2019 it has been mixed into the illicit fentanyl supply, first in Philadelphia and Puerto Rico and then across the US. Mixtures are called "tranq" or "tranq dope." In 2023 the White House named fentanyl mixed with xylazine an emerging threat.

## What it does to people

People describe very long, heavy blackouts, hours of unconsciousness after a fentanyl high that would otherwise be short. Blood pressure and heart rate drop. Many wake up confused, in unsafe places, or injured. Xylazine causes severe, slow-healing wounds that can appear anywhere on the body, not only where drugs were injected, and can lead to amputation if untreated. Withdrawal from xylazine adds intense anxiety, restlessness, and high blood pressure on top of opioid withdrawal.

## Overdose and naloxone

Naloxone does not reverse xylazine, but **always give naloxone**, because xylazine is almost always mixed with fentanyl and naloxone will restore breathing from the opioid part. The person may stay sedated after breathing returns. Focus on breathing: call 911, give naloxone, give rescue breaths, and put them in the recovery position.

## Wounds

Xylazine wounds need medical care. Keep them clean and covered, and see a clinic, syringe service program, or wound-care service early. Wounds can become deep and infected.

## Testing

Xylazine test strips are available from many harm-reduction programs.

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357**. See also [opioid overdose](opioid-overdose.md) and [naloxone](../narcan/naloxone.md).
