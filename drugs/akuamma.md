# Akuamma (akuammine)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Seeds of Picralima nitida, a West African tree, containing akuammine and related alkaloids that weakly activate opioid receptors.

**Brand and street names:** Sold as powdered seeds and capsules.

## History and origins

Used in traditional West African medicine for pain, fever, and malaria. Sold online in the West since the 2010s.

## Medical use, then and now

No approved medical use.

## How it entered recreational use

Used occasionally as a kratom alternative.

## Legal status

Not controlled in the US.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Akuamma is described as a mild, sometimes barely noticeable calm with slight pain relief and relaxation, much weaker than kratom. Many report a bitter taste and nausea.

Research suggests its alkaloids are weak opioid agonists, so the experience is subtle and variable.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Begins 30 to 60 minutes after swallowing.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Mild relaxation.

### Peak (1-2 h)

Subtle calm.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

A few hours of mild effect.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

Fades.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Possible nausea.

### Next day

Little known about dependence.

Little studied; combine risk with other sedatives.
