# Ephedrine, pseudoephedrine and ephedra

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Plant-derived and synthetic stimulants that release noradrenaline.

**Brand and street names:** Ephedra (ma huang), Primatene tablets, Bronkaid; pseudoephedrine: Sudafed.

## History and origins

Ma huang has been used in Chinese medicine for thousands of years. Nagai Nagayoshi isolated ephedrine in 1885. The FDA banned ephedra supplements in 2004 after deaths, and the Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act of 2005 moved pseudoephedrine behind the pharmacy counter.

## Medical use, then and now

Low blood pressure during anesthesia, asthma (limited), and nasal congestion (pseudoephedrine).

## How it entered recreational use

Used in weight-loss and bodybuilding products, often with caffeine.

## Legal status

Ephedrine and pseudoephedrine are DEA List I chemicals with sales limits.

## What the pills feel like

Sudafed tablets are small and red; generics vary.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Ephedrine feels like jittery, physical stimulation more than euphoria: racing heart, sweating and alertness.

It raises blood pressure and can trigger heart attacks, strokes and seizures, especially with caffeine.

Pseudoephedrine is milder but causes insomnia and raised blood pressure.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Ephedrine, from the ephedra (ma huang) plant and used in some decongestants and to raise blood pressure during anesthesia, acts on adrenaline receptors. Swallowed, it comes on over a short while with a warm flush, a faster, harder heartbeat, opening airways and a tingling restlessness in the limbs.

Mentally the onset is a jittery alertness, like a strong dose of adrenaline: more awake, a little excited, sometimes anxious. Euphoria is mild compared with amphetamine. Some people describe their heart 'kicking' and a nervous, butterflies-in-the-stomach feeling.

Ephedra supplements were banned in the US in 2004 after strokes, heart attacks and deaths, particularly when combined with caffeine in diet and sports products. Its effects on blood pressure are why it is still used carefully during surgery.

Ephedrine acts both directly on adrenergic receptors and by releasing noradrenaline.

Blood pressure and heart rate rise quickly.

### Come-up (about 1 h)

Energy and jitteriness.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At its peak the body is tense and stimulated: pounding pulse, raised blood pressure, sweating, tremor, dry mouth and trouble urinating for some men. Breathing feels open. Hands may tremble visibly, and some people feel hot and flushed even in a cool room.

Mentally there is energy and focus mixed with nervousness, irritability and racing thoughts at higher amounts; some feel a sense of impending doom. Small worries can swell into anxiety, and some people feel a pressure in the head.

Danger signs include chest pain, severe headache, palpitations, confusion or weakness on one side, which can signal heart attack or stroke. It also raises body temperature, adding risk during exercise or heat.

At high levels, especially with caffeine, ephedrine can cause stroke, heart attack, arrhythmia and seizures; these led to the 2004 ephedra ban.

Pseudoephedrine has weaker effects but can still raise blood pressure.

### Plateau (3-6 h)

The plateau lasts a few hours. The body stays revved, hot and restless, with reduced appetite and sometimes nausea. Urination may be difficult, the heart may skip or flutter, and sleep feels impossible until it wears off.

Mentally people feel wired and edgy, finding it hard to settle, with an underlying anxiousness. Conversation may feel rushed and focus scattered, with a constant urge to move or do something. Thoughts may jump from one thing to another, and it can be hard to finish what was started.

Combining with caffeine or other stimulants multiplies heart risk. People with high blood pressure, thyroid disease, glaucoma or heart problems are at particular risk.

### Comedown (6-8 h)

As it fades, tiredness, headache and a heavy, washed-out feeling follow. Muscles may ache from tension, and the heart may still feel jumpy for a while. Appetite returns and a dull headache is common.

Emotionally mild low mood or irritability. Sleep may be broken the night after, and the next day can feel foggy and flat. Some people feel a mild, gloomy flatness or nervous exhaustion as the adrenaline-like push fades away.

Regular use can cause tolerance and some dependence; it is a controlled precursor because it can be turned into methamphetamine. Anyone who has had chest pain or palpitations on it should stop and see a doctor. Its sale is restricted in many countries.

Half-life is about 6 hours.

Insomnia and urinary retention are common.

### After-effects (8-12 h)

Insomnia.

### Next day

Usually none.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Ephedrine, pseudoephedrine and ephedra

High levels cause dangerous blood pressure spikes, heart rhythm problems and strokes.
