# Delta-8-THC

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Semi-synthetic or converted cannabinoids marketed from hemp.

**Brand and street names:** Delta-8, D8; sold as gummies, vapes and 'diet weed' in gas stations and online.

## History and origins

After the 2018 US Farm Bill legalized hemp with low delta-9-THC, companies began chemically converting hemp CBD into delta-8-THC, and later HHC and THC-O, selling them in gas stations and online.

## Medical use, then and now

None are approved medicines.

## How it entered recreational use

Marketed as a legal or 'lighter' high; FDA and poison centers report rising exposures, many in children.

## Legal status

A legal gray area. The DEA stated in 2023 that THC-O is a controlled substance; many states ban delta-8 and HHC. A 2025 federal law redefined hemp to close this loophole, effective in late 2026.

## What the products look and feel like

Often sold as brightly colored gummies or disposable vape pens. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Delta-8 feels like a milder, clearer version of ordinary THC: relaxed, mildly euphoric and sleepy, with less anxiety for many people.

It occurs naturally only in tiny amounts, so commercial delta-8 is made by chemically converting hemp CBD; leftover acids, solvents and by-products are a concern.

Products are often mislabeled and may contain delta-9-THC or other cannabinoids. Poison centers report many child exposures.

Tolerance to the high builds quickly with daily use and fades after a break of a few weeks; people returning after a break, or switching to concentrates or edibles, are often surprised by how strong the effect is.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (minutes)

Delta-8-THC is a close chemical cousin of regular THC (delta-9), usually made by converting hemp-derived CBD and sold in gas stations, vape shops and online, often in gummies and vape cartridges. Inhaled it acts within minutes; eaten, it can take an hour or more. The first sensations are a gentle warmth, dry mouth, red eyes and a light, relaxing heaviness.

Mentally the onset is often described as a milder, clearer, less anxious high than regular THC: relaxed, gently euphoric and a little spacey, though in larger amounts it can feel very similar.

Because products are loosely regulated, labels can be wrong, and byproducts of the chemical conversion may be present. Poison centers have reported many cases of children eating delta-8 gummies.

Delta-8-THC is a partial CB1 agonist with roughly half to two-thirds the potency of delta-9-THC in older studies.

Body signs mirror THC: dry mouth, red eyes and a faster heartbeat.

### Come-up (5-15 min)

A gradual relaxation and lightness.

### Peak (15-45 min)

At the peak the body feels relaxed and heavy, with a faster heart, dry mouth and impaired coordination. Some feel sleepy, dizzy or nauseated. Eyes become red and heavy, and the mouth is dry. Some people notice their heart thumping and feel slightly lightheaded on standing. Edible forms can feel heavier and more sedating than vapes.

Mentally there is calm, euphoria, slowed time and heightened senses, with impaired short-term memory. Anxiety and paranoia are less common than with strong delta-9 but still possible. Some find it easier to stay socially comfortable than with regular THC, while others notice slow speech and losing track of thoughts mid-sentence.

Delta-8 impairs driving and can cause positive drug tests for THC. Products often contain unlabeled amounts of delta-9 THC or other cannabinoids, so effects may be stronger than expected.

Peak effects include sedation and time distortion; poison center reports show confusion, vomiting and loss of consciousness, especially in children.

Contaminants from the conversion process, not just delta-8 itself, may contribute to harm.

### Plateau (1 h)

The plateau lasts a couple of hours inhaled and longer eaten. The body stays relaxed, appetite may increase, and drowsiness grows. Muscles feel loose, the eyes droop, and lying down feels very comfortable. Some people feel a mild headache or dizziness, especially after vaping a lot.

Mentally thoughts drift and focus is poor; many feel content and mellow. Some people enjoy music, food or films more; others feel foggy and want to sleep. Short-term memory remains poor and time feels slow.

Frequent use builds tolerance and can lead to dependence like regular cannabis. Children who eat delta-8 gummies can become very sleepy or unresponsive and need medical care.

### Comedown (2-3 h)

The comedown brings tiredness, dry mouth and some grogginess, especially after edibles. Some wake the next day slightly groggy, with dry eyes and mouth, especially after gummies taken late. Appetite may stay high. Some notice they sleep deeply but wake feeling less rested than usual, with few remembered dreams.

Mentally there may be lingering fogginess and low motivation. Some feel slightly slow, unmotivated or forgetful for some hours. Anxiety after a strong experience usually fades with rest.

Regular users who stop may notice irritability, poor sleep and craving for a week or two. Daily heavy vaping can build dependence much like regular cannabis; cutting down gradually helps.

Effects fade over a few hours (vapes) or many hours (edibles).

Delta-8 shows up on standard cannabis drug tests.

### After-effects (3-5 h)

Dry mouth and grogginess.

### Next day (next morning)

Possible next-day fog, particularly with edibles.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cannabis brings relaxation, mild euphoria, giggliness, and heightened senses. At higher doses, time distortion, anxiety, paranoia, a racing heart, nausea, dizziness and, for some, hallucinations or panic appear. Very high doses, especially edibles, can cause hours of confusion, vomiting and frightening psychotic symptoms.

Strength varies from batch to batch, so the jump from mild to overwhelming is unpredictable.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.
