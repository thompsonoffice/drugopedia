# Metaxalone (Skelaxin)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## At a glance

Centrally acting muscle relaxant.

**Brand and street names:** Skelaxin, Metaxall.

## History and origins

Approved in 1962.

## Medical use, then and now

Acute painful muscle conditions; marketed as less sedating.

## How it entered recreational use

Little misuse.

## Legal status

Prescription-only, not controlled.

## What the pills feel like

Skelaxin tablets are pale pink, oval, and scored.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Metaxalone is one of the least sedating muscle relaxants: many people feel only slight drowsiness and mild muscle easing.

Food increases absorption noticeably.

Overdose and serotonin interactions have been reported rarely.

Tolerance to the pleasant and sleep effects builds with regular use, but tolerance to slowed breathing builds more slowly, so the danger stays. Daily use can lead to dependence, and stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 1 h)

Metaxalone (Skelaxin) builds slowly and quietly, and it is absorbed better when taken with a meal. Over an hour or so, the muscles of the back and neck feel slightly less clenched, and a soft drowsiness gathers behind the eyes. Compared with carisoprodol or cyclobenzaprine, the sleepiness is lighter and the mind stays clearer.

Mentally the onset is subtle: a mild calm, a slight dreaminess, and a sense of tension easing. Most people do not feel 'high'. Some describe a light, pleasant heaviness that makes it easier to rest.

Early side effects can include mild nausea, headache, dizziness, nervousness or irritability. A few people feel a little restless instead of relaxed.

Medically, the onset reflects absorption from the gut and entry into the brain. Metaxalone is processed by several liver enzymes and its absorption is increased by food. Muscle relaxants work mostly through central sedation rather than acting on the muscles themselves (dantrolene is the exception), so drowsiness usually arrives together with the loosening of tension.

Clinicians warn that reaction time slows early, often before the person notices, and that dizziness on standing is common, especially in older adults.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Mild relaxation.

### Peak (about 3 h)

At the peak the body feels somewhat looser and heavier, and painful movement eases. Dizziness is usually mild, and coordination is mostly intact, though fine tasks may feel slightly clumsy.

Emotionally the peak is calm and gently sleepy, without the drunken disinhibition of carisoprodol or the anticholinergic fog of cyclobenzaprine. Larger amounts mostly bring more drowsiness, nausea and dizziness rather than euphoria.

Serious effects are uncommon, but metaxalone can contribute to serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol or other serotonergic drugs, and it adds to the sedation of alcohol and other depressants. People with liver disease or certain anemias should avoid it.

Medically, the peak is when sedation and dizziness are strongest. Several muscle relaxants have anticholinergic or blood-pressure effects as well, which can cause dry mouth, blurred vision, a fast or slow heartbeat, or fainting.

Alone they rarely stop breathing in healthy adults, but combined with opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol, or gabapentinoids they add to respiratory depression; carisoprodol in particular was a common part of the deadly 'holy trinity' with opioids and benzodiazepines.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The metaxalone plateau is a mild, steady relaxation lasting a few hours. The back and neck stay looser than before, and there is a light, pleasant heaviness in the limbs. People can usually carry on quiet activities such as reading or watching television, though many prefer to rest or nap.

Thinking remains mostly clear, with some slowing and drowsiness, and mood is calm and neutral rather than euphoric. A few people feel slightly irritable or restless instead of relaxed, and some notice a mild headache or upset stomach lingering from the onset.

Reaction time is reduced enough that drivers should be cautious until they know how it affects them, and alcohol or other sedatives deepen the drowsiness.

### Comedown (4-6 h total)

The comedown is gentle. Stiffness gradually returns over a few hours and a light tiredness lingers, sometimes with a mild headache, slight nausea or dry mouth. There is no real hangover, and most people simply notice the medicine wearing off as their pain comes back.

Mood generally stays steady, without a crash, craving or rebound anxiety. Some people feel a little sluggish for the rest of the evening.

Dependence and withdrawal are rare. Very large overdoses, especially combined with other drugs, have caused deaths, so it should still be stored safely away from children and others.

As levels fall, sedation lifts and muscle tension may return. Short-acting drugs wear off within hours, while drugs with active metabolites (carisoprodol to meprobamate) linger.

People who use carisoprodol, meprobamate, or baclofen daily can develop dependence; stopping suddenly can cause anxiety, insomnia, hallucinations, and seizures, and abrupt baclofen withdrawal can be life-threatening.

### After-effects (6-12 h)

Mild drowsiness.

### Next day (little)

Little next-day effect.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, muscle relaxants mainly cause drowsiness and eased tension. At higher doses, dizziness, heavy sedation, poor coordination, blurred vision, and confusion grow, and some (carisoprodol, meprobamate, orphenadrine, baclofen, tizanidine) bring additional dangers: seizures, heart rhythm problems, very low blood pressure, or coma. With opioids, alcohol, or benzodiazepines, breathing can slow and stop.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, age, liver and kidney function, genetics, and especially other depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines) change the effect. Older adults and people with sleep apnea or lung disease are at higher risk. Stronger effects do not stop at more pleasure: they shade into blackouts, falls, and slowed breathing.

## Overdose note for Metaxalone (Skelaxin)

Overdose causes drowsiness, and in rare cases serotonin syndrome or coma, especially with other drugs.
