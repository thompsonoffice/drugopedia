# Ramelteon (Rozerem)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## At a glance

Melatonin receptor agonist.

**Brand and street names:** Rozerem; tasimelteon (Hetlioz) is a related drug.

## History and origins

Approved in 2005 by Takeda as the first prescription sleep drug with no evidence of abuse potential.

## Medical use, then and now

Trouble falling asleep.

## How it entered recreational use

No meaningful misuse.

## Legal status

Not controlled.

## What the pills feel like

Small, round, film-coated tablets.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Ramelteon's character is a gentle nudge toward sleep, similar to melatonin, without the heavy sedation or amnesia of other hypnotics.

It does not cause dependence.

Side effects include dizziness and next-day tiredness.

Tolerance to the pleasant and sleep effects builds with regular use, but tolerance to slowed breathing builds more slowly, so the danger stays. Daily use can lead to dependence, and stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 30 min)

Ramelteon (Rozerem) works like the body's own melatonin, binding melatonin receptors in the brain's internal clock and nudging it toward night. The onset is subtle, usually within half an hour: a mild sense that it is time for bed, heavier eyelids and a slight slowing of thoughts, but no drugged or heavy feeling. It is usually taken shortly before bedtime and works best on an empty stomach, since a heavy, fatty meal delays its effect and makes the sleepiness arrive later than expected.

Mentally it is neutral; there is no high, calm, giddiness or disinhibition. Many people say they would not know they had taken anything except that falling asleep felt a little easier.

Some feel mild dizziness, tiredness, nausea or a light headache.

Medically, the onset reflects absorption and entry into the brain. Ramelteon is processed by CYP1A2; fluvoxamine strongly raises its levels. Most hypnotics are designed to work quickly so the person falls asleep soon after taking them.

Clinicians advise taking hypnotics only right before bed, because people who stay awake can experience confusion, hallucinations, and memory loss.

### Come-up (30-45 min)

Gentle sleepiness.

### Peak (about 45 min)

At the peak there is gentle, natural-feeling sleepiness, similar to the drowsiness that comes at the end of a long day. People can still get up, talk and move normally, though they feel ready to sleep.

Emotionally it is unchanged, without the euphoria or confusion of benzodiazepines or Z-drugs. Memory is not affected. Because its action is gentle, people with severe insomnia sometimes find it too weak, while others appreciate the lack of drugged feeling.

It is not known to cause overdose deaths on its own and has little effect on breathing, but alcohol and other sedatives still add drowsiness. Fluvoxamine greatly raises its levels and should not be combined.

Medically, the peak is when sleep pressure is strongest. People who resist sleep at this point may walk, eat, talk, drive, or send messages with no memory afterward ('complex sleep behaviors'), which led to a 2019 FDA boxed warning for zolpidem, zaleplon, and eszopiclone.

Combined with alcohol, opioids, or other sedatives, breathing can slow dangerously, and amnesia is much more likely.

### Plateau (sleep)

The ramelteon plateau is a quiet, natural-feeling sleep. It helps more with falling asleep than staying asleep, so some people still wake in the night. The body is relaxed but not heavy. Breathing and heart rate stay much as in ordinary sleep, and people can usually be woken easily and respond clearly, unlike with heavier sedatives. Those who wake during the night are generally able to get up, walk to the bathroom and return to bed without the unsteadiness or confusion that Z-drugs and benzodiazepines can cause.

Some people notice vivid or unusual dreams, and a few report mild changes in hormone levels with long-term use.

Next-day grogginess is uncommon, and coordination and memory are generally unaffected.

### Comedown (2-5 h)

Waking is usually clear and normal, without hangover, heaviness or a bad taste. Some people notice mild tiredness or dizziness in the morning. For most people the morning after feels like any other morning, which is the main reason it is chosen over stronger sleep drugs. Some people notice its benefit builds over several weeks as their sleep schedule becomes more regular.

Mood is unchanged, and there is no rebound anxiety or craving.

It is not considered addictive and is not a controlled substance; stopping does not cause withdrawal or rebound insomnia, which is why it is often used in older adults and people with a history of substance use.

As levels fall, most people sleep through the comedown. Those who wake early can feel groggy, unsteady, and slow, and next-morning impairment can affect driving, especially in women and older adults.

Nightly use can cause tolerance and rebound insomnia on stopping; some hypnotics (GHB, methaqualone, older drugs) cause severe dependence and dangerous withdrawal.

### After-effects (next morning)

Mild tiredness.

### Next day (little)

Little next-day effect.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, hypnotics bring sleepiness and calm. At higher doses, or when the person stays awake, confusion, memory loss, hallucinations, and sleep-walking or sleep-driving become likely. With alcohol or opioids, or with drugs like GHB and methaqualone, effects can tip into unconsciousness, vomiting while unconscious, and stopped breathing.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, age, liver and kidney function, genetics, and especially other depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines) change the effect. Older adults and people with sleep apnea or lung disease are at higher risk. Stronger effects do not stop at more pleasure: they shade into blackouts, falls, and slowed breathing.
