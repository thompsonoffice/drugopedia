# Pentazocine (Talwin)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Mixed agonist-antagonist opioid: activates kappa receptors and partly blocks or weakly activates mu receptors, with a ceiling effect.

**Brand and street names:** Talwin, Talwin NX (with naloxone), Talacen, Fortral (UK).

## History and origins

Developed by Sterling Winthrop in 1958 and marketed in 1967. In the 1970s, 'Ts and Blues' (Talwin with the antihistamine tripelennamine) became a street injection drug in the US Midwest, prompting Talwin NX with naloxone in 1983.

## Medical use, then and now

Moderate to severe pain, labor, and migraine (butorphanol). Rarely used today.

## How it entered recreational use

Less sought after than full agonists because of unpleasant kappa effects. Injected tablets caused severe skin and muscle damage.

## Legal status

Butorphanol and pentazocine US Schedule IV; nalbuphine not controlled.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Pentazocine (Talwin) is described as an odd, mixed experience: mild opioid warmth and sedation combined with spacey, dreamlike, or uneasy feelings from kappa receptors, sometimes dysphoria, racing thoughts, or hallucinations. People rarely describe it as purely pleasant.

In people dependent on full opioids, it can trigger withdrawal because it partly blocks the mu receptor.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Injected, it starts within minutes; nasal spray within about 15 minutes; swallowed within 15 to 30 minutes. Often a light-headed, floaty start.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

The come-up brings sleepiness, a 'drunk' or detached feeling, and sometimes dizziness or sweating.

### Peak (1-2 h)

The peak combines pain relief and sedation with dreaminess; some feel anxiety, strange thoughts, or vivid imagery.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The plateau is sedated and spacey, lasting a few hours.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

It fades over 3 to 6 hours.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Some feel hungover, dizzy, or low.

### Next day

Next day is usually normal after single use.

## Overdose note for Pentazocine (Talwin)

Has a ceiling on breathing suppression alone, but is dangerous with other sedatives. Can cause hallucinations and precipitate withdrawal in people dependent on other opioids.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). Also see [naloxone (Narcan)](../narcan/naloxone.md).
