# Propoxyphene (Darvon, Darvocet)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic opioid related to methadone; weak mu agonist whose metabolite norpropoxyphene is toxic to the heart.

**Brand and street names:** Darvon, Darvocet (with acetaminophen), Darvon-N, Distalgesic and Co-proxamol (UK).

## History and origins

Introduced by Eli Lilly in 1957 and once one of the most prescribed painkillers in the US. The UK withdrew co-proxamol in 2005 over suicide overdoses, and the FDA had it withdrawn in November 2010 after finding it disrupts heart rhythm even at normal doses.

## Medical use, then and now

No longer used. Withdrawn worldwide.

## How it entered recreational use

Commonly misused in the 1960s to 1980s; Darvon was implicated in many overdose deaths.

## Legal status

US Schedule IV (no longer marketed).

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Propoxyphene was described as a weak, mild opioid buzz: light warmth and relaxation, often barely more than acetaminophen alone. Its danger far outweighed its effect.

People recalled it as sleepy and dull rather than euphoric.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Effects began 30 to 60 minutes after swallowing.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

A gentle warmth and drowsiness.

### Peak (1-2 h)

A mild calm peaking around 2 hours.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

Mild sedation for a few hours.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

Faded over 4 to 6 hours.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Its metabolite stayed much longer, affecting the heart.

### Next day

Mild next-day grogginess.

## Overdose note for Propoxyphene (Darvon, Darvocet)

Heart rhythm disturbances and rapid death in overdose, which naloxone could not fully reverse. Combination products added acetaminophen liver toxicity.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md).
