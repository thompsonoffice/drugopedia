# 2C-B

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic phenethylamine psychedelic.

**Brand and street names:** Nexus, Venus, bees, tusi (in Latin America, 'tusi' often contains ketamine and MDMA instead).

## History and origins

Alexander Shulgin synthesized 2C-B in 1974 and described it in PiHKAL (1991). It was sold as 'Nexus' in Germany in the 1980s, then banned.

## Medical use, then and now

None; briefly used in German psychotherapy.

## How it entered recreational use

Popular in club settings.

## Legal status

US Schedule I (1995); UN 1971 Schedule II (2001).

## What the products look and feel like

Powder or small pills. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

2C-B combines mild empathogenic warmth with colorful visuals; it is shorter and more 'body' than LSD.

Stronger levels bring intense visuals and can be overwhelming.

'Tusi' or 'pink cocaine' sold in clubs usually contains ketamine, MDMA and other drugs, not 2C-B.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-75 min)

2C-B (Nexus, Bees) has a moderate onset of tingling body energy, warmth, goosebumps and sometimes a brief queasy stomach, usually within an hour. The body feels charged, sensual and lightly electric, and many feel an urge to stretch, move or touch soft fabrics. Note that street 'tusi' or 'pink cocaine' rarely contains 2C-B. Pupils widen and the heart rate climbs a little as effects build.

Mentally the onset is lighthearted and giggly, an open, playful mood often described as halfway between a psychedelic and MDMA. Laughter comes easily and people feel social and curious.

Colors start to brighten, edges gain a sheen, and faint patterns may appear on surfaces.

2C-B is absorbed from the gut and acts as a partial agonist at 5-HT2A and 5-HT2C receptors.

Heart rate rises modestly; nausea is possible.

### Come-up (1-1.5 h)

Body tingles and colors brighten.

### Peak (1.5-3 h)

At the peak, vivid colors, flowing patterns and a sensual, energized body with heightened touch are typical. Music feels physical, and many feel drawn to dance or be close to others.

Emotionally playful and euphoric at lower amounts; at higher amounts intensely visual, bodily and potentially overwhelming or frightening, with strong distortions.

Psychedelics rarely threaten the body directly, but they impair judgment and coordination, so falls, traffic, water and heights are real dangers. Panic, paranoia and terrifying experiences can happen to anyone, and people with a personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder face a higher risk of lasting harm.

At peak, visual and tactile effects are strong; high levels cause anxiety and confusion.

Mixing with stimulants raises heart risk.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The 2C-B plateau is several hours of steady effects. Colors stay bright, surfaces ripple, and the body remains warm, energized and sensual, with touch and music especially pleasurable. Some feel mild stomach discomfort or muscle tension throughout.

Mood stays warm, talkative and playful, often with a sense of closeness to friends. At higher amounts, the plateau can feel heavy and overwhelmingly visual, with distorted faces and difficulty communicating. Effects shift noticeably with small changes in amount, so the intensity can surprise even experienced users.

Overheating and dehydration are possible in clubs, and combining with alcohol or stimulants raises the strain on the body.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

The comedown is fairly gentle, with tiredness, fading visuals and a relaxed, slightly heavy body. Some feel mild headache or a sore jaw. Appetite usually returns as the stimulation fades, and many people feel ready to eat, shower and rest, with a light, slightly sleepy body and a fading glow of warmth.

Mood is usually stable, sometimes slightly flat, and many describe a pleasant afterglow without the deep low that can follow MDMA. Sleep comes more easily than after LSD. Fatigue and low mood the next day are usually mild.

Mixing with other drugs, especially stimulants or MDMA ('nexus flip'), raises risk, and street products may not contain 2C-B at all.

Half-life is about 2.5 hours.

Little hangover for most.

### After-effects (6-8 h)

Light afterglow.

### Next day

Usually little next-day effect.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
