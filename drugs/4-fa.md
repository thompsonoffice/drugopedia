# 4-FA (4-fluoroamphetamine)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Fluorinated amphetamine with stimulant and mild empathogenic effects.

**Brand and street names:** 4-FA, 4-FMP, flux.

## History and origins

Sold online from about 2007, and widely used in the Netherlands until controlled in 2017 after strokes and brain bleeds were reported.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Used as a milder MDMA-amphetamine hybrid.

## Legal status

Controlled in the Netherlands and many EU countries; UN Schedule II (2017).

## What the products look and feel like

Powders or capsules. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

4-FA feels like a cross between amphetamine and MDMA: energy, focus, mild empathy.

Headaches are common, and severe headaches with high blood pressure can signal brain bleeding.

Dutch reports linked it to strokes in young people.

Tolerance to the warmth fades with frequent use while side effects remain.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-90 min)

4-FA (4-fluoroamphetamine), popular in the Netherlands before its ban, has a moderate onset of clean energy and mild warmth. 4-FA (4-FMP) usually begins within an hour or so, with alertness, a rising heart rate, wide pupils and a slight body warmth. The body may feel jittery, with a tight jaw and suppressed appetite, while mild tingling comes and goes. Thoughts feel crisp and quick, and some people feel unusually motivated or sociable as the stimulant lift builds, with a slight buzz behind the eyes.

Mentally a mix of amphetamine focus and mild MDMA-like sociability. Mood lifts into focus, chattiness and a light sociable warmth.

Heart rate rises.

4-FA is absorbed well.

Blood pressure rises substantially.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Energy rises.

### Peak (2-3 h)

At the peak there is euphoria, sociability and energy. The peak sits between amphetamine and MDMA: stimulating and focused with a mild empathogenic glow. The body feels energized, with a fast heartbeat, raised blood pressure, sweating and jaw tension. Severe headaches and strokes have been reported, and blood pressure can climb dangerously. Thoughts race pleasantly, and music and conversation feel engaging.

Emotionally upbeat and talkative. Mood is upbeat and confident, with modest warmth toward others. Pupils are wide, the mouth is dry, and appetite vanishes.

Severe headaches, bleeding in the brain and strokes have been reported, sometimes hours after use.

At peak, severe headache can signal brain bleeding and needs emergency care.

Strokes were reported in the Netherlands.

### Plateau (3-6 h)

The plateau is long, lasting several hours, with continued stimulation, alertness and light euphoria. The body stays energized, with a fast pulse, tight jaw, dry mouth and little appetite, and some people get a pounding headache that warns of high blood pressure. Fatigue hides.

Mood is steady, chatty and focused, more stimulant-like than MDMA, with mild sociability. Some become anxious, restless or irritable as the hours pass.

Severe or sudden headache, chest pain, confusion or weakness needs emergency care, since cerebral hemorrhages have been reported. Mixing with other stimulants or alcohol increases the strain on the heart and blood vessels.

### Comedown (6-8 h)

The comedown is gradual, with lingering stimulation, tiredness, a sore jaw and sometimes headache. The body may feel drained but restless, and appetite returns slowly. The heart may stay quick, the mouth dry, and the jaw tense, while a dull headache or eye strain can linger as the body finally slows down.

Mood often dips mildly into flatness, irritability or low motivation, usually less severe than after MDMA. Some feel anxious or depleted the next day. Concentration may feel patchy. Rest and fluids help recovery.

Sleep is often difficult for many hours. Repeated use builds tolerance and can lead to compulsive redosing, raising cardiovascular risk.

Effects last 4 to 8 hours.

Insomnia follows.

### After-effects (8-24 h)

Insomnia.

### Next day

Low mood.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses empathogens bring warmth, openness and mild stimulation. At higher doses, stimulation, jaw clenching, sweating and overheating rise while the pleasant effects do not increase much. At very high levels, serotonin toxicity, overheating, seizures and collapse can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
