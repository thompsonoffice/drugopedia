# Modafinil and armodafinil (Provigil, Nuvigil)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Wakefulness-promoting agent; weak dopamine reuptake inhibitor.

**Brand and street names:** Provigil, Nuvigil; Modalert and Waklert (India); street: moda.

## History and origins

Developed in France by Lafon Laboratories in the 1970s and 1980s and approved in the US in 1998; armodafinil (the R-enantiomer) in 2007.

## Medical use, then and now

Narcolepsy, sleepiness from obstructive sleep apnea, and shift-work disorder.

## How it entered recreational use

Used as a 'smart drug' by students and workers, often bought online without a prescription.

## Legal status

US Schedule IV.

## What the pills feel like

Per DailyMed, Provigil tablets are white and capsule-shaped, debossed 'PROVIGIL' with the strength number. Products bought online are often unregulated.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Modafinil feels like steady wakefulness rather than a rush: clear and alert, sometimes flat, with little euphoria.

Side effects include headache, nausea, anxiety and insomnia.

Rare but serious skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome) can occur, and it makes hormonal birth control less effective.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 1 h)

Modafinil (Provigil) and its relative armodafinil (Nuvigil) are wakefulness drugs for narcolepsy, sleep apnea and shift-work sleep disorder. Swallowed, they come on slowly over an hour or more. There is no rush: the body simply stops feeling heavy, the eyelids lose their droop and the dragging fatigue of sleepiness lifts.

Mentally the onset is a quiet clearing, like the moment after a good night's sleep and a strong coffee, without the jittery spike. Thoughts feel organized and it becomes easier to start and stay on tasks; many people say they barely notice it beyond not feeling tired.

A dull headache, mild nausea or a slightly 'wired' feeling behind the eyes are the most common early side effects, and drinking less water than usual can make the headache worse.

Modafinil is absorbed slowly, peaking in 2 to 4 hours, and has a half-life of about 15 hours.

It weakly blocks dopamine reuptake and increases histamine, orexin and noradrenaline activity.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Alertness builds.

### Peak (2-4 h)

At its peak the body feels awake and steady, with a slightly faster pulse, a dry mouth and sometimes reduced appetite. Unlike amphetamines it rarely causes a strong heart-pounding or teeth grinding, though some notice tense muscles or a tight jaw.

Mentally there is calm alertness and stamina: long reading, studying or driving sessions feel manageable. Some feel a mild mood lift; others feel flat, a little detached or emotionally muted, as if running on willpower rather than enjoyment.

Higher amounts bring anxiety, irritability, insomnia and a racing mind. Rare but serious skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome) and psychiatric effects have been reported, and it can make hormonal birth control less effective.

At peak, effects are wakefulness and modest focus; high amounts cause anxiety, tachycardia and insomnia.

Rare but serious rashes (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, DRESS) need immediate medical care.

### Plateau (10-15 h)

The plateau is very long, often twelve hours or more, which is why it is taken in the morning. The body stays awake and fairly comfortable, though headaches, dehydration and a dry, metallic taste can build through the day. Some people notice a faint, persistent tension at the temples and a need to urinate more often.

Mentally focus and wakefulness persist, sometimes with a tunnel-vision quality where people forget to eat, drink or take breaks. Late in the plateau some feel tense, restless or 'tired but wired.'

Taking it after midday commonly ruins the night's sleep, and sleep loss stacks up into poorer judgment over days. Because it feels subtle, people sometimes underestimate how impaired their sleep and judgment become after several days.

### Comedown (12-16 h)

As it fades the body feels gradually tired rather than crashing; muscles may ache slightly and hunger returns, sometimes with a lingering headache. Sleep that night may be lighter than usual, and the next morning can feel slow and heavy.

Emotionally the comedown is usually mild, though some notice irritability, low mood or anxiety, especially after several days of short sleep. A few people describe a short-tempered, frayed feeling in the evening, as though they ran out of patience along with the drug.

Its misuse potential is lower than amphetamines but not zero; using it to skip sleep repeatedly leads to exhaustion and mood problems, and any rash means stopping and seeing a doctor.

Its long half-life means sleep may be disrupted the following night.

It speeds up the breakdown of hormonal contraceptives for up to a month after stopping.

### After-effects (next night)

Insomnia if taken late.

### Next day

Usually little next-day effect.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Modafinil and armodafinil (Provigil, Nuvigil)

Overdose causes agitation, insomnia, fast heart rate and high blood pressure; deaths are rare.
