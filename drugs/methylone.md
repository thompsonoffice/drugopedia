# Methylone (bk-MDMA)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic cathinones with MDMA-like effects.

**Brand and street names:** Methylone, bk-MDMA, M1; often sold as 'molly'.

## History and origins

Methylone was patented by Peyton Jacob and Alexander Shulgin in 1996 as a possible antidepressant and sold in the Netherlands as 'Explosion' around 2004. It was one of the most common substitutes in US 'molly' in the early 2010s.

## Medical use, then and now

None; methylone has been studied for PTSD.

## How it entered recreational use

Commonly sold as MDMA at festivals.

## Legal status

US Schedule I (2013); UN 1971 Convention Schedule II (2015).

## What the products look and feel like

Off-white crystals or capsules sold as 'molly'. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Methylone feels like a weaker, shorter MDMA: euphoria, empathy and energy, with more stimulation and less emotional warmth.

Its effects arrive and fade faster than MDMA, which tempts redosing.

People expecting MDMA may take more when it feels different, raising risks of overheating, seizures and low blood sodium.

Tolerance and compulsive redosing develop quickly with cathinones, and binges are common.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Methylone (bk-MDMA, sold as 'M1' or passed off as 'molly') is a cathinone built like MDMA with one extra oxygen. Swallowed, it comes on faster than MDMA, often in under an hour: a warm, rising tingle through the body, a quickening heart, widening pupils and a slight clench in the jaw.

Mentally the onset is an upwelling of happiness and sociability that people compare to a lighter, shorter, more stimulating MDMA. Music feels appealing, conversation comes easily and anxiety drops.

Because it is often sold as MDMA, people may not know what they took, and the shorter, shallower effect tempts redosing, which raises the risk of overheating and heart strain.

Methylone releases serotonin, dopamine and noradrenaline, but less potently than MDMA.

Heart rate rises.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

A quick rise of energy and mild euphoria.

### Peak (1-1.5 h)

At its peak the body is energized and warm, with sweating, a fast pulse, jaw tension and teeth grinding, and sometimes nausea. Touch feels pleasant, but there is less of the melting body-high of MDMA. Body temperature rises, especially when dancing in hot rooms, and some feel flushed.

Mentally there is euphoria, warmth toward others and talkativeness, mixed with a more clear-headed, stimulant buzz. Some feel a background nervousness rather than full openness. Many describe it as more 'talky' than 'huggy'.

Overheating, very low blood sodium from drinking too much water, high blood pressure and serotonin toxicity (especially with antidepressants or other drugs) are the main dangers.

At peak, overheating and low sodium are the main dangers.

Redosing increases risk.

### Plateau (2-3 h)

The plateau is fairly short, an hour or two, and the body stays wired, hot and thirsty. Repeated redosing keeps the heart racing and the jaw sore. Nausea may return, and the jaw and tongue can become sore from clenching and chewing. Sweat may soak clothing, and some people shiver despite feeling hot.

Mentally the warmth thins into restless energy and an urge to keep the night going, with less emotional depth each time. Conversations can loop, and some feel let down that the warmth was not deeper.

Signs like confusion, very high temperature, muscle rigidity or seizures need emergency care. Drinking small amounts of water steadily, resting and cooling off reduce risk.

### Comedown (3-5 h)

The comedown brings tiredness, aching jaw and muscles, poor sleep and dehydration. Some feel achy and drained the next day, with poor appetite and a sore jaw. Some notice tender muscles from hours of dancing and clenching.

Emotionally people may feel low, anxious and irritable for a day or two, though often less severely than after heavy MDMA use. Concentration can feel fuzzy and sleep may be shallow. A short-lived sadness, sometimes called a 'Tuesday blues,' can appear midweek.

Frequent use can lead to compulsive redosing and low mood between uses; spacing and avoiding mixing with other stimulants or alcohol reduce harm. Taking it with antidepressants, tramadol or other serotonin drugs is especially risky.

Half-life is short.

Low mood may follow.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Insomnia and tiredness.

### Next day

Low mood the next day.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cathinones bring energy, euphoria and sociability. At higher doses, agitation, a racing heart, sweating, paranoia and a strong urge to redose dominate. At very high levels, psychosis, overheating, seizures, muscle breakdown and heart failure can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.
