# Heroin (diacetylmorphine)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Semi-synthetic opioid made from morphine; a prodrug that becomes 6-monoacetylmorphine and morphine in the body, and enters the brain faster than morphine.

**Brand and street names:** Heroin (Bayer, 1898 to about 1913); Diamorphine (UK medical use).

## History and origins

C.R. Alder Wright first made diacetylmorphine in London in 1874. In 1897 Felix Hoffmann at Bayer (who also made aspirin) re-made it, and Bayer sold it from 1898 as 'Heroin' (from German 'heroisch', heroic), a cough medicine and supposedly non-addictive morphine substitute, even for children. Within a few years its addictiveness was obvious; Bayer stopped making it around 1913, the US Harrison Act of 1914 restricted it, and it was banned in the US in 1924. Heroin waves followed in the 1950s to 1970s (including among Vietnam veterans) and again from the 2000s, when people with prescription opioid addiction switched to cheaper heroin. Since about 2013, the US heroin supply has been largely replaced or contaminated by fentanyl.

## Medical use, then and now

As diamorphine, used in the UK and some other countries for severe pain, heart attacks, and palliative care. Switzerland, the Netherlands, Germany, Denmark, and Canada run heroin-assisted treatment for people for whom other treatments have not worked.

## How it entered recreational use

One of the most well-known drugs of misuse in the world, linked with jazz and rock subcultures and later with the overdose crisis.

## Legal status

US Schedule I (no accepted medical use). UK Class A, but prescribable as diamorphine.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Heroin is often described as the deepest, warmest, most complete opioid euphoria: a body-wide warmth and safety, "like being hugged by God" or "returning to the womb", with all pain, anxiety, and loneliness gone. Compared with morphine it arrives faster and feels sharper; compared with fentanyl it is warmer, dreamier, and longer.

Writers like Thomas De Quincey (on opium), William Burroughs, and many people in recovery have described how this feeling becomes the center of life, and then turns into the daily need to avoid withdrawal. Today, almost all street 'heroin' in North America contains fentanyl, so the experience and risk are no longer what they were.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (IV seconds; IM/SC 5-15 min)

Smoked, heroin acts within seconds; injected, within seconds to a minute; snorted, a few minutes. The first feeling is a rush of warmth from the stomach up through the chest into the head.

### Come-up (IV under 1 min; IM/SC 10-20 min)

Injected, the rush is described as overwhelming: a hot flush, a sense of release, heavy limbs, and sometimes nausea or vomiting. Smoked or snorted, it is gentler and builds over minutes.

### Peak (IV 2-10 min; IM/SC 20-60 min)

The peak is the deep heroin calm: complete safety and warmth, pain and worry gone, heavy eyelids, pinpoint pupils, dry mouth, and strong itch. Many describe it as the most comforting feeling of their lives.

### Plateau (30 min-2 h)

The plateau is classic nodding: drifting in and out, chin dropping, dreamy visions, then waking with a start, for hours.

### Comedown (2-4 h)

After 3 to 5 hours, the comedown starts: warmth fades, and in dependent people craving and restlessness begin.

### After-effects (4-12 h)

Constipation, dry mouth, and fatigue. With street supply now dominated by fentanyl, duration and intensity are unpredictable.

### Next day

After regular use, withdrawal begins 6 to 12 hours after last use and peaks at 1 to 3 days: sweats, chills, goosebumps, aches, diarrhea, vomiting, restless legs, and insomnia.

## How the route changes the experience

- [Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)](../routes/injected/heroin.md): IV, IM ('muscling'), and SC ('skin-popping'); medical diamorphine injection in the UK.
- [Inhaled and smoked](../routes/inhaled-smoked/heroin.md): 'Chasing the dragon', smoking vapor from foil.
- [Intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting)](../routes/intranasal/heroin.md): Snorting, common with powder heroin; medical diamorphine nasal spray is used for children's pain in UK emergency departments.
- [Oral (swallowed)](../routes/oral/heroin.md): Medical diamorphine tablets exist in the UK; swallowed heroin largely turns into morphine and feels like oral morphine.

Not covered: No transdermal or common rectal use; rectal use exists but is rare and not covered.

## Overdose note for Heroin (diacetylmorphine)

Since street heroin now usually contains fentanyl, overdose risk is unpredictable. Overdoses cluster after time away from use (jail, rehab, hospital), when tolerance is lower.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). Also see [naloxone (Narcan)](../narcan/naloxone.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
