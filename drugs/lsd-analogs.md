# LSD analogs (1P-LSD, ALD-52, AL-LAD, 1cP-LSD, ETH-LAD)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Lysergamides closely related to LSD.

**Brand and street names:** 1P-LSD, 1cP-LSD, 1V-LSD, ALD-52, AL-LAD, ETH-LAD, LSZ.

## History and origins

Several were studied by Hofmann and later by Alexander Shulgin; from 2015 1P-LSD and others were sold online as legal research chemicals in Europe. Many are now controlled.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Sold online as 'legal LSD'.

## Legal status

Varies; 1P-LSD and others are controlled in many EU countries; in the US, analog laws may apply, and ALD-52 is Schedule I.

## What the products look and feel like

Blotter squares. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Many analogs (1P-LSD, ALD-52) are thought to act as prodrugs that turn into LSD in the body, so effects feel very similar.

AL-LAD is often described as shorter and more visual; ETH-LAD as stronger.

Because they are sold online with uncertain purity, contents may not match the label.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-90 min)

LSD analogs such as 1P-LSD, 1cP-LSD, AL-LAD and ETH-LAD are research chemicals sold online, often as blotters. Some, like 1P-LSD, appear to be converted to LSD in the body. The onset is like LSD: a mild, tingly body energy, yawning, a slight tightness in the jaw and stomach, and a sense that something is shifting. Pupils widen early.

Mentally there is anticipation and a slight brightening of thought and color, along with a buzzy, slightly anxious alertness.

Because these are unregulated, what is sold may not be what the label says, and some 'LSD' blotters contain dangerous NBOMes instead. A bitter or numbing taste on the blotter is a warning sign.

Most 1-acyl analogs are converted to LSD by enzymes in the blood and liver.

Body signs mirror LSD.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Visual and emotional changes build.

### Peak (2-5 h)

At the peak, breathing surfaces, color enhancement, trails and geometric patterns are common. AL-LAD is often described as more visual, playful and shorter; ETH-LAD as more intense, immersive and bodily; 1P-LSD as nearly identical to LSD.

Emotionally the peak can be euphoric, insightful, funny or anxious and confusing. Thoughts loop and branch, and ordinary things can feel deeply significant.

Psychedelics rarely threaten the body directly, but they impair judgment and coordination, so falls, traffic, water and heights are real dangers. Panic, paranoia and terrifying experiences can happen to anyone, and people with a personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder face a higher risk of lasting harm.

At peak, effects are those of LSD or a close relative at 5-HT2A.

Panic, confusion and accidents are the main dangers.

### Plateau (4-7 h)

The plateau of LSD analogs is long, often many hours, with continued visuals such as breathing walls, color shifts and geometric patterns, and a buzzing, energized body that makes it hard to sit still or rest. Some people feel cold or warm in waves, with a tight jaw and occasional yawning.

Mood varies, moving between wonder, laughter, deep reflection and fatigue, and thought loops can be absorbing or tiring. Time feels distorted, and people may lose track of how long they have been in the experience. AL-LAD's plateau tends to be shorter and lighter; ETH-LAD's heavier and more immersive.

Body energy can make resting difficult, and decisions or travel during this stretch are risky because judgment remains impaired.

### Comedown (7-12 h)

The comedown is a slow taper with lingering stimulation, light visuals such as faint patterns or color trails, and a wired tiredness. The body may feel drained but restless, with a slight headache or sore jaw.

Mood is often reflective and calm, sometimes drained, flat or a little anxious, especially if the trip was intense. Many people want quiet, food and gentle company. Many people feel a quiet sense of perspective, though some notice lingering unease, irritability or difficulty concentrating into the next day, which usually passes with rest.

Sleep can be hard for hours after the visuals fade. Tolerance builds immediately and cross-tolerance with LSD and psilocybin means repeated use over consecutive days produces much weaker effects.

Durations are similar to or slightly shorter than LSD.

HPPD and lasting anxiety are possible.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Insomnia.

### Next day

Next-day tiredness.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
