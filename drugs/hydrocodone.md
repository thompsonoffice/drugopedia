# Hydrocodone

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Semi-synthetic opioid made from codeine (or thebaine); full mu-opioid agonist.

**Brand and street names:** Vicodin, Lortab, Norco, Lorcet, Zohydro ER, Hysingla ER, Vicoprofen (with ibuprofen), Hycodan and Tussionex (cough).

## History and origins

Synthesized in Germany by Carl Mannich and Helene Löwenheim in 1920 and approved in the US in 1943. Through the 2000s, hydrocodone with acetaminophen (Vicodin and Norco) was the single most prescribed medication in the United States.

## Medical use, then and now

Moderate to severe pain, and cough in some products. Extended-release forms are used for long-term pain.

## How it entered recreational use

Vicodin became a pop-culture name in the 1990s and 2000s (for example in TV's House, M.D.) and was central to the early prescription opioid epidemic. In 2014 the DEA moved combination products to Schedule II, sharply reducing prescriptions.

## Legal status

US Schedule II (all forms since 2014). Not marketed in most of Europe.

## What it feels like

### The character of its euphoria

Hydrocodone is described as a friendly, mellow, talkative euphoria, a little lighter and more relaxed than oxycodone, with a pleasant warm body glow. Many people describe it as "feel-good" and sociable at first, then sleepy. It is less sharp and less 'clean-headed' than oxycodone.

Itching is common. Many combination tablets contain acetaminophen, which does not add to the high but makes misuse especially dangerous for the liver.

Tolerance changes everything below. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

The first sign is often subtle: a faint warmth in the stomach or chest, a slight heaviness behind the eyes, and a loosening of tension in the shoulders. People describe it as the edge coming off whatever they were feeling. With Hydrocodone, many say they notice their breathing slow slightly and their thoughts get quieter before they feel anything they would call pleasure.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

The warmth spreads outward to the arms, legs, and face. Pain, if present, fades into the background rather than disappearing outright; people describe still knowing it is there but no longer caring. A calm, contented mood rises. Itching, usually moderate, often starts on the nose, face, or upper body, caused by histamine release and by opioid effects on the nervous system. Some people feel a wave of nausea at this point, especially if they are new to opioids or moving around.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At the peak, people describe a deep, cozy contentment, as if wrapped in a warm blanket, with worries feeling far away and unimportant. Body feels heavy and relaxed; speech can slow. Pupils shrink to pinpoints. People often describe a chatty, contented warmth and a sense that everything is fine. This is also when breathing is most suppressed and overdose is most likely, often without the person noticing anything wrong.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The effects level off into a steady, calm state. "Nodding" (moderate with Hydrocodone) can occur: drifting in and out of a light sleep, head dropping, then jerking awake, sometimes mid-sentence or with a cigarette or drink in hand. People report vivid, dreamlike half-sleep. Talkativeness or a quiet, inward contentment are both common. Mouth dryness, flushed skin, and difficulty urinating can appear.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

The glow fades gradually. People describe the world getting "louder" again: light, noise, and small problems regain their weight. Tiredness and a flat, slightly gray mood are common. Pain, if it was being treated, returns. In people with dependence, this is where restlessness, yawning, and craving start.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Over the following hours: constipation, dry mouth, mild headache, and sluggishness. Sleep may be heavy but not restful. Some feel a mild low mood or irritability as the drug clears.

### Next day

After a single medical exposure, most people feel close to normal, perhaps a little foggy or constipated. After repeated use, the next day can bring early withdrawal: anxiety, runny nose, goosebumps, aches, diarrhea, and strong craving. Tolerance builds quickly, which means the same effect needs more drug and overdose risk rises, especially after a break, when tolerance drops but habits do not.

## How the route changes the experience

- [Oral (swallowed)](../routes/oral/hydrocodone.md): Almost all hydrocodone is swallowed: tablets, capsules, extended-release, liquids.
- [Intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting)](../routes/intranasal/hydrocodone.md): Misused by snorting; acetaminophen fillers make it painful and harmful to nasal tissue.

Not covered: No injected, rectal, transdermal, sublingual, or smoked medical forms; injection of tablets is dangerous because of fillers.

## Overdose note for Hydrocodone

Acetaminophen combination products can cause fatal liver failure. Extended-release forms (Zohydro, Hysingla) hold a large reserve that is released all at once if damaged.

Read the shared page: [What an overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md).

## Getting help
