# Hydrocodone

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Semi-synthetic opioid made from codeine (or thebaine); full mu-opioid agonist.

**Brand and street names:** Vicodin, Lortab, Norco, Lorcet, Zohydro ER, Hysingla ER, Vicoprofen (with ibuprofen), Hycodan and Tussionex (cough).

## History and origins

Synthesized in Germany by Carl Mannich and Helene Löwenheim in 1920 and approved in the US in 1943. Through the 2000s, hydrocodone with acetaminophen (Vicodin and Norco) was the single most prescribed medication in the United States.

## Medical use, then and now

Moderate to severe pain, and cough in some products. Extended-release forms are used for long-term pain.

## How it entered recreational use

Vicodin became a pop-culture name in the 1990s and 2000s (for example in TV's House, M.D.) and was central to the early prescription opioid epidemic. In 2014 the DEA moved combination products to Schedule II, sharply reducing prescriptions.

## Legal status

US Schedule II (all forms since 2014). Not marketed in most of Europe.

## What the pills feel like

Hydrocodone-acetaminophen tablets (Norco and generics) are usually oblong (capsule-shaped) tablets, fairly large because of the acetaminophen, typically with a score line down the middle and a debossed imprint. Hysingla ER is an oblong, film-coated tablet; Zohydro ER is a capsule.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Hydrocodone is described as a friendly, mellow, talkative euphoria, a little lighter and more relaxed than oxycodone, with a pleasant warm body glow. Many people describe it as "feel-good" and sociable at first, then sleepy. It is less sharp and less 'clean-headed' than oxycodone.

Itching is common. Many combination tablets contain acetaminophen, which does not add to the high but makes misuse especially dangerous for the liver.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Hydrocodone usually starts 15 to 30 minutes after swallowing with a pleasant warmth in the chest and a lift in mood, sometimes before any body heaviness. Many describe a sudden sense that the day has gotten easier.

From a medical point of view, the onset reflects how quickly the drug reaches the brain. Hydrocodone is metabolized by CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 (into hydromorphone); half-life about 4 hours. As blood levels rise, mu-opioid receptors in the brainstem, limbic system, and spinal cord begin to activate, which is why pain signals soften and mood changes before the body feels fully affected.

Clinicians watch for early signs at this stage: the pupils begin to narrow, breathing rate drops slightly, the face may flush from histamine release, and the gut slows. Patients often report relief of tension or pain before any pleasure, and some feel lightheaded when standing because blood vessels relax.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

The come-up brings a talkative, friendly glow. People report wanting to chat, clean, or do small tasks, a 'productive buzz' lighter than oxycodone. A mild itch on the face or arms is common, and some feel light nausea.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At the peak, around 1 to 1.5 hours, hydrocodone gives a warm, contented, sociable euphoria with a relaxed, comfortable body. Compared with oxycodone, people describe it as softer, dreamier, and less sharp. Pupils constrict and breathing slows.

Medically, the peak corresponds to the highest drug levels at the receptors. The same receptors that produce euphoria and pain relief also tell the brainstem to breathe less, so respiratory depression is greatest during the peak. Oxygen levels can fall without the person noticing, because opioids blunt the brain's alarm response to rising carbon dioxide.

Hospital staff monitor sedation and breathing most closely at this point. Signs that the effect is turning dangerous include deepening sleepiness, snoring or gurgling, breathing slower than about one breath every five seconds, and difficulty waking. Mixing with alcohol, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or other sedatives makes this phase far more dangerous.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The plateau tilts toward sleepiness: the chatty phase gives way to quiet contentment and light nodding, especially when lying down.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

The comedown, around 4 to 6 hours, is gradual. People describe a slide into tiredness and mild irritability as the warmth leaves, and some feel the urge to take more, which is how use escalates.

The comedown reflects falling blood levels as the liver and kidneys clear the drug. Receptor activity decreases, and the brain's normal pain and stress signals return. In people who have used opioids regularly, the nervous system has adapted, so falling levels trigger the beginnings of withdrawal: increased noradrenaline activity causes anxiety, sweating, yawning, and goosebumps.

For long-acting or extended-release forms, the comedown can be very drawn out, and drug can remain active well after the person feels 'normal'. That is one reason taking more during the comedown is a common cause of overdose: the new amount stacks on drug that is still present.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

After-effects include constipation, dry mouth, and sometimes headache. Because many products contain acetaminophen, repeated use can damage the liver without warning signs.

### Next day

After a single use, the next day is usually normal. After regular use, withdrawal appears within a day: anxiety, sweats, yawning, aches, and strong cravings.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance is the biggest factor, since daily users may need many times more to feel the same effect, and tolerance drops quickly after a break. Liver enzymes (especially CYP2D6 and CYP3A4) vary by genetics and are changed by other medicines and even grapefruit. Body size, age, kidney and liver health, sleep apnea, and lung disease all matter. Mixing with alcohol, benzodiazepines, or other sedatives multiplies the effect on breathing. Stronger effects do not stop at a higher level of pleasure: the line between deep sedation and overdose is thin and invisible from the inside.

## How the route changes the experience

- [Oral (swallowed)](../routes/oral/hydrocodone.md): Almost all hydrocodone is swallowed: tablets, capsules, extended-release, liquids.
- [Intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting)](../routes/intranasal/hydrocodone.md): Misused by snorting; acetaminophen fillers make it painful and harmful to nasal tissue.

Not covered: No injected, rectal, transdermal, sublingual, or smoked medical forms; injection of tablets is dangerous because of fillers.

## Overdose note for Hydrocodone

Acetaminophen combination products can cause fatal liver failure. Extended-release forms (Zohydro, Hysingla) hold a large reserve that is released all at once if damaged.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). Also see [naloxone (Narcan)](../narcan/naloxone.md).
