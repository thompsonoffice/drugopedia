# Quetiapine (Seroquel) as misused

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Atypical antipsychotic with strong antihistamine effects.

**Brand and street names:** Seroquel, Seroquel XR. Street: 'quell', 'baby heroin', 'Susie-Q'.

## History and origins

Approved in 1997. Widely prescribed off-label for sleep; misuse is reported in prisons and among people who use other drugs.

## Medical use, then and now

Schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and add-on treatment for depression.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused for sedation and to ease stimulant comedowns.

## Legal status

Prescription-only, not controlled federally.

## Immediate-release vs extended-release

Immediate-release quetiapine acts within an hour; Seroquel XR releases slowly over the day. XR tablets should be swallowed whole.

## What the products look and feel like

Per DailyMed, Seroquel tablets are round or capsule-shaped, film-coated, in various colors by strength.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Quetiapine causes heavy sedation, dizziness and weight gain.

It can drop blood pressure on standing and prolong the QT interval.

Mixing with opioids or alcohol deepens sedation.

Tolerance and dependence vary by substance.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Sedation within an hour.

Quetiapine is absorbed quickly.

Blocks H1 receptors (sedation) and alpha-1 receptors (low blood pressure).

### Come-up (1 h)

Heaviness.

### Peak (1-2 h)

Peak at 1 to 2 hours.

At high levels, deep sedation and QT prolongation.

Mixing with depressants adds danger.

### Plateau (4-8 h)

Sedation for many hours.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

Slow fade.

Half-life about 6 hours (XR longer).

Morning grogginess.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Grogginess.

### Next day

Next-day hangover.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses effects are mild; at higher doses side effects and toxicity dominate, as described for each substance.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Quetiapine (Seroquel) as misused

Overdose causes deep sedation, low blood pressure, fast heart rate and arrhythmias.

Read the shared page: [Other psychoactives safety](../safety/other-psychoactives-safety.md).
