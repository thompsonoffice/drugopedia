# Phentermine (Adipex-P, Lomaira) and benzphetamine

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Amphetamine-like appetite suppressants (anorectics).

**Brand and street names:** Phentermine: Adipex-P, Lomaira; with topiramate in Qsymia. Benzphetamine: Didrex, Regimex. Withdrawn relatives: fenfluramine (Pondimin), sibutramine (Meridia).

## History and origins

Phentermine was approved in 1959. In the 1990s 'fen-phen' (fenfluramine with phentermine) was linked to heart valve damage and pulmonary hypertension, and fenfluramine was withdrawn in 1997. Sibutramine was withdrawn in 2010 for heart risks.

## Medical use, then and now

Short-term weight loss; fenfluramine returned in 2020 (Fintepla) for rare childhood epilepsies.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused for energy and weight loss; less euphoric than amphetamine.

## Legal status

Phentermine and diethylpropion: Schedule IV; phendimetrazine and benzphetamine: Schedule III.

## What the pills feel like

Per DailyMed, Adipex-P capsules are blue and white, and Adipex-P tablets are white with blue specks. Stimulant drugs sold on the street, including counterfeit Adderall, are often contaminated with methamphetamine or fentanyl.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Phentermine feels like a mild amphetamine: alert, wired and not hungry, with dry mouth and insomnia.

It raises heart rate and blood pressure and can cause anxiety.

Older anorectics carried risks of heart valve disease and pulmonary hypertension.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 1 h)

Phentermine (Adipex-P, Lomaira, and part of Qsymia) is an amphetamine relative prescribed for short-term weight loss. Swallowed, it comes on gradually over an hour or so: the mouth dries, the heart beats a little faster and hunger quietly switches off.

Mentally the onset is an energetic, slightly restless lift. People feel more driven and talkative, with a busy mind; it is less euphoric than amphetamine for most. Some people feel a pleasant sense of control over eating that can be motivating at first.

Some notice a jittery, buzzing feeling in the chest or a bitter, metallic taste early on, and trouble sitting still. People often notice they are tapping, pacing or cleaning more than usual within the first hour.

Phentermine is absorbed well and releases noradrenaline more than dopamine.

Heart rate and blood pressure rise.

### Come-up (1-3 h)

Energy rises and appetite drops.

### Peak (3-4 h)

At its peak the body is wired: raised pulse and blood pressure, dry mouth, sweating, constipation and sometimes a tremor or tight jaw. Food feels unnecessary or unappealing. Some feel their heart thumping in their ears, and urination may become difficult.

Mentally there is energy and motivation that can tip into irritability, anxiety and an edgy, short-tempered mood. Some feel mildly euphoric; others just feel tense. Conversation can speed up, and small delays or frustrations may feel unusually irritating.

Risks include high blood pressure, heart rhythm problems and, rarely, lung hypertension or psychosis; it is not for people with heart disease or hyperthyroidism.

At high levels, agitation, psychosis and arrhythmias can occur.

Combined with serotonergic drugs (as with fenfluramine in fen-phen), heart valve and lung artery damage occurred.

### Plateau (8-12 h)

The plateau lasts much of the day, which is why it is taken in the morning. The body stays mildly revved, with restless energy and very little appetite. The mouth may feel sticky and dry, and some people chew gum or sip water constantly; headaches and constipation can build.

Mentally people feel driven and busy, sometimes scattered or obsessive about tasks, and may become easily annoyed in the afternoon. Some feel productive and upbeat, while others feel trapped in a restless, can't-switch-off state that wears on them by evening.

Late doses cause insomnia, and poor sleep worsens the edginess the following day. Blood pressure and pulse stay elevated for hours, which is why regular checks are part of treatment.

### Comedown (12-16 h)

As it wears off, tiredness, headache and returning hunger arrive, sometimes with an intense craving for carbohydrates. Some people feel a heavy, sluggish drag in the evening and a strong urge to eat everything they skipped.

Emotionally people may feel low, irritable or anxious. The contrast between daytime drive and evening fatigue can feel discouraging. Some people feel guilty about evening eating, which can make the cycle harder.

Tolerance to its appetite effect develops within weeks; stopping after long or high use can bring fatigue and low mood for some days. Combining it with other stimulants or certain antidepressants raises serious risks, so all medicines should be reviewed with the prescriber.

Half-life is about 20 hours, explaining day-long effects.

Insomnia and dry mouth are common.

### After-effects (next night)

Insomnia.

### Next day

Possible next-day fatigue.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Phentermine (Adipex-P, Lomaira) and benzphetamine

Overdose causes agitation, high blood pressure, fast heart rate and psychosis.
