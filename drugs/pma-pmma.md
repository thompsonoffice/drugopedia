# PMA and PMMA ('Dr. Death')

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Toxic amphetamines sold as ecstasy.

**Brand and street names:** PMA, PMMA, 'Dr. Death', 'Superman' pills.

## History and origins

PMA appeared in the 1970s in Canada, causing deaths. Pills containing PMA or PMMA have caused clusters of deaths worldwide since the 1990s, including the 'Superman' pills in the UK in 2015.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Almost always taken unknowingly as ecstasy.

## Legal status

US Schedule I; UN Schedule I.

## What the products look and feel like

Pressed pills sold as ecstasy. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

PMA and PMMA feel weak and slow, with little euphoria, so people take more, thinking their ecstasy is weak.

They cause much greater overheating than MDMA.

They are among the most dangerous drugs sold as ecstasy.

Tolerance to the warmth fades with frequent use while side effects remain.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-2 h)

PMA and PMMA are often sold as fake ecstasy. The onset is slow and weak, which leads people to take more — a deadly pattern.

Mentally there is less euphoria than expected, and some feel disappointed or confused.

Body heat and heart rate climb steadily while the person feels relatively little.

PMA is absorbed slowly, so effects seem weak at first.

Temperature rises more than with MDMA.

### Come-up (2-3 h)

Mild stimulation without much euphoria.

### Peak (3-5 h)

At the peak, people may feel only mildly high while their body is overheating dangerously, with flushed skin, rigid muscles and a racing heart.

Emotionally confused or agitated.

PMA/PMMA have caused many deaths from extreme overheating and organ failure. A very hot, confused person needs 911 immediately.

At peak, serotonin release plus MAO-A inhibition can cause extreme overheating and serotonin toxicity.

Redosing after a 'weak' first pill is the classic fatal pattern.

### Plateau (5-8 h)

The plateau is dangerous.

Thinking confused.

Cooling and medical help are crucial.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

Survivors feel exhausted.

Mood low.

Never re-dose a drug that seems weak.

Long half-life prolongs danger.

Survivors may have organ damage.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Exhaustion.

### Next day (days)

Days of recovery.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses empathogens bring warmth, openness and mild stimulation. At higher doses, stimulation, jaw clenching, sweating and overheating rise while the pleasant effects do not increase much. At very high levels, serotonin toxicity, overheating, seizures and collapse can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

PMA and PMMA cause severe overheating, seizures and death.
