# 6-APB, 5-APB, 5-MAPB and other benzofurans

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Benzofuran empathogens related to MDA.

**Brand and street names:** 6-APB ('Benzo Fury'), 5-APB, 5-MAPB, 6-MAPB, 5-EAPB.

## History and origins

Described by David Briner in 1993 (patent) and sold as legal highs ('Benzo Fury') in the UK from about 2010 until controlled in 2013-2014.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Sold as legal MDMA substitutes.

## Legal status

Controlled in the UK and many EU countries; 5-APB and 6-APB under US analog laws.

## What the products look and feel like

Powders or pellets. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

6-APB and relatives feel like a long, warm MDMA with more visual effects.

Onset is slow, which led people to redose.

They act on 5-HT2B receptors, raising theoretical concern about heart valve damage with repeated use.

Tolerance to the warmth fades with frequent use while side effects remain.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-2 h)

Benzofurans (5-APB, 6-APB, sometimes sold as 'Benzo Fury') have a slow onset of warm energy, tingling and gentle stimulation. Benzofurans such as 5-APB, 6-APB and 5-MAPB are slower to begin than MDMA, often taking an hour or two, which tempts people to take more. The body warms gradually, with tingling, wide pupils, a rising pulse, a tight jaw and sometimes nausea or a heavy stomach. Pupils widen and the pulse climbs as warmth spreads.

Mentally a loving, MDMA-like openness develops, often described as smoother but longer. Anticipation, restlessness and mild anxiety can precede the lift in mood. Skin flushes.

Nausea, jaw tension and dilated pupils appear.

Benzofurans are absorbed slowly, which delays onset.

Heart rate rises.

### Come-up (2-3 h)

Slow build.

### Peak (3-5 h)

At the peak there is warmth, euphoria and mild visuals. Body heat, sweating, jaw tension and tactile pleasure are strong, and the heart stays fast. Touch feels lovely and music feels immersive, while the body stays hot.

Emotionally affectionate and sociable.

Empathogens push body temperature, heart rate and blood pressure up. Overheating, dehydration, and low blood sodium from drinking too much water are the classic dangers; combining with other stimulants, MAOIs, SSRIs, tramadol or DXM can cause serotonin syndrome. A hot, confused, rigid or seizing person needs 911 and cooling. There are also concerns about heart valve damage with repeated use.

At peak, empathogenic and visual effects; overheating is possible.

Strong 5-HT2B activation is linked to valve disease with chronic exposure.

### Plateau (5-7 h)

The plateau is long, often longer than MDMA, with sustained warmth, gentle euphoria, emotional openness and mild visuals such as color brightening and soft patterns. The body stays energized, with jaw clenching, sweating and a fast heartbeat. Jaw strain builds.

Mood is affectionate and talkative, and some describe it as a gentler, more drawn-out version of MDMA. Others notice more stimulation and anxiety, and the long duration can become tiring.

Overheating, dehydration, serotonin toxicity and heart strain are real risks, and some benzofurans may affect heart valves with repeated use. Mixing with other stimulants or antidepressants is dangerous.

### Comedown (7-12 h)

The comedown is slow, with stimulation lingering for hours after euphoria fades. The body feels tired, achy and restless, with a sore jaw, headache and poor appetite. Muscles feel stiff and sore, the skin feels sweaty or chilled, and the heart may stay quick and fluttery for hours.

Mood can drop into flatness, irritability or anxiety, sometimes for a day or two afterward, as with other serotonin releasers. Many people feel emotionally drained and want rest. Gentle food, fluids and rest help.

Sleep is often disrupted well into the night. Because onset is slow and products are poorly characterized, redosing and accidental high amounts are a frequent problem.

Long duration extends risk.

Insomnia is common.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Insomnia.

### Next day

Low mood next day.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses empathogens bring warmth, openness and mild stimulation. At higher doses, stimulation, jaw clenching, sweating and overheating rise while the pleasant effects do not increase much. At very high levels, serotonin toxicity, overheating, seizures and collapse can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for 6-APB, 5-APB, 5-MAPB and other benzofurans

Overheating, anxiety and heart effects are reported; deaths often involved other drugs.
