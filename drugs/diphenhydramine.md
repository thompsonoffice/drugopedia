# Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) as misused

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

First-generation antihistamine with strong anticholinergic effects.

**Brand and street names:** Benadryl, ZzzQuil, Unisom SleepGels, Tylenol PM and Advil PM (combinations), Sominex. Street: 'the hat man drug'.

## History and origins

Discovered by George Rieveschl in 1943 and approved in 1946 as the first prescription antihistamine; made over-the-counter in the 1980s. A 2020 TikTok 'Benadryl challenge' led to an FDA warning after teen deaths.

## Medical use, then and now

Allergies, hives, motion sickness, sleep aid, and as part of treatment for severe allergic reactions and drug-induced movement disorders.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused at very high amounts for delirium; the experience is almost universally described as frightening.

## Legal status

Over-the-counter; not controlled.

## What the products look and feel like

Pink tablets (Benadryl) or blue gel caps (sleep aids). Check for combination ingredients.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

At medical levels diphenhydramine is simply sedating. Far above that, it becomes a deliriant: confusion, muttering, and realistic hallucinations such as shadow people, insects, or imaginary objects.

People often cannot tell hallucinations from reality, and many describe dark, threatening figures ('the hat man').

It can cause seizures and dangerous heart rhythms, and combination products add acetaminophen or ibuprofen toxicity.

Tolerance develops to some effects, but delirium risk remains.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Drowsiness begins within 30 to 60 minutes.

Diphenhydramine is absorbed well and crosses easily into the brain, unlike newer antihistamines.

Blocking histamine H1 receptors causes sedation; blocking muscarinic receptors causes dry mouth, flushing, wide pupils, urinary retention and fast heart rate.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Dry mouth, fast heartbeat, and confusion at higher amounts.

### Peak (2-4 h)

Peak delirium at 2 to 4 hours.

At high levels, muscarinic blockade in the brain causes delirium, and sodium channel blockade in the heart widens the QRS complex, risking fatal arrhythmias.

Seizures are common in severe poisoning; overheating occurs because sweating stops.

### Plateau (4-8 h)

Delirium can last many hours.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

Long comedown with confusion.

Delirium can outlast the drowsiness by many hours.

Chronic use in older adults is linked to cognitive decline and is on the Beers list of drugs to avoid.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Grogginess and memory gaps.

### Next day

Next-day fog.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses deliriants cause drowsiness, dry mouth and a fast heartbeat. At higher doses, confusion, blurred vision and hallucinations start. At very high levels, full delirium with realistic hallucinations, overheating, seizures and dangerous heart rhythms occur; no one finds this pleasant.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) as misused

Diphenhydramine overdose causes delirium, seizures, high temperature, dangerous heart rhythms and death.

Read the shared page: [Deliriant safety](../safety/deliriant-safety.md).
