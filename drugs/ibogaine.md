# Ibogaine (iboga)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Indole alkaloid from the Tabernanthe iboga shrub; oneirogen.

**Brand and street names:** Iboga root bark; ibogaine hydrochloride.

## History and origins

Used in the Bwiti religion of Gabon and Cameroon. Isolated in 1901. Sold in France as Lambarène until 1966. Howard Lotsof reported in 1962 that it ended his heroin cravings; clinics in Mexico and elsewhere offer it for addiction. Texas funded ibogaine research in 2025.

## Medical use, then and now

No approved use; studied for opioid use disorder and traumatic brain injury.

## How it entered recreational use

Mostly sought for addiction treatment, not recreation.

## Legal status

US Schedule I; legal or unregulated in Mexico, New Zealand (prescription), and others.

## What the products look and feel like

Root bark or capsules of uncertain purity.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Ibogaine produces a long, dreamlike state with life-review visions, followed by many hours of wakeful reflection.

It is physically punishing: severe nausea, loss of coordination, and inability to walk.

It prolongs the QT interval of the heart and has caused sudden cardiac deaths, especially in people with heart problems, low potassium or magnesium, or opioids in their system.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-3 h)

Ibogaine, from the root bark of the West African iboga shrub, comes on slowly with nausea, dizziness, and a heavy, uncoordinated body. Moving the head makes nausea and spinning worse, and a buzzing, droning or ringing sound is common. Many people lie still for hours. Ataxia, dizziness and nausea often arrive early, with vomiting common and walking unsteady. A low hum or buzzing sound in the ears is frequently described, and the heart rhythm may already be affected.

Mentally a dreamlike state begins, with closed-eye visions that feel like watching a film. The mind becomes inward.

Coordination becomes very poor and walking can be impossible without help.

Ibogaine is absorbed slowly and converted by CYP2D6 into noribogaine.

Nausea, vomiting and severe loss of coordination occur early.

### Come-up (2-4 h)

Nausea and ataxia.

### Peak (4-8 h)

At the peak, long, vivid, dreamlike visions, often of personal memories, childhood scenes or symbolic figures, play out with eyes closed for hours.

Emotionally the experience is confronting and introspective rather than euphoric; it is studied for opioid addiction because it can reduce withdrawal and craving.

Ibogaine can cause dangerous heart rhythm problems (QT prolongation) and has caused many deaths, especially in people using opioids or with heart conditions. It requires medical screening and cardiac monitoring. Ibogaine prolongs the QT interval of the heart and has caused fatal arrhythmias, especially in people with heart disease, low potassium or magnesium, or those still using opioids.

At peak, ibogaine blocks cardiac hERG potassium channels, lengthening the QT interval and risking torsades de pointes and sudden death.

Medical screening (ECG, electrolytes) and monitoring are essential; deaths have occurred at unregulated clinics.

### Plateau (8-20 h)

The ibogaine plateau, sometimes called the evaluative or reflective phase, can last many hours after the dreamlike visions fade. The body is heavy, weak and uncoordinated, with ongoing nausea, sensitivity to light and sound, and difficulty standing. The heart remains under strain, so cardiac monitoring matters throughout.

Mentally people often review their life, behavior and relationships with unusual detachment, and many describe intense insight, guilt, grief or resolve. The mind can stay racing and alert, with difficulty sleeping, even as the body is exhausted.

Opioid withdrawal is often dramatically reduced for some, which is why ibogaine is used in addiction treatment clinics abroad, but this does not remove risk. Medical supervision with ECG monitoring is strongly advised.

### Comedown (20-36 h)

The comedown, sometimes called residual stimulation, may last a day or more, with insomnia, fatigue, muscle weakness, unsteady gait and continued lack of appetite. Heart rhythm risks can persist, and deaths have occurred days after ingestion. Light and noise may still feel harsh, and many people need help moving around safely.

Mood is often reflective and fragile, with some feeling renewed and motivated and others anxious, low or confused. Many describe a heightened clarity mixed with exhaustion and an altered sense of time.

Recovery can take several days. Opioid tolerance may drop sharply, so resuming opioids at previous amounts carries a serious overdose risk.

Noribogaine persists for days, extending heart risk.

People with opioid tolerance can overdose if they resume opioids afterward, because tolerance drops.

### After-effects (1-3 days)

Exhaustion.

### Next day (days)

Fatigue for days.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
