# Ibogaine (iboga)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Indole alkaloid from the Tabernanthe iboga shrub; oneirogen.

**Brand and street names:** Iboga root bark; ibogaine hydrochloride.

## History and origins

Used in the Bwiti religion of Gabon and Cameroon. Isolated in 1901. Sold in France as Lambarène until 1966. Howard Lotsof reported in 1962 that it ended his heroin cravings; clinics in Mexico and elsewhere offer it for addiction. Texas funded ibogaine research in 2025.

## Medical use, then and now

No approved use; studied for opioid use disorder and traumatic brain injury.

## How it entered recreational use

Mostly sought for addiction treatment, not recreation.

## Legal status

US Schedule I; legal or unregulated in Mexico, New Zealand (prescription), and others.

## What the products look and feel like

Root bark or capsules of uncertain purity.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Ibogaine produces a long, dreamlike state with life-review visions, followed by many hours of wakeful reflection.

It is physically punishing: severe nausea, loss of coordination, and inability to walk.

It prolongs the QT interval of the heart and has caused sudden cardiac deaths, especially in people with heart problems, low potassium or magnesium, or opioids in their system.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-3 h)

Effects begin 1 to 3 hours after swallowing.

Ibogaine is absorbed slowly and converted by CYP2D6 into noribogaine.

Nausea, vomiting and severe loss of coordination occur early.

### Come-up (2-4 h)

Nausea and ataxia.

### Peak (4-8 h)

Visionary phase for 4 to 8 hours.

At peak, ibogaine blocks cardiac hERG potassium channels, lengthening the QT interval and risking torsades de pointes and sudden death.

Medical screening (ECG, electrolytes) and monitoring are essential; deaths have occurred at unregulated clinics.

### Plateau (8-20 h)

Reflective phase for many hours.

### Comedown (20-36 h)

Residual effects for a day or more.

Noribogaine persists for days, extending heart risk.

People with opioid tolerance can overdose if they resume opioids afterward, because tolerance drops.

### After-effects (1-3 days)

Exhaustion.

### Next day (days)

Fatigue for days.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

Ibogaine causes dangerous heart rhythms and has caused many deaths.
