# Nicotine

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonist.

**Brand and street names:** Cigarettes, cigars, vapes, nicotine pouches (such as Zyn), chewing tobacco; medicines: patches, gum, lozenges, inhaler, nasal spray.

## History and origins

Tobacco was used in the Americas for centuries and brought to Europe in the 1500s. Nicotine was isolated in 1828. The 1964 US Surgeon General's report linked smoking to lung cancer.

## Medical use, then and now

Nicotine replacement therapy helps people quit smoking.

## How it entered recreational use

Smoking remains the leading preventable cause of death; vaping and pouches are common among young people.

## Legal status

Legal for adults 21 and over in the US (Tobacco 21, 2019).

## What the pills feel like

Vapes are small devices; pouches are small white packets. Keep e-liquids and pouches away from children.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Nicotine gives a quick lift of alertness and calm at the same time, which is part of why it is so habit-forming.

Strong dependence develops; withdrawal brings irritability, anxiety and craving.

Liquid nicotine is highly poisonous to children if swallowed. Most harm from smoking comes from the smoke itself, not the nicotine.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (seconds)

Smoked or vaped nicotine hits within seconds: a light head rush, a tingling buzz and a slight drop of tension.

Mentally a brief, calming focus. For new users, dizziness and nausea dominate.

Heart rate rises.

Inhaled nicotine reaches the brain within about 10 to 20 seconds.

It activates nicotinic receptors, releasing dopamine, adrenaline and other chemicals, raising heart rate and blood pressure.

### Come-up (seconds)

A brief buzz.

### Peak (1-5 min)

The peak is brief: a relaxed alertness.

Emotionally calming relief of craving.

Liquid nicotine is highly toxic if swallowed, especially by children.

At high exposure, nicotine causes nausea, vomiting, sweating and, in poisoning, seizures and breathing muscle paralysis.

Children who swallow e-liquid or pouches are at greatest risk.

### Plateau (15-60 min)

The effect fades within minutes.

Craving returns.

Addiction develops quickly.

### Comedown (1-2 h)

The comedown is irritability and craving.

Mood edgy.

Withdrawal brings anxiety, irritability and hunger.

Half-life is about 2 hours, which drives frequent use.

Dependence is strong; withdrawal brings irritability, anxiety, poor concentration and craving.

### After-effects (hours)

Irritability.

### Next day (withdrawal)

Withdrawal without use.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Nicotine

Nicotine poisoning causes vomiting, sweating, fast heart rate and seizures; a child who swallows e-liquid needs emergency care.

Read the shared page: [Stimulant safety: overheating, heart risks, psychosis, fentanyl contamination](../safety/stimulant-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
