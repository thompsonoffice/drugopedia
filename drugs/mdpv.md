# MDPV

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Potent synthetic cathinone; dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor.

**Brand and street names:** Bath salts, monkey dust, cloud nine, ivory wave.

## History and origins

First patented by Boehringer Ingelheim in 1969; sold as 'bath salts' from about 2009 to 2011, causing a wave of US emergency visits.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Sold as bath salts or plant food; linked to violent agitation and psychosis.

## Legal status

US Schedule I (emergency 2011, permanent 2013); UN 1971 Schedule II (2015).

## What the products look and feel like

White or tan powder. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

MDPV blocks dopamine reuptake far more strongly than cocaine, causing intense stimulation, euphoria and compulsive redosing.

High levels cause terror, paranoia, hallucinations and violent agitation; people may sweat heavily and overheat.

Effects can last many hours and cause muscle breakdown, kidney failure and death.

Tolerance and compulsive redosing develop quickly with cathinones, and binges are common.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (5-15 min)

MDPV (methylenedioxypyrovalerone) was one of the main ingredients in 'bath salts' around 2010 to 2012. It is a powerful dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake blocker, much stronger than cocaine in lab studies. Snorted or swallowed, it comes on quickly with a surge of heat, a racing heart, widened pupils and tense, twitchy muscles. Skin may flush and feel hot to the touch. Some feel an immediate tightness in the chest and jaw.

Mentally the onset is an intense, focused euphoria and arousal, with racing thoughts and a strong compulsion to keep using.

Even small differences in amount make a big difference, and the urge to redose is extreme; overheating and agitation can come on fast.

MDPV is very lipophilic and strongly blocks dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake.

Heart rate, blood pressure and temperature rise quickly.

### Come-up (15-30 min)

A steep rise in energy.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At its peak the body is extremely stimulated: very fast heart, high blood pressure, sweating, clenched jaw, tremor and sometimes muscle spasms. Sleep and food become irrelevant. Some feel their heart thumping painfully and notice their jaw locking tightly.

Mentally there can be euphoria, hypersexuality and intense focus, but this often turns into agitation, paranoia, panic and hallucinations, especially with repeated dosing or sleep loss. Strangers can seem threatening and people may feel hunted.

Severe cases include 'excited delirium', with extreme agitation, very high body temperature, muscle breakdown, kidney failure and death. Emergency care is urgent.

At peak, MDPV is linked to excited delirium, hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis and death.

Animal studies show MDPV is more reinforcing than cocaine.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The plateau is long, several hours, and binges can last days. The body stays overheated, dehydrated and exhausted, yet unable to rest. Muscles may ache and cramp, and urination can become dark and scanty, a sign of muscle breakdown. Body heat may stay dangerously high for hours.

Mentally paranoia, suspicion, hearing voices and violent or self-harming behavior can emerge, sometimes persisting after the drug fades. Long binges can cause frightening delusions such as being followed or that bugs are under the skin.

Calm, cool surroundings and prompt medical help are critical. Never leave someone in this state alone. Cooling the body and getting emergency care can save a life.

### Comedown (4-8 h)

The comedown is severe: deep exhaustion, aches, broken sleep and sometimes lingering palpitations. Sleep may come in long, heavy stretches, followed by days of tiredness and low appetite. Some notice tremors and headaches for days. Appetite returns slowly, and weakness lingers.

Emotionally intense depression, anxiety and cravings, sometimes with suicidal thoughts or persistent psychosis. Memory gaps and confusion about what happened are common. Anger or tearfulness can come on suddenly.

Dependence develops rapidly; treatment and mental-health support are important. Relapse is common without ongoing support. Family support helps recovery. Specialist stimulant programs can help.

Half-life of several hours.

Psychosis can persist for days.

### After-effects (1-3 days)

Days of insomnia and paranoia.

### Next day (days)

Lasting depression and craving.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cathinones bring energy, euphoria and sociability. At higher doses, agitation, a racing heart, sweating, paranoia and a strong urge to redose dominate. At very high levels, psychosis, overheating, seizures, muscle breakdown and heart failure can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

MDPV poisoning causes overheating, agitation, heart failure and kidney failure.
