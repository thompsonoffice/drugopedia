# Diethylpropion (amfepramone)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Amphetamine-like appetite suppressant (anorectic), schedule IV.

**Brand and street names:** Tenuate, Tenuate Dospan (extended-release), Tepanil; outside the US: amfepramone.

## History and origins

Diethylpropion was approved in the US in 1959. Chemically, it is a cathinone, related to bupropion. It was withdrawn in parts of Europe after safety reviews, and the EU recommended withdrawal of amfepramone in 2022.

## Medical use, then and now

Short-term treatment of obesity alongside diet and exercise.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused for weight loss and energy; less common than phentermine.

## Legal status

US Schedule IV; UN 1971 Convention Schedule IV.

## Immediate-release vs extended-release

Tenuate immediate-release tablets act for about 4 hours; Tenuate Dospan is a controlled-release tablet designed to last most of the day and should be swallowed whole.

## What the pills feel like

Generic tablets and capsules vary by manufacturer. Stimulant drugs sold on the street, including counterfeit Adderall, are often contaminated with methamphetamine or fentanyl.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Diethylpropion is one of the milder anorectics: appetite suppression and mild alertness with less euphoria than phentermine.

Side effects include dry mouth, restlessness, insomnia and raised blood pressure; it can lower the seizure threshold.

Like other anorectics, it is linked rarely to pulmonary hypertension and heart valve problems, especially with long-term use.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 1 h)

Diethylpropion has a mild onset of energy and appetite loss.

Mentally alert.

Heart rate rises.

Diethylpropion is absorbed quickly and converted to active metabolites.

It releases noradrenaline, raising heart rate modestly.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Appetite falls and alertness rises.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At the peak, steady stimulation.

Emotionally upbeat.

Stimulants strain the heart: chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, severe headache, very high body temperature, seizures or extreme agitation and paranoia are emergencies. Street stimulants are increasingly contaminated with fentanyl, so naloxone and test strips matter even for people who do not use opioids.

At high levels, it causes agitation and can trigger seizures in susceptible people.

Long-term use is rarely linked to pulmonary hypertension.

### Plateau (4-12 h)

The plateau lasts hours.

Mood stable.

Sleep difficult.

### Comedown (6-14 h)

The comedown is tired.

Mood dips.

Dependence possible.

Effects fade over several hours.

Insomnia and restlessness may persist.

### After-effects (next night)

Insomnia if taken late.

### Next day

Possible next-day fatigue.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Diethylpropion (amfepramone)

Overdose causes agitation, high blood pressure, fast heart rate, hallucinations and seizures.
