# Dronabinol and nabilone (prescription THC)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Prescription synthetic THC (dronabinol) and a THC analog (nabilone).

**Brand and street names:** Marinol, Syndros (dronabinol); Cesamet (nabilone).

## History and origins

Dronabinol was approved in 1985 for chemotherapy nausea and in 1992 for AIDS-related appetite loss; nabilone was approved in 1985.

## Medical use, then and now

Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting; appetite loss in AIDS (dronabinol).

## How it entered recreational use

Little recreational use; effects are slow, like a strong edible.

## Legal status

Marinol and generic dronabinol capsules are Schedule III; Syndros and nabilone are Schedule II.

## What the products look and feel like

Marinol is a round soft gelatin capsule; Cesamet is a capsule; Syndros is a liquid.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Prescription THC feels like an edible: slow onset, sleepy, dreamy, with appetite stimulation.

Some patients feel anxious, dizzy or 'too high', especially older adults.

Its effects are more predictable than street cannabis because the content is known.

Tolerance to the high builds quickly with daily use and fades after a break of a few weeks; people returning after a break, or switching to concentrates or edibles, are often surprised by how strong the effect is.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Dronabinol (Marinol, Syndros) is a prescription form of THC, and nabilone (Cesamet) is a synthetic relative. They are prescribed for nausea from chemotherapy, appetite loss in HIV/AIDS and sometimes pain. Taken as capsules, they act slowly, over half an hour to a couple of hours, starting with drowsiness, a dry mouth and a mild heaviness.

Mentally the onset can be subtle, with relaxation and easing of nausea, or, in some people, a 'high' with euphoria, dizziness and a dreamy mind. Older adults and people new to THC may feel it strongly.

Because effects are delayed, taking extra early can stack into an uncomfortable experience. Doctors usually start low and increase slowly.

Dronabinol capsules are absorbed slowly, with only about 10 to 20 percent reaching the bloodstream after first-pass metabolism.

Older adults are more sensitive to dizziness, low blood pressure and confusion.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Gradual relaxation and appetite.

### Peak (2-4 h)

At their peak, the body feels relaxed, sleepy and sometimes dizzy, with a faster heart, dry mouth and low blood pressure on standing. Appetite often rises and nausea eases. Some feel unsteady when walking or standing up quickly, and eyes may be red. Nausea relief can be very welcome for chemotherapy patients.

Mentally there may be euphoria, calm, mild confusion or, in some, anxiety, paranoia or mood changes. Memory and concentration are impaired. Some patients find the high unpleasant or disorienting and prefer lower doses; others feel calmer and less distressed by their illness.

Falls in older people and driving impairment are key concerns. People with a history of psychosis or severe heart disease need special caution.

At peak, patients may feel 'high', anxious or sleepy, which is the main reason doses are adjusted.

Nabilone is longer-acting, with active metabolites lasting much longer than THC.

### Plateau (4-6 h)

The plateau lasts several hours. The body stays drowsy and relaxed, appetite continues and nausea stays improved. Sleepiness may deepen, and some people rest or nap. Dizziness on standing may continue, and the mouth stays dry. Eating often becomes easier.

Mentally thinking may be slow and foggy, and mood generally calm. Mild confusion or slowed thinking may continue, and some people notice changes in mood, such as feeling unusually cheerful or flat. Memory for this period may be hazy.

Misuse potential is lower than smoked cannabis because of the slow onset, but it exists. Patients should not drive or operate machinery until they know how the medicine affects them, and should avoid alcohol.

### Comedown (6-12 h)

As it fades, people may feel tired and groggy, with a dry mouth. Grogginess may linger into the next morning, especially if taken late in the day. Balance may be off for some hours, so older adults should take care with stairs.

Mentally there may be mild fogginess. Some feel slightly flat, unmotivated or forgetful; others feel rested. Any troubling mood changes, confusion or hallucinations should be reported to the prescriber.

Stopping after long, regular use can cause irritability, poor sleep and loss of appetite for a short time. Doctors may adjust timing, such as taking it at night, to reduce daytime effects. Stopping should be discussed with the care team.

Effects fade over several hours.

Labels warn against driving until patients know how they respond.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Drowsiness.

### Next day

Possible next-day fog.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cannabis brings relaxation, mild euphoria, giggliness, and heightened senses. At higher doses, time distortion, anxiety, paranoia, a racing heart, nausea, dizziness and, for some, hallucinations or panic appear. Very high doses, especially edibles, can cause hours of confusion, vomiting and frightening psychotic symptoms.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Dronabinol and nabilone (prescription THC)

Too much causes anxiety, dizziness, fast heartbeat and confusion; serious harm is rare in adults.
