# Propofol

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Intravenous general anesthetic acting at GABA-A receptors.

**Brand and street names:** Diprivan; nickname 'milk of amnesia'.

## History and origins

Developed by John Glen and colleagues at ICI, first tested clinically in 1977 and reformulated as an emulsion; FDA-approved in 1989. It became the most widely used IV anesthetic. Misuse occurs mainly among healthcare workers; it drew public attention after Michael Jackson's death in 2009.

## Medical use, then and now

Starting and maintaining anesthesia, ICU sedation, and procedural sedation, always with airway monitoring.

## How it entered recreational use

Rare outside healthcare; misuse by anesthesia staff has a high death rate.

## Legal status

Not federally scheduled in the US; prescription-only and restricted to clinical settings.

## What the products look and feel like

Propofol is a white, milky emulsion in glass vials, used only in hospitals and clinics.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Propofol causes very fast, smooth loss of consciousness, often with a pleasant, dreamy waking and sometimes euphoria or disinhibition.

It stops breathing easily and drops blood pressure, so it is only used where breathing can be supported.

Outside a monitored setting it is one of the most dangerous drugs to misuse.

Tolerance to dissociatives builds quickly, and ketamine tolerance leads people to use more, raising the risk of bladder and memory damage.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 s)

Given intravenously, unconsciousness occurs within about 30 to 60 seconds.

Propofol is extremely fat-soluble, crossing into the brain within one arm-to-brain circulation time (about 30 seconds).

It causes pain on injection and quickly lowers blood pressure by relaxing blood vessels.

### Come-up (about 1 min)

Breathing slows and blood pressure falls.

### Peak (1-5 min)

Deep sedation lasts minutes.

At the peak, propofol suppresses breathing and airway reflexes; apnea is common at induction, which is why airway equipment and trained staff are required.

Long infusions can cause propofol infusion syndrome (acid build-up, heart failure, muscle breakdown).

### Plateau (as long as infused)

Effects last only as long as it is given.

### Comedown (5-10 min)

People wake within minutes of stopping.

Rapid redistribution to fat ends its effect within minutes.

Healthcare worker misuse often ends in death because people use it alone without airway support.

### After-effects (under 1 h)

Mild drowsiness, sometimes euphoria.

### Next day (none)

Usually none.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses dissociatives bring numbness, lightness, mild euphoria and dreamy detachment. At higher doses, the body feels far away, movement becomes difficult, and vision and sound distort. At still higher levels, people lose contact with surroundings entirely, cannot move or respond, and are at risk of injury, vomiting and choking, with breathing danger when combined with depressants.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Propofol

Propofol stops breathing and causes dangerously low blood pressure; outside monitoring it is frequently fatal.

Read the shared page: [Dissociative safety: K-holes, injuries, bladder damage, mixing](../safety/dissociative-safety.md).
