# Opium, laudanum, and paregoric

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Dried latex of the opium poppy (Papaver somniferum), containing morphine, codeine, thebaine, papaverine, and noscapine. Laudanum is a tincture of opium in alcohol; paregoric is camphorated tincture of opium.

**Brand and street names:** Laudanum (Opium Tincture USP), Paregoric, Dover's Powder (historical). Street: 'O', 'black tar' is a different product (heroin).

## History and origins

Opium was used in Mesopotamia by 3400 BCE and in ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome. Paracelsus popularized laudanum in the 1500s, and Thomas Sydenham standardized it in 1676. In the 1800s, laudanum was sold freely and given to babies in 'soothing syrups'. Britain fought the Opium Wars (1839 to 1842 and 1856 to 1860) to force China to accept opium imports. Writers like Thomas De Quincey ('Confessions of an English Opium-Eater', 1821) and Samuel Taylor Coleridge described its effects.

## Medical use, then and now

Today, opium tincture and paregoric are still prescribed in the US for severe diarrhea.

## How it entered recreational use

Opium smoking was common in 19th-century China and in Chinese immigrant communities in the US, leading to the first US anti-drug law (San Francisco, 1875). Opium is still used traditionally and recreationally in Iran, Afghanistan, and India.

## Legal status

Opium tincture US Schedule II; paregoric Schedule III. Raw opium is illegal in most countries.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Opium's effect is described as gentler, dreamier, and more 'rounded' than pure morphine, because it contains several alkaloids. People describe a pleasant, reverie-like state, vivid daydreams, and a sense of peaceful detachment. De Quincey called it 'the true hero of the tale' for its calm.

Smoked opium is described as light, relaxing, and social; swallowed opium is heavier and longer, with more nausea.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Smoked: within seconds to minutes; swallowed: 30 to 60 minutes. A soft warmth and loosening.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Dreamy relaxation builds, with itch and some nausea.

### Peak (1-2 h)

A peaceful, contemplative euphoria, rich daydream imagery.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

Long reverie and light nodding.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

Gradual fade over several hours.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Constipation, dry mouth, and lethargy.

### Next day

After regular use, typical opioid withdrawal.

## Overdose note for Opium, laudanum, and paregoric

Strength varies widely between batches. Laudanum contains much more morphine than paregoric, and confusing the two has caused deaths.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md).
