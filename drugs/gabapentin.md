# Gabapentin (Neurontin)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## At a glance

Gabapentinoid (alpha-2-delta ligand).

**Brand and street names:** Neurontin, Gralise (once-daily), Horizant (gabapentin enacarbil, extended-release prodrug). Street: 'gabbies', 'johnnies'.

## History and origins

Developed by Parke-Davis and approved in 1993 for epilepsy. Off-label marketing led to a $430 million settlement in 2004. It became one of the most prescribed US drugs.

## Medical use, then and now

Seizures, postherpetic neuralgia, restless legs (Horizant); widely used off-label for nerve pain, anxiety, alcohol use disorder and sleep.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused for relaxation and to boost opioids or ease withdrawal, especially among people who use opioids.

## Legal status

Not federally controlled in the US, but Schedule V in several states (Kentucky, Michigan, Tennessee, Virginia, and others). UK Class C (2019).

## Immediate-release vs extended-release

Immediate-release gabapentin is taken several times daily. Gralise is a once-daily gastric-retentive tablet taken with an evening meal, and Horizant is an extended-release prodrug; both give steadier levels and are not interchangeable with regular gabapentin.

## What the pills feel like

Per DailyMed, Neurontin capsules are hard capsules (in different colors by strength) and higher-strength tablets are white, elliptical, film-coated and scored.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Gabapentin's character is a mild, drunk-like calm with loosened social anxiety and dizziness. Some people feel little; others feel a pleasant, sociable warmth.

Because absorption is limited, the effect does not rise in a straight line with the amount.

Combined with opioids, it greatly raises the risk of fatal breathing depression.

Tolerance to the pleasant and sleep effects builds with regular use, but tolerance to slowed breathing builds more slowly, so the danger stays. Daily use can lead to dependence, and stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-3 h)

Gabapentin (Neurontin) has a slow, uneven onset because the gut absorbs it in a limited, saturable way. As it builds, the body feels heavy and slightly numb, as if wrapped in padding, and a light, wobbly dizziness appears, especially on standing.

Mentally gabapentin brings a muted calm. Anxiety and nerve pain are dulled, and some people feel a mild, mellow, alcohol-like ease; for many others it simply feels sleepy and foggy.

Blurry or doubled vision, mild unsteadiness and a 'drunk legs' feeling are common early signs.

Medically, the onset reflects slow and variable absorption. Gabapentin is absorbed by a gut transporter that saturates and is cleared unchanged by the kidneys with a half-life of about 5 to 7 hours. Gabapentin is absorbed by a transporter in the gut that saturates, so more is not absorbed in proportion; pregabalin is absorbed more fully and quickly.

Early effects include calm, mild dizziness, and relaxation. Because gabapentinoids are cleared by the kidneys, people with kidney disease or older adults are affected more strongly and for longer.

### Come-up (2-3 h)

A gradual calm and lightness.

### Peak (2-4 h)

At the peak the body feels loose and slow, with unsteady walking and a warm, slightly tingly heaviness. Some describe a gentle euphoria and sociability; others feel spacey and clumsy.

Emotionally the peak can be relaxed and contented, sometimes chatty, but memory and attention are noticeably dulled.

Alone, gabapentin rarely stops breathing, but with opioids it sharply increases the risk of fatal overdose, a combination increasingly seen in deaths. Kidney problems raise blood levels too.

Medically, the peak brings the strongest dizziness, unsteadiness, and sedation. Some people feel calm sociability or mild euphoria; others feel only clumsy and sleepy.

In 2019 the FDA warned that gabapentinoids can cause serious breathing problems when combined with opioids or other depressants, or in people with lung disease or older age. Gabapentinoids appear in many opioid overdose deaths.

### Plateau (3-6 h)

The plateau is a long, steady haze lasting several hours.

Thinking is slow and forgetful, and words may be hard to find.

Falls are common, especially in older adults.

### Comedown (5-8 h total)

The comedown is tiredness and mild fog, sometimes with headache.

Mood may dip, with irritability or low mood; rarely, suicidal thoughts are reported.

Stopping after regular use can cause anxiety, insomnia, sweating, pain and, rarely, seizures.

As levels fall, dizziness and sedation fade over several hours. Sleep is often deep.

Daily use can cause dependence; stopping suddenly can cause anxiety, insomnia, sweating, pain, agitation, and occasionally seizures, so medical tapering is advised.

### After-effects (8-24 h)

Tiredness.

### Next day (little)

Little next-day effect.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, gabapentinoids cause calm, mild dizziness, and relaxation. At higher doses, unsteadiness, slurred speech, double vision, and heavy sedation increase, and some people feel a drunk-like euphoria. With opioids or other depressants, breathing can slow dangerously.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, age, liver and kidney function, genetics, and especially other depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines) change the effect. Older adults and people with sleep apnea or lung disease are at higher risk. Stronger effects do not stop at more pleasure: they shade into blackouts, falls, and slowed breathing.

## Overdose note for Gabapentin (Neurontin)

Overdose alone usually causes drowsiness and unsteadiness, but with opioids it causes deadly breathing depression; gabapentin appears in many US overdose deaths.
