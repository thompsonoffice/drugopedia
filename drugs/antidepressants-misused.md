# Antidepressants as misused (bupropion, trazodone, amitriptyline)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Antidepressants occasionally misused.

**Brand and street names:** Bupropion (Wellbutrin, Zyban), trazodone (Desyrel), amitriptyline (Elavil).

## History and origins

Bupropion was approved in 1985 and reintroduced in 1989 after seizure concerns. Bupropion misuse by snorting or injecting has been reported in prisons. Tricyclics like amitriptyline were among the first antidepressants (1960s).

## Medical use, then and now

Depression, smoking cessation (bupropion), insomnia off-label (trazodone).

## How it entered recreational use

Bupropion is misused for stimulation; tricyclics are rarely misused but are dangerous in overdose.

## Legal status

Prescription-only.

## Immediate-release vs extended-release

Bupropion comes in IR, SR and XL forms. Extended-release tablets must be swallowed whole; damaging them raises seizure risk.

## What the products look and feel like

Tablets vary by manufacturer.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Bupropion can produce mild stimulation; misuse raises seizure risk sharply.

Trazodone causes sedation and dizziness.

Tricyclic overdose is one of the most dangerous medicine overdoses: seizures and fatal heart rhythms.

Tolerance and dependence vary by substance.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Misused antidepressants (bupropion, trazodone, amitriptyline and others) vary. Bupropion brings a jittery stimulant-like edge; trazodone and amitriptyline bring heavy sedation and dry mouth.

Mentally there is usually little pleasure.

Side effects dominate.

Bupropion blocks dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake; trazodone blocks serotonin 5-HT2A and H1; amitriptyline blocks reuptake and many receptors.

Body effects vary: stimulation or sedation.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Stimulation (bupropion) or sedation (trazodone, amitriptyline).

### Peak (2-4 h)

At the peak, stimulation (bupropion) or deep sedation (trazodone, tricyclics).

Emotionally unpleasant for many.

Bupropion can cause seizures, and tricyclic overdose causes deadly heart rhythms.

At high levels, bupropion lowers the seizure threshold; tricyclics block cardiac sodium channels.

Serotonin syndrome is possible when combined with other serotonergic drugs.

### Plateau (4-8 h)

The plateau varies.

Thinking foggy or wired.

Medical help may be needed.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

The comedown is draining.

Mood low.

Combinations can cause serotonin syndrome.

Half-lives range widely.

Lingering effects vary.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Lingering effects.

### Next day

Next-day effects.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses effects are mild; at higher doses side effects and toxicity dominate, as described for each substance.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Antidepressants as misused (bupropion, trazodone, amitriptyline)

Bupropion overdose causes seizures; tricyclic overdose causes seizures, coma and fatal arrhythmias.

Read the shared page: [Other psychoactives safety](../safety/other-psychoactives-safety.md).
