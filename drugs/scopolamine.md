# Scopolamine (hyoscine)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Anticholinergic tropane alkaloid.

**Brand and street names:** Transderm Scop patch; hyoscine butylbromide (Buscopan, does not enter the brain); 'burundanga' (Colombia).

## History and origins

Isolated in the 1880s. Used as 'twilight sleep' in childbirth in the early 1900s and briefly as a 'truth serum'. In Colombia, reports describe 'burundanga' used to incapacitate crime victims, though many reports are disputed.

## Medical use, then and now

Motion sickness and postoperative nausea (patch); drying secretions at end of life.

## How it entered recreational use

Rarely used recreationally because effects are unpleasant.

## Legal status

Prescription-only.

## What the products look and feel like

Transderm Scop is a small round patch worn behind the ear.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Scopolamine causes drowsiness, amnesia and, at higher levels, delirium with hallucinations.

Patches can cause confusion in older adults and blurred vision if a finger touches the eye after handling.

It leaves the person compliant and confused, which is why it has been linked to crimes.

Tolerance develops to some effects, but delirium risk remains.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the transdermal (skin patches) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (4-6 h)

Scopolamine, used medically for motion sickness, causes dry mouth and drowsiness. Misused, it brings a confused, sedated delirium.

Mentally a fog, with loss of will and memory.

It has been used in crimes ('burundanga') to make victims compliant.

Scopolamine from patches is absorbed slowly through the skin behind the ear.

Dry mouth, blurred vision and drowsiness are common.

### Come-up (6-8 h)

Dry mouth and drowsiness.

### Peak (8-24 h)

At the peak, delirium and amnesia.

Emotionally confused.

Deliriants cause true delirium: people lose track of what is real, cannot recognize that they are intoxicated, and may wander into traffic or water. Very fast heart rate, high temperature, seizures and dangerous heart rhythms can occur; it is a medical emergency.

At high levels, delirium, amnesia and hallucinations.

Older adults are especially vulnerable to confusion.

### Plateau (up to 72 h)

The plateau is long.

Memory absent.

Hospital care needed.

### Comedown (after removal)

The comedown is foggy.

Mood anxious.

Memory of events often never returns.

Effects persist after patch removal.

Withdrawal after long patch use can cause nausea and dizziness.

### After-effects (1-2 days)

Blurred vision.

### Next day (days)

Memory gaps.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses deliriants cause drowsiness, dry mouth and a fast heartbeat. At higher doses, confusion, blurred vision and hallucinations start. At very high levels, full delirium with realistic hallucinations, overheating, seizures and dangerous heart rhythms occur; no one finds this pleasant.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Scopolamine (hyoscine)

Overdose causes delirium, fast heart rate, high temperature and seizures.
