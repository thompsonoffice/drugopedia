# Scopolamine (hyoscine)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Anticholinergic tropane alkaloid.

**Brand and street names:** Transderm Scop patch; hyoscine butylbromide (Buscopan, does not enter the brain); 'burundanga' (Colombia).

## History and origins

Isolated in the 1880s. Used as 'twilight sleep' in childbirth in the early 1900s and briefly as a 'truth serum'. In Colombia, reports describe 'burundanga' used to incapacitate crime victims, though many reports are disputed.

## Medical use, then and now

Motion sickness and postoperative nausea (patch); drying secretions at end of life.

## How it entered recreational use

Rarely used recreationally because effects are unpleasant.

## Legal status

Prescription-only.

## What the products look and feel like

Transderm Scop is a small round patch worn behind the ear.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Scopolamine causes drowsiness, amnesia and, at higher levels, delirium with hallucinations.

Patches can cause confusion in older adults and blurred vision if a finger touches the eye after handling.

It leaves the person compliant and confused, which is why it has been linked to crimes.

Tolerance develops to some effects, but delirium risk remains.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the transdermal (skin patches) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (4-6 h)

Scopolamine (hyoscine) is an anticholinergic medicine used in patches for motion sickness and nausea; it is also found in datura and related plants. At medical doses, people may notice a dry mouth, mild drowsiness and slightly blurred vision. In large amounts, it builds into a deliriant state with flushed skin, racing heart and wide pupils.

Mentally medical use usually brings only mild sleepiness. In poisoning, confusion, restlessness and loss of memory set in, and the person may seem strangely compliant or dreamy.

Scopolamine ('burundanga') has been reported in crimes in which victims were drugged and robbed; it causes confusion and memory loss.

Scopolamine from patches is absorbed slowly through the skin behind the ear.

Dry mouth, blurred vision and drowsiness are common.

### Come-up (6-8 h)

Dry mouth and drowsiness.

### Peak (8-24 h)

At the peak of poisoning the body is hot, dry and flushed with a very fast heart, wide pupils, urinary retention and unsteady movements. Speech may be slurred and walking very unsteady; the person may seem to follow instructions without understanding. Muscles may twitch and breathing quicken, and some people collapse.

Mentally there can be delirium, hallucinations, agitation and complete loss of memory for the period. People may not recognize friends or know where they are, and fear and agitation can come in waves.

Emergency care is needed; physostigmine can sometimes reverse severe effects under monitoring. Monitoring of heart rhythm and temperature is essential.

At high levels, delirium, amnesia and hallucinations.

Older adults are especially vulnerable to confusion.

### Plateau (up to 72 h)

Effects last many hours. The body stays dry, flushed and racing. Pupils stay wide and vision blurred, the mouth and throat parched, and urination difficult or painful. The skin may feel hot to touch, and twitching or jerking movements can continue. Breathing may be fast.

Mentally delirium and confusion persist. Confusion and hallucinations persist, and the person may wander, talk to unseen people or repeat actions. They usually cannot remember what is happening.

Older adults are especially vulnerable to confusion even at medical doses. Someone should stay with them and call emergency services; victims of drugging should be helped to seek medical and police support.

### Comedown (after removal)

The comedown brings tiredness, dry mouth and blurred vision. Vision may stay blurred and light painful for a day or two, and the mouth dry and stomach sluggish. Exhaustion can be heavy. Muscles may ache, and sleep may be broken by vivid dreams.

Mentally confusion and memory gaps may last a day or more. Some feel frightened, ashamed or confused about missing time, especially if they were drugged without knowing. Memory gaps are often permanent.

Stopping a patch after long use can cause nausea, dizziness and headache. Medical follow-up and support services can help people who were drugged. Reporting a suspected drugging quickly helps testing, since scopolamine clears the body fast.

Effects persist after patch removal.

Withdrawal after long patch use can cause nausea and dizziness.

### After-effects (1-2 days)

Blurred vision.

### Next day (days)

Memory gaps.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses deliriants cause drowsiness, dry mouth and a fast heartbeat. At higher doses, confusion, blurred vision and hallucinations start. At very high levels, full delirium with realistic hallucinations, overheating, seizures and dangerous heart rhythms occur; no one finds this pleasant.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Scopolamine (hyoscine)

Overdose causes delirium, fast heart rate, high temperature and seizures.

Read the shared page: [Deliriant safety](../safety/deliriant-safety.md).
