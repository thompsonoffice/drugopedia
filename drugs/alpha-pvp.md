# Alpha-PVP (flakka)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic cathinone closely related to MDPV.

**Brand and street names:** Flakka, gravel.

## History and origins

Appeared around 2012 and caused a public health crisis in South Florida in 2015.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Cheap, extremely potent, and linked to bizarre, violent behavior.

## Legal status

US Schedule I (2014); UN 1971 Schedule II (2015).

## What the products look and feel like

White or pink crystals ('gravel'). Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Alpha-PVP causes a fast, intense high with racing thoughts and a powerful urge to redose.

Excited delirium is common at higher levels: paranoia, agitation, very high temperature and extreme strength reported by paramedics.

Deaths from overheating, heart failure and kidney failure have been reported.

Tolerance and compulsive redosing develop quickly with cathinones, and binges are common.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (seconds-minutes)

Alpha-PVP ('flakka', 'gravel') is a close relative of MDPV that caused waves of emergencies in Florida around 2015. Smoked or vaped it hits within seconds; snorted or swallowed, within minutes. The body surges with heat and energy, the heart races, pupils widen and muscles tense and twitch. The jaw clenches and the chest tightens almost at once, and sweat breaks out quickly.

Mentally the onset is an intense, rushing euphoria and alertness, with racing thoughts and a powerful drive to redose almost immediately. Some feel an almost electrical buzzing through the body.

The high is short and the craving is fierce, which leads to rapid binges. Vaping or smoking makes the cycle especially fast.

Alpha-PVP is a potent dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor.

Heart rate and temperature rise.

### Come-up (minutes)

Rapid stimulation.

### Peak (15-60 min)

At its peak the body is extremely wired: very fast pulse, high blood pressure, sweating, overheating and jerky movements. Muscles can spasm and the person may be unable to sit still, pacing or flailing.

Mentally excitement can rapidly tip into paranoia, terror, hallucinations and violent or bizarre behavior; people may strip because they feel so hot or run without purpose. Some people believe they are being chased, filmed or attacked. Hallucinations may be vivid and frightening.

Excited delirium, extreme body temperature, muscle breakdown and kidney failure can be fatal. Emergency care is urgent. Restraint can worsen overheating; calm, cooling and medical teams are safer.

At peak, excited delirium, paranoia and hyperthermia occur.

Deaths followed overheating and cardiac arrest.

### Plateau (1-3 h)

The plateau is short per dose but binges can run days. The body becomes dehydrated, depleted and overheated. The heart races continuously, the mouth is parched and muscles ache and cramp; urine may darken from muscle breakdown. Some lose track of time entirely and skip water and food for a day or more.

Mentally paranoia, picking at skin, hearing voices and confusion grow as sleep is lost. Repetitive behaviors such as searching floors or taking things apart are common, as is suspicion of friends.

Cooling, calm surroundings and medical help are critical. People in this state need someone sober watching over them. Emergency teams can sedate and cool someone safely.

### Comedown (3-6 h)

The comedown brings collapse-level exhaustion, aches and insomnia. Some sleep for very long stretches afterward and wake weak, sore and starving. Muscles may stay weak and sore for days, and the heart may race at rest.

Emotionally deep depression, anxiety and intense craving, sometimes with lingering psychosis. Thoughts may be confused and memories fragmented, and shame about the binge is common. Depression can be crushing in the first days. Some feel numb and hopeless.

Dependence develops very quickly, and treatment and support are essential. Ongoing counseling and support reduce relapse. Medical checkups for kidney and heart health are wise. Peer support groups also help.

Effects last a few hours.

Psychosis may last longer.

### After-effects (1-2 days)

Insomnia.

### Next day (days)

Days of low mood.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cathinones bring energy, euphoria and sociability. At higher doses, agitation, a racing heart, sweating, paranoia and a strong urge to redose dominate. At very high levels, psychosis, overheating, seizures, muscle breakdown and heart failure can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.
