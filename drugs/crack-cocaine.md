# Crack cocaine

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Smokable base form of cocaine.

**Brand and street names:** Crack, rock, hard, base.

## History and origins

Crack spread in US cities in the mid-1980s; the 1986 Anti-Drug Abuse Act set far harsher penalties than for powder, a disparity reduced by the Fair Sentencing Act of 2010.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Cheap and intensely reinforcing; associated with the 1980s crack epidemic.

## Legal status

Same as cocaine (US Schedule II).

## What the pills feel like

Crack looks like off-white rocks or chunks. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Crack produces an immediate, intense rush within seconds that fades within minutes, driving rapid, repeated use.

Craving is intense, and binges can last until the drug or money runs out.

Smoking causes lung injury ('crack lung'), burns and cracked lips; shared pipes can spread hepatitis C.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (seconds)

Smoked crack cocaine hits within seconds of inhaling. A rush of heat and pressure surges up from the chest into the head, the heart pounds hard and fast, the ears may ring or buzz, and the whole body feels electrified and tingling, like a jolt running through every nerve. Many describe the first seconds as the most intense pleasure they have ever felt, and some sweat, flush or feel briefly faint.

Mentally the rush is an explosion of euphoria, confidence and power. Every worry vanishes, the mind feels supercharged, clear and invincible, and the person may feel an overwhelming sense of wellbeing.

Pupils widen sharply, the mouth and throat feel numb and dry, the jaw clenches and breathing quickens. The chemical smell and taste linger.

Smoked cocaine reaches the brain in seconds, faster than snorting, producing a sharp dopamine spike.

Blood pressure and heart rate surge immediately.

### Come-up (seconds)

An intense rush.

### Peak (1-5 min)

The peak is short and intense: a buzzing, hyper-alert body, clenched jaw, racing heart, sweating and an inability to sit still. Senses feel sharpened, especially hearing.

Emotionally euphoric, talkative and grandiose at first, but within minutes an edge of anxiety, paranoia and urgent craving creeps in. People may listen intently for sounds, peek out windows, search the floor for dropped crumbs ('ghosting' or 'tweaking'), and feel watched.

Stimulants strain the heart: chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, severe headache, very high body temperature, seizures or extreme agitation and paranoia are emergencies. Street stimulants are increasingly contaminated with fentanyl, so naloxone and test strips matter even for people who do not use opioids.

At peak, the same heart and stroke risks as cocaine apply, plus lung injury (crack lung) from hot smoke.

Binge use can cause seizures and severe agitation.

### Plateau (5-15 min)

There is almost no plateau; the high drops off sharply within minutes, which is why people use repeatedly in binges that can last hours or days.

Each hit brings a smaller rush and more anxiety, paranoia and craving, while the crash pulls harder between hits.

Binges cause sleeplessness, dehydration, chest pain, burned lips and fingers, and sometimes hallucinations such as bugs crawling under the skin.

### Comedown (15-30 min)

The comedown is a hard crash: deep exhaustion, a heavy, aching body, hunger and an overwhelming need for sleep, often for many hours.

Emotionally it brings depression, irritability, anxiety, shame and intense craving, sometimes with suicidal thoughts.

Crack carries a very high addiction risk. Lung damage ('crack lung'), heart attacks, strokes and seizures are documented, and street supplies may contain fentanyl.

The rapid fall in dopamine drives intense craving within minutes.

Chronic use damages lungs, causes burns to lips and fingers, and spreads hepatitis C through shared pipes.

### After-effects (hours)

Exhaustion after binges.

### Next day (days)

Days of low mood and craving.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Crack cocaine

Overdose risks are the same as cocaine, made more likely by binging; fentanyl contamination is common.

Read the shared page: [Stimulant safety: overheating, heart risks, psychosis, fentanyl contamination](../safety/stimulant-safety.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
