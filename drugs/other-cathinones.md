# Other synthetic cathinones (N-ethylpentylone, 4-MEC, pentedrone, hexen, alpha-PHP and others)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

A large family of designer stimulants.

**Brand and street names:** N-ethylpentylone (ephylone), 4-MEC, pentedrone, N-ethylhexedrone (hexen), alpha-PHP.

## History and origins

New cathinones have appeared every year since about 2010 to replace banned ones; European monitoring has identified more than 150.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Sold as bath salts, 'molly', or by chemical name.

## Legal status

Many are Schedule I in the US and UN-controlled; others fall under analog laws.

## What the products look and feel like

Powders and crystals of varying color. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Each cathinone feels somewhat different, from MDMA-like to cocaine-like, but all cause stimulation, a fast heart rate and compulsive redosing.

N-ethylpentylone caused clusters of deaths and hospitalizations in the US and New Zealand around 2017 and 2018.

Because products are mislabeled, users rarely know which one they have.

Tolerance and compulsive redosing develop quickly with cathinones, and binges are common.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (5-20 min)

'Other cathinones' covers dozens of related stimulants sold as bath salts, plant food, research chemicals or fake MDMA, such as pentedrone, 4-MEC, N-ethylpentylone, 4-CMC and hexen. Their onsets range from a mild, mephedrone-like warmth to a sharp, MDPV-like rush, with a racing heart, wide pupils and jaw tension. Some feel cold hands and nausea early. Some feel jittery, flushed or slightly dizzy from the start.

Mentally most bring stimulation and some euphoria, with sociability or focus; some lean empathic, others purely speedy and anxious.

Because the specific chemical is usually unknown, people cannot predict strength or duration, and compulsive redosing is common across the group.

These compounds vary in potency and mechanism.

Heart rate and blood pressure rise.

### Come-up (15-30 min)

Stimulation rises.

### Peak (30-90 min)

At their peak the body is hot and wired, with sweating, a pounding heart, raised blood pressure and restlessness. Jaw clenching, tremor and widened pupils are typical, and some people feel short of breath or flushed. Some feel their heart thumping hard.

Mentally euphoria often mixes with nervousness; at higher amounts paranoia, agitation and hallucinations appear. Some feel affectionate and chatty, others tense and suspicious; the same powder can feel different from batch to batch.

Overheating, seizures, heart rhythm problems and excited delirium have all been reported. Mixing with alcohol, other stimulants or serotonin drugs raises danger considerably.

At peak, overheating, agitation and seizures.

Deaths have followed N-ethylpentylone and others.

### Plateau (1-3 h)

The plateau varies from an hour to many hours depending on the compound. The body stays tense and dehydrated. Fingers may stay cold, the jaw sore and the nose irritated if snorted; urination can be difficult and heart rate remains raised for hours. Some feel chest tightness and headache.

Mentally restlessness and craving dominate as the pleasant effects fade. Many people take more again and again even when it no longer feels good, and paranoia can develop after a sleepless night. Conversations may become repetitive. Some feel trapped and frightened.

Sleep is often impossible. Long binges cause dehydration, overheating and exhaustion. Cooling down and resting matter.

### Comedown (3-6 h)

The comedown brings exhaustion, aches and insomnia. Muscles ache, the jaw is sore, headaches and palpitations are common, and appetite is poor for a day or more. Sleep may be broken and unrefreshing. Some feel shaky and cold.

Emotionally low mood, anxiety and craving. Depression, irritability and anxiety can last several days, and some people feel shame or regret after a long session. Craving may return in waves. Mood dips can be steep.

Drug-checking services can identify unknown cathinones before use. Help lines and stimulant-use services can support people who want to cut down or stop. Not using alone and carrying naloxone in case of opioid contamination are wise.

Durations vary.

Psychosis may persist.

### After-effects (1-2 days)

Insomnia and paranoia.

### Next day (days)

Days of low mood.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cathinones bring energy, euphoria and sociability. At higher doses, agitation, a racing heart, sweating, paranoia and a strong urge to redose dominate. At very high levels, psychosis, overheating, seizures, muscle breakdown and heart failure can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.
