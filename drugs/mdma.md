# MDMA (ecstasy, molly)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Ring-substituted amphetamine; serotonin, dopamine and noradrenaline releaser.

**Brand and street names:** Ecstasy, E, X, molly, mandy, XTC, beans.

## History and origins

Merck patented MDMA in 1912 as an intermediate chemical. Alexander Shulgin resynthesized it in 1976 and introduced it to therapist Leo Zeff, who shared it with hundreds of therapists. Club use spread in the 1980s (rave culture); the DEA emergency-scheduled it in 1985. MAPS-sponsored trials for PTSD led to an FDA application that the FDA declined in 2024, requesting another study.

## Medical use, then and now

Investigated for MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD; Australia allowed psychiatrist prescribing in 2023.

## How it entered recreational use

Popular at clubs, raves and festivals; many 'molly' samples contain cathinones or other drugs.

## Legal status

US Schedule I; UN 1971 Schedule I.

## What the products look and feel like

Ecstasy pills come in many colors and logos, which say nothing about content; molly is usually off-white crystals or capsules. High-strength pills are common in Europe. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

MDMA's character is emotional openness and warmth: deep empathy, a sense of connection, talkativeness, and pleasure in touch and music.

Body effects include jaw clenching, teeth grinding, sweating, eye wiggles and raised temperature.

The comedown and the days after ('Tuesday blues') bring low mood as serotonin is depleted. Deaths result mainly from overheating, low blood sodium and drug interactions.

Tolerance to the warmth fades with frequent use while side effects remain.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

MDMA begins with a rush: tingling, warm waves through the body, a fluttering stomach and sometimes nausea. The jaw starts to clench, and the pupils widen.

Mentally people feel a growing euphoria and openness, often with excitement or nervousness.

Heart rate and body temperature rise, and people may feel restless.

MDMA is absorbed from the gut and reverses the serotonin transporter, flooding synapses with serotonin; it also releases dopamine and noradrenaline.

Heart rate, blood pressure and body temperature begin to rise; pupils dilate.

### Come-up (1-1.5 h)

A wave of warmth and energy builds.

### Peak (1.5-2.5 h)

At the peak, the body feels warm, energized and very sensitive to touch, music and movement. Jaw clenching, sweating and fast heartbeat are common.

The emotional tone is deep empathy, affection and joy, with a strong desire to connect and talk.

Risks include overheating, low blood sodium from drinking too much water, and serotonin syndrome with other drugs. Pills sold as MDMA may contain other substances.

At peak, MDMA triggers release of oxytocin and vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone); vasopressin makes the kidneys hold water, so drinking large amounts of water can dangerously dilute blood sodium, causing brain swelling.

Overheating is worsened by hot crowded venues and dancing; serotonin syndrome is more likely with antidepressants, tramadol, DXM, MAOIs and other serotonergic drugs.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The plateau holds warmth, energy and heightened touch sensitivity: hugging, stroking or dancing feels unusually pleasant. The jaw stays tight and people may grind their teeth or chew. Sweating continues, and the body is hot, especially in crowded venues. The eyes may flicker slightly (nystagmus) and pupils remain wide.

Mentally people stay open, talkative and affectionate. Conversations can feel deep and honest, and people may share things they normally keep private. The intense rush of the peak smooths into a warm, contented glow, though some feel a slight restlessness or wish for more.

Overheating, dehydration and, from drinking very large amounts of water, low blood sodium remain the main dangers. Combining with other stimulants or serotonergic drugs adds risk.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

As MDMA wears off, the body feels tired, achy and heavy; the jaw and teeth may be sore, and some feel chilled or sweaty. Sleep can be difficult despite exhaustion.

Emotionally the warmth drains away, often leaving a flat, empty or tearful mood. The next few days can bring the 'Tuesday blues' of low mood, irritability and poor concentration, linked to depleted serotonin.

Repeated or heavy use worsens these effects and is linked to lasting mood and memory problems. Severe headache, confusion or vomiting after heavy water drinking is an emergency sign of low blood sodium.

MDMA's half-life is about 7 to 9 hours; because it inhibits CYP2D6, redosing raises levels more than expected.

Serotonin depletion explains low mood for days afterward; heavy repeated use is linked to memory problems.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Low mood and jaw ache.

### Next day (2-4 days)

Low mood for days in some people.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses empathogens bring warmth, openness and mild stimulation. At higher doses, stimulation, jaw clenching, sweating and overheating rise while the pleasant effects do not increase much. At very high levels, serotonin toxicity, overheating, seizures and collapse can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for MDMA (ecstasy, molly)

MDMA deaths are mostly from overheating, low blood sodium (from drinking too much water plus MDMA's hormone effects), serotonin syndrome and heart problems.

Read the shared page: [Empathogen (MDMA) safety](../safety/empathogen-safety.md).
