# Tianeptine ('gas station heroin')

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Atypical antidepressant (tricyclic structure) that is also a full mu-opioid agonist, discovered in 2014.

**Brand and street names:** Prescription: Stablon, Coaxil, Tatinol (Europe, Asia, Latin America). US products: Zaza, Tianaa, Pegasus, Neptune's Fix, sold in gas stations and smoke shops as 'supplements'.

## History and origins

Developed by the French company Servier in the 1960s and marketed as an antidepressant in 1989. In 2014, researchers at Columbia showed it activates mu-opioid receptors (Gassaway et al.). It was never approved in the US, but from the 2010s it was sold in convenience stores. The FDA warned against it in 2018, 2022, and again in 2025 after clusters of seizures, comas, and deaths, some linked to Neptune's Fix products that also contained synthetic cannabinoids. Several US states have banned it.

## Medical use, then and now

Prescribed for depression and anxiety in some countries. Not approved in the US.

## How it entered recreational use

Sold as a 'nootropic' or mood supplement, it became known as 'gas station heroin' because people develop opioid-like dependence and sometimes use it many times a day.

## Legal status

Not federally controlled in the US, but banned in a growing number of states (including Michigan, Alabama, Florida, Ohio, and others). Prescription-only elsewhere; banned in the UK under the Psychoactive Substances Act.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Tianeptine's effect is described as a short, clean, mildly stimulating opioid-like euphoria: mood lift, motivation, and calm, with a light body warmth. At higher amounts it becomes more clearly opioid-like, with itch, nausea, and sedation.

Because it lasts only 2 to 3 hours, many people take it repeatedly through the day. Users describe the withdrawal as disproportionately harsh compared with how mild the high felt.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Swallowed, it starts in about 30 to 60 minutes with a subtle mood lift.

### Come-up (15-30 min)

The come-up is quick, 15 to 30 minutes: calm, a bit of energy, and a warm glow.

### Peak (60-90 min)

The peak, about 60 to 90 minutes in, is a mild euphoria with motivation and lowered anxiety; at higher amounts, opioid warmth, itch, and nodding.

### Plateau (under 1 h)

Plateau is short.

### Comedown (2-3 h)

Comedown begins around 2 hours: a fairly abrupt drop in mood, sometimes with irritability.

### After-effects (3-6 h)

After-effects can include nausea, headache, and restlessness.

### Next day

With daily repeated use, withdrawal sets in quickly: severe anxiety, sweating, diarrhea, agitation, and insomnia, sometimes lasting weeks.

## How the route changes the experience

- [Oral (swallowed)](../routes/oral/tianeptine.md): Tablets (prescription), capsules, powders, and liquid shots sold in stores.

Not covered: Injection of tianeptine powder has been reported and caused serious harm, but is not covered.

## Overdose note for Tianeptine ('gas station heroin')

Seizures, comas, and dangerous heart effects have been reported, especially from US store products, some of which were contaminated. Naloxone reverses its opioid effects. Overdose is much more likely when combined with other sedatives.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). Also see [naloxone (Narcan)](../narcan/naloxone.md).
