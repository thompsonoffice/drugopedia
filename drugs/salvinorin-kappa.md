# Salvinorin A and kappa-opioid agonists

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Salvinorin A, from Salvia divinorum, is a potent kappa-opioid agonist, not a mu agonist. Included because it acts on opioid receptors, but its effects are hallucinogenic, not euphoric.

**Brand and street names:** Salvia, diviner's sage, 'Sally D'. Related research kappa agonists include U-50488 and enadoline.

## History and origins

Mazatec healers in Oaxaca, Mexico, have used Salvia divinorum in ceremonies for centuries. Its active compound was identified in 1982, and in 2002 Bryan Roth's lab showed it is a kappa-opioid agonist. YouTube videos of people smoking salvia led many US states to ban it around 2008 to 2012.

## Medical use, then and now

None approved; studied in research.

## How it entered recreational use

Smoked or chewed for intense, short hallucinations.

## Legal status

Not federally controlled in the US; banned in many states. UK Psychoactive Substances Act.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Salvia is nothing like other opioids. People describe sudden, overwhelming dissociation: feeling pulled, merged with objects, reality folding or 'zippering', loss of body, and often fear or dysphoria. Euphoria is rare.

Kappa agonists tend to cause dysphoria, which is why they have been studied as non-addictive painkillers and are not used for pleasure.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-60 sec)

Smoked, effects begin within 15 to 60 seconds.

### Come-up (under 1 min)

Reality shifts rapidly.

### Peak (1-5 min)

Peak at 1 to 5 minutes: intense dissociation and hallucinations.

### Plateau (5-10 min)

Lasts a few minutes.

### Comedown (10-20 min)

Fades over 10 to 20 minutes.

### After-effects (20-60 min)

Confusion and an afterglow or unease.

### Next day

Usually normal next day.

## Overdose note for Salvinorin A and kappa-opioid agonists

Overdose like mu opioids does not happen, but people can injure themselves during the experience. Naloxone does not reliably help. Have a sober sitter.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). Also see [naloxone (Narcan)](../narcan/naloxone.md).
