# Caffeine

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Adenosine receptor blocker.

**Brand and street names:** Coffee, tea, energy drinks, NoDoz, Vivarin; pure caffeine powders; caffeine citrate (for premature babies).

## History and origins

Tea has been drunk in China for thousands of years, and coffee spread from Ethiopia and Yemen in the 1400s. Friedlieb Runge isolated caffeine in 1819.

## Medical use, then and now

Apnea of prematurity; added to some headache medicines.

## How it entered recreational use

The most widely used psychoactive drug in the world.

## Legal status

Legal; in 2018 the FDA declared bulk pure and highly concentrated caffeine products sold to consumers unlawful because of deaths.

## What the pills feel like

Caffeine pills are usually white tablets; pure powders are fine and white.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Caffeine brings alertness, reduced fatigue and a mild mood lift by blocking adenosine, a chemical that builds sleep pressure.

Too much causes jitters, anxiety, fast heartbeat, stomach upset and insomnia.

Pure caffeine powders and concentrated liquids have caused deaths, because a tiny amount can be lethal.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Caffeine, found in coffee, tea, energy drinks, sodas and pills, blocks adenosine, the brain chemical that builds up sleep pressure. Swallowed, it begins within about half an hour: a warmth in the chest, a slightly quicker heartbeat, a loosening of drowsiness in the eyes and face, and for many a nudge to the bowels.

Mentally the onset is a gentle brightening. Fog lifts, mood rises a notch and thoughts move a little faster; for regular users much of this is simply relief from the headache and sluggishness of mild caffeine withdrawal.

Sensitivity varies a lot between people, partly due to genes and liver enzymes. Pregnancy, some medications and smoking status change how long it lasts.

Caffeine is almost completely absorbed within 45 minutes.

It blocks adenosine A1 and A2A receptors, raising alertness, heart rate and urine production.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Alertness rises.

### Peak (about 1 h)

At its peak the body feels alert and energized, with a faster pulse, more frequent urination and sometimes a fine tremor in the hands. On an empty stomach it can cause heartburn or nausea. Some feel their heartbeat as a flutter, and the skin may feel warm and slightly sweaty.

Mentally there is focus, motivation and sociability. With too much, this tips into jitteriness, racing thoughts, irritability and anxiety, and sometimes a sense of dread or a panic attack in sensitive people.

Concentrated caffeine powders and pills have caused deaths from heart rhythm disturbances and seizures because tiny measuring errors give enormous amounts; very large intake from energy drinks combined with alcohol also hides drunkenness.

At high levels, it causes tremor, palpitations, vomiting and arrhythmias; severe poisoning lowers potassium and causes seizures.

Concentrated powders and liquids have caused deaths because the line between a normal and a lethal amount is invisible.

### Plateau (3-5 h)

The plateau lasts several hours as caffeine is slowly cleared. The body stays mildly revved, with restlessness in the legs, a slightly raised heart rate and increased urination that can lead to mild dehydration. The stomach may stay acidic and unsettled, and some notice a slight ringing in the ears.

Mentally alertness continues but the pleasant edge fades; overstimulated people may feel scattered, tense or easily startled, and may find it hard to relax. Small sounds can feel louder, and people may talk faster or tap their feet without realizing.

Caffeine in the afternoon often lingers into bedtime, making sleep lighter and shorter even when people do fall asleep.

### Comedown (5-8 h)

As it wears off, a wave of tiredness returns, often heavier than before because sleep pressure built up underneath. Some notice a mild headache. Hands may feel slightly shaky until a meal, and the eyes can feel dry and heavy.

Emotionally people may feel flat, irritable or foggy and reach for another cup. Some people get a short burst of low mood or anxiety in the late afternoon that is really the caffeine leaving.

Daily users develop dependence; skipping it brings withdrawal headaches, fatigue, low mood and poor concentration for a few days, which fade with gradual cutting down. Cutting down slowly over a week or two avoids most of the withdrawal headache.

Half-life is about 5 hours, longer in pregnancy and with some medicines.

Daily users get withdrawal headaches, fatigue and low mood within a day of stopping.

### After-effects (8-12 h)

Possible headache.

### Next day (next day (withdrawal headache))

Regular users get withdrawal headaches if they skip a day.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Caffeine

Caffeine overdose causes vomiting, fast irregular heartbeat and seizures, and can be fatal with concentrated products.

Read the shared page: [Stimulant safety: overheating, heart risks, psychosis, fentanyl contamination](../safety/stimulant-safety.md).
