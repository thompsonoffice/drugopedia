# Cannabis concentrates (wax, shatter, dabs, vape oil)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Extracts with very high THC content.

**Brand and street names:** Dabs, wax, shatter, budder, rosin, live resin, hash oil, BHO, vape carts.

## History and origins

Hashish is centuries old; modern solvent-extracted concentrates spread in the 2010s with legal markets and 'dabbing'.

## Medical use, then and now

THC vape oil and concentrates are sold through some state medical programs.

## How it entered recreational use

Concentrates and vape cartridges are popular because they are discreet and very strong, often several times the THC of flower.

## Legal status

Same as cannabis flower. Illicit vape carts were linked to the 2019 EVALI lung-injury outbreak, caused largely by vitamin E acetate.

## What the products look and feel like

Concentrates range from glassy brittle sheets (shatter) to soft wax or thick oil in cartridges. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Concentrates hit fast and hard: a heavy rush to the head, pounding heart and a sudden, intense high that can overwhelm even experienced flower users.

The high is more 'pure THC' and can feel narrower and more disorienting, with stronger dizziness and time distortion.

Heavy daily concentrate use is linked in studies to higher risk of psychosis, cannabis use disorder, and cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome.

Tolerance to the high builds quickly with daily use and fades after a break of a few weeks; people returning after a break, or switching to concentrates or edibles, are often surprised by how strong the effect is.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (seconds)

Inhaled concentrates take effect within seconds: a strong head rush and pounding heart.

Concentrates deliver a large amount of THC through the lungs at once, so blood levels spike higher and faster than with flower.

The heart-rate jump is larger, and dizziness or fainting is more common, especially when standing up.

### Come-up (2-10 min)

Within minutes the high rises steeply; coughing is common.

### Peak (10-30 min)

The peak is intense and early: disorientation, heavy body, and for some, panic.

The peak is dominated by very high CB1 activation with little CBD to balance it; studies link daily high-potency use to roughly several-fold higher odds of psychosis.

Very high exposure is associated with cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome in heavy users and with acute panic and vomiting in occasional users.

### Plateau (30-60 min)

The plateau lasts about an hour.

### Comedown (1-3 h)

Fades over 1 to 3 hours.

Because the dose is concentrated, the fall from peak is steeper and fatigue can be heavy.

Tolerance builds especially fast with daily dabbing, and withdrawal (irritability, insomnia, low appetite, vivid dreams) is more marked.

### After-effects (2-4 h)

Heavy fatigue and hunger.

### Next day (next morning)

Daily users can feel foggy and irritable between uses.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cannabis brings relaxation, mild euphoria, giggliness, and heightened senses. At higher doses, time distortion, anxiety, paranoia, a racing heart, nausea, dizziness and, for some, hallucinations or panic appear. Very high doses, especially edibles, can cause hours of confusion, vomiting and frightening psychotic symptoms.

Because concentrates are so strong, even a small amount moves people from mild to overwhelming effects.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Cannabis concentrates (wax, shatter, dabs, vape oil)

Concentrates cause more panic, fainting, vomiting and psychosis emergencies than flower; illicit vape carts can contain harmful additives.

Read the shared page: [Cannabis safety: edibles, high-potency products, synthetic cannabinoids](../safety/cannabis-safety.md).
