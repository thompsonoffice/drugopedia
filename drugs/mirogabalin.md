# Mirogabalin (Tarlige)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## At a glance

Gabapentinoid with selective, slow release from alpha-2-delta-1.

**Brand and street names:** Tarlige (Japan, Korea, Taiwan).

## History and origins

Developed by Daiichi Sankyo and approved in Japan in 2019.

## Medical use, then and now

Nerve pain from diabetes, shingles and spinal cord injury.

## How it entered recreational use

Little reported misuse.

## Legal status

Not approved in the US or EU.

## What the pills feel like

Film-coated tablets.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Mirogabalin's character in patients is drowsiness and dizziness with pain relief; recreational reports are rare.

It is cleared by the kidneys, so effects are stronger in kidney disease.

Interactions with other depressants are the main risk.

Tolerance to the pleasant and sleep effects builds with regular use, but tolerance to slowed breathing builds more slowly, so the danger stays. Daily use can lead to dependence, and stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 1 h)

Swallowed, it begins within about 1 hour.

Medically, the onset reflects slow and variable absorption. Mirogabalin is cleared mostly by the kidneys with a half-life of about 3 hours. Gabapentin is absorbed by a transporter in the gut that saturates, so more is not absorbed in proportion; pregabalin is absorbed more fully and quickly.

Early effects include calm, mild dizziness, and relaxation. Because gabapentinoids are cleared by the kidneys, people with kidney disease or older adults are affected more strongly and for longer.

### Come-up (1 h)

Gradual dizziness.

### Peak (about 1 h)

Peak around 1 hour for blood levels; effects feel steady.

Medically, the peak brings the strongest dizziness, unsteadiness, and sedation. Some people feel calm sociability or mild euphoria; others feel only clumsy and sleepy.

In 2019 the FDA warned that gabapentinoids can cause serious breathing problems when combined with opioids or other depressants, or in people with lung disease or older age. Gabapentinoids appear in many opioid overdose deaths.

### Plateau (several hours)

Several hours.

### Comedown (about 12 h)

Fades over about 12 hours.

As levels fall, dizziness and sedation fade over several hours. Sleep is often deep.

Daily use can cause dependence; stopping suddenly can cause anxiety, insomnia, sweating, pain, agitation, and occasionally seizures, so medical tapering is advised.

### After-effects (next day)

Drowsiness.

### Next day (little)

Little.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, gabapentinoids cause calm, mild dizziness, and relaxation. At higher doses, unsteadiness, slurred speech, double vision, and heavy sedation increase, and some people feel a drunk-like euphoria. With opioids or other depressants, breathing can slow dangerously.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, age, liver and kidney function, genetics, and especially other depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines) change the effect. Older adults and people with sleep apnea or lung disease are at higher risk. Stronger effects do not stop at more pleasure: they shade into blackouts, falls, and slowed breathing.

## Overdose note for Mirogabalin (Tarlige)

Overdose causes heavy sedation; risks rise with opioids.
