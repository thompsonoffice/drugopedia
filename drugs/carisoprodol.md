# Carisoprodol (Soma)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## At a glance

Centrally acting muscle relaxant; a prodrug partly converted to meprobamate.

**Brand and street names:** Soma, Vanadom; combinations with aspirin (Soma Compound) and with codeine (discontinued). Street: 'somas', 'dans', 'D's'.

## History and origins

Developed by Frank Berger's team at Wallace Laboratories as a successor to meprobamate and approved in 1959. It was unscheduled federally for decades, despite widespread misuse, until the DEA placed it in Schedule IV in 2012.

## Medical use, then and now

Short-term (two to three weeks) relief of acute, painful back and muscle conditions. Many guidelines now discourage it because of dependence.

## How it entered recreational use

It became part of the 'holy trinity' or 'Houston cocktail' (an opioid, a benzodiazepine, and carisoprodol) promoted by pill mills in the 2000s, a combination linked to many deaths.

## Legal status

US Schedule IV (2012). Withdrawn from the European market in 2008 because of misuse.

## What the pills feel like

Per DailyMed labels, brand Soma tablets are round, convex and white, inscribed 'SOMA' with the strength number; many generics are white and round.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Carisoprodol's character comes largely from meprobamate, its metabolite: a loose, drunk-like relaxation with calm and sometimes a warm, chatty euphoria, more 'alcohol-like' than other muscle relaxants.

People describe heavy limbs, a pleasant fuzziness, and clumsy coordination. Combined with opioids it was said to 'boost' the opioid, which is exactly the combination that stops breathing.

It also causes dependence; withdrawal after daily use brings anxiety, tremor, insomnia, hallucinations and sometimes seizures.

Tolerance to the pleasant and sleep effects builds with regular use, but tolerance to slowed breathing builds more slowly, so the danger stays. Daily use can lead to dependence, and stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 30 min)

Carisoprodol (Soma) arrives as a creeping looseness rather than a jolt. The first thing most people notice is the back, shoulders and thighs softening, as if a clenched fist inside each muscle slowly opens. A mild heaviness gathers behind the eyes, the head feels slightly too large for the neck, and the hands may feel faintly numb or buzzy at the fingertips.

Mentally the onset feels like the edges of the day being sanded down. Irritation and worry lose their bite, and a calm, slightly silly contentment can appear. Because part of carisoprodol is converted in the liver to meprobamate, an older anxiety drug, many people describe a calm that feels more like a barbiturate or alcohol than like a simple muscle relaxer.

Small signs of sedation show up early: blinking slows, speech becomes a little lazy, and standing up quickly can bring a brief wave of dizziness. Some people feel a warm flush or a slight queasy, hollow sensation in the stomach. Clumsiness begins before the person realizes they are impaired.

Medically, the onset reflects absorption from the gut and entry into the brain. Carisoprodol is processed by the CYP2C19 liver enzyme into meprobamate, which has a much longer half-life; people with low CYP2C19 activity feel stronger, longer effects. Muscle relaxants work mostly through central sedation rather than acting on the muscles themselves (dantrolene is the exception), so drowsiness usually arrives together with the loosening of tension.

Clinicians warn that reaction time slows early, often before the person notices, and that dizziness on standing is common, especially in older adults.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Over the next half hour a drunk-like looseness builds; speech can slur slightly.

### Peak (1.5-2 h)

At the peak the body feels heavy, warm and pleasantly boneless. Lying down feels like sinking, and muscles that were aching or spasming go quiet. Many describe a tingling, slightly drunk body buzz, with arms and legs that feel far away. Balance is clearly off: walking is wobbly, the room can tilt, and fine movements like typing or buttoning become fumbling.

Emotionally the peak is a loose, sociable, carefree mellowness, often compared to a couple of drinks combined with a mild sedative. People may become chatty and giggly, or simply drowsy and dreamy. Thinking is slowed and memory for this period may be patchy, especially in someone new to it.

This is the riskiest window. Carisoprodol is notorious for being combined with opioids and benzodiazepines (the old 'holy trinity'), and that combination deepens breathing suppression sharply. Very heavy drowsiness, slurred speech, jerking or twitching muscles, confusion, or slow and noisy breathing mean danger; seizures and coma have been reported in overdose.

Medically, the peak is when sedation and dizziness are strongest. Several muscle relaxants have anticholinergic or blood-pressure effects as well, which can cause dry mouth, blurred vision, a fast or slow heartbeat, or fainting.

Alone they rarely stop breathing in healthy adults, but combined with opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol, or gabapentinoids they add to respiratory depression; carisoprodol in particular was a common part of the deadly 'holy trinity' with opioids and benzodiazepines.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The plateau holds a steady, heavy relaxation. Muscles remain soft and the body feels warm and slightly numb. Some people get a pleasant drifting feeling and drop into short naps, waking with a start; others feel restless legs or slight muscle twitches as the meprobamate builds up.

Mentally the plateau is foggy and contented. Conversations are easy but forgetful, and concentration is poor. The early silliness tends to settle into a quiet, sleepy calm in which nothing seems urgent.

Because the meprobamate metabolite lasts much longer than carisoprodol itself, sedation can linger well after the pleasant part fades, so driving or mixing in other depressants later in the day remains dangerous.

### Comedown (4-6 h total (meprobamate longer))

As carisoprodol wears off, the body feels tired and heavy rather than relaxed, and the original muscle tension or pain may creep back. Some notice a dull headache, dry mouth, or mild nausea.

Emotionally the comedown is often flat and groggy, with irritability or a slightly low, empty mood. Many wake the next morning feeling hungover and slow.

Regular use builds tolerance and real physical dependence, mostly through meprobamate. Stopping suddenly after heavy daily use can cause anxiety, insomnia, tremor, vomiting, hallucinations and, rarely, seizures, so stopping should be medically guided.

As levels fall, sedation lifts and muscle tension may return. Short-acting drugs wear off within hours, while drugs with active metabolites (carisoprodol to meprobamate) linger.

People who use carisoprodol, meprobamate, or baclofen daily can develop dependence; stopping suddenly can cause anxiety, insomnia, hallucinations, and seizures, and abrupt baclofen withdrawal can be life-threatening.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Drowsiness, headache, and sometimes irritability.

### Next day

Next-day grogginess; dependence develops with daily use.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, muscle relaxants mainly cause drowsiness and eased tension. At higher doses, dizziness, heavy sedation, poor coordination, blurred vision, and confusion grow, and some (carisoprodol, meprobamate, orphenadrine, baclofen, tizanidine) bring additional dangers: seizures, heart rhythm problems, very low blood pressure, or coma. With opioids, alcohol, or benzodiazepines, breathing can slow and stop.

At higher doses carisoprodol causes heavy sedation, double vision, muscle jerks and seizures, and with opioids breathing can stop.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, age, liver and kidney function, genetics, and especially other depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines) change the effect. Older adults and people with sleep apnea or lung disease are at higher risk. Stronger effects do not stop at more pleasure: they shade into blackouts, falls, and slowed breathing.

## Overdose note for Carisoprodol (Soma)

Overdose causes stupor, coma, muscle jerks, seizures, and slowed breathing; most deaths involve opioids, benzodiazepines, or alcohol.
