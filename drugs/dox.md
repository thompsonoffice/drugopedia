# DOx amphetamines (DOM, DOB, DOC, DOI)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Long-acting psychedelic amphetamines.

**Brand and street names:** DOM (STP), DOB, DOC, DOI.

## History and origins

DOM was described by Shulgin in 1963 and appeared as 'STP' in Haight-Ashbury in 1967, where many people were hospitalized because it was much longer and stronger than expected.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Sometimes sold as LSD on blotter.

## Legal status

DOM, DOB: US Schedule I, UN Schedule I; DOC: controlled in many countries.

## What the products look and feel like

Usually blotter. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

DOx drugs are very long, often lasting most of a day, with LSD-like visuals and strong stimulation.

Slow onset tempts people to take more.

Vasoconstriction can be severe; DOB has caused deaths.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-3 h)

DOx compounds (DOM, DOB, DOC, DOI) have a very slow onset, sometimes hours, which leads people to take more before the first has taken effect. Body stimulation and tension build gradually: a tight jaw, cold extremities, restlessness and a growing buzz. DOx compounds (DOM, DOB, DOC, DOI) are very slow to begin, sometimes taking several hours to be felt, which makes people believe nothing has happened and take more. The body may feel stimulated, tense and cold, with nausea, rising heart rate and a tight jaw.

Mentally a slow, building intensity, sometimes with impatience or anxiety. Mentally the onset may be confusing, as subtle changes accumulate unnoticed.

Pupils widen and the jaw may clench.

DOx compounds are absorbed slowly, with onset often taking hours.

Blood vessels constrict and heart rate rises.

### Come-up (2-4 h)

Slow build.

### Peak (4-8 h)

At the peak there are strong visuals and stimulation, often for many hours, with a physical edginess like amphetamine. Body stimulation is strong. Pupils are wide and the jaw stays tight.

Emotionally intense; can be euphoric or anxious and exhausting.

DOB and DOC can constrict blood vessels and have caused deaths. Psychedelics rarely threaten the body directly, but they impair judgment and coordination, so falls, traffic, water and heights are real dangers. Panic, paranoia and terrifying experiences can happen to anyone, and people with a personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder face a higher risk of lasting harm.

At peak, intense psychedelic effects with stimulation; vasospasm can cause limb ischemia.

Very long duration increases exhaustion and risk.

### Plateau (8-14 h)

The DOx plateau is extremely long, often lasting most of a day or more, with steady visuals, a stimulated body and persistent alertness. Vasoconstriction can cause cold hands and feet, muscle tension and aching, and the heart may stay fast for many hours.

Mood varies between euphoric, introspective and exhausted, and people may feel worn down by the sheer length of the experience. Thinking can loop, and fatigue combined with stimulation creates a wired, tired feeling.

Overheating, severe vasoconstriction, seizures and heart strain are dangers at higher amounts. DOx substances are sometimes sold as LSD, and their long duration and slow onset make misjudgment dangerous.

### Comedown (14-24 h)

The comedown is drawn out, with stimulation persisting well into the next day. The body feels exhausted yet restless, with muscle aches, headache, sore jaw and a poor appetite. Faint visuals may linger at the edges of vision, and the skin often feels clammy, cold or oversensitive.

Mood may be flat, irritable, anxious or quietly relieved that it is over, and many people find concentration difficult for a day or more. Some feel emotionally drained and need extended rest.

Sleep is often impossible until the next night. Planning for a very long experience, with no responsibilities or travel, matters, and emergency signs need immediate medical care.

Effects can last a day or more.

Sleep loss and anxiety may persist.

### After-effects (24-36 h)

Insomnia.

### Next day (next 1-2 days)

Effects may linger into the next day.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
