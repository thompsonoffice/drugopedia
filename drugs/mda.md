# MDA (sass, sally)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Ring-substituted amphetamine, more psychedelic than MDMA.

**Brand and street names:** Sass, sally, Sassafras, love drug.

## History and origins

First synthesized in 1910; tested as an appetite suppressant and antidepressant in the 1950s. Popular as the 'love drug' in the late 1960s, before MDMA. Also an MDMA metabolite.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Sold alone or within ecstasy pills.

## Legal status

US Schedule I; UN 1971 Schedule I.

## What the products look and feel like

Off-white crystals or capsules. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

MDA is longer and more stimulating and visual than MDMA, with less emotional softness.

Body load is heavier: sweating, jaw clenching and raised temperature.

Animal studies suggest MDA is more neurotoxic than MDMA; deaths from overheating have occurred.

Tolerance to the warmth fades with frequent use while side effects remain.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (45-90 min)

MDA, MDMA's older cousin (once called the 'love drug'), has a moderate onset with a tingly rush, jaw tension, warmth and often nausea. The body feels energized and sensual. MDA usually begins within an hour with warmth, tingling, rising heart rate, wide pupils and a tight jaw. Nausea and a churning stomach are common early, and the body feels energized, restless and hot, with flushed skin and an urge to move. Pupils widen.

Mentally euphoric and energized, with warmth, but more trippy, visual and stimulating and less 'loved-up' than MDMA. Mood rises into excitement, sometimes with jitteriness or anxiety.

Pupils widen and heart rate climbs; sweating begins.

MDA is absorbed slowly and releases serotonin and dopamine.

Temperature and heart rate rise.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Energy and euphoria rise.

### Peak (2-3 h)

At the peak there is euphoria, stimulation, mild visuals and strong urges to move or dance. Jaw clenching and sweating are strong. The body can overheat quickly, and visuals stay vivid.

Emotionally warm and energized, sometimes with an edge of anxiety. Pupils are huge, and touch feels electric.

Empathogens push body temperature, heart rate and blood pressure up. Overheating, dehydration, and low blood sodium from drinking too much water are the classic dangers; combining with other stimulants, MAOIs, SSRIs, tramadol or DXM can cause serotonin syndrome. A hot, confused, rigid or seizing person needs 911 and cooling. MDA is thought to be more toxic to the brain than MDMA.

At peak, 5-HT2A activation adds visual effects; overheating and serotonin toxicity are dangers.

Neurotoxicity in animals appears greater than MDMA.

### Plateau (3-5 h)

The MDA plateau lasts several hours, longer than MDMA, with sustained body energy, warmth, tactile pleasure and light visuals such as color shifts, trails and patterns. The heart stays fast, the jaw tense, and the body hot; overheating is a real danger in crowded, warm places.

Mood is euphoric and sociable, though often described as more stimulating and psychedelic and less emotionally soft than MDMA. Some feel giddy and playful; others become anxious, confused or overwhelmed, especially at higher amounts.

Hyperthermia, dehydration, hyponatremia from drinking too much water, and serotonin toxicity are key risks, especially when combined with other serotonergic drugs. MDA appears to be more neurotoxic than MDMA in animal studies.

### Comedown (5-8 h)

The comedown brings fatigue, a sore jaw, aching muscles and lingering restlessness. The body may feel overheated, dehydrated and wrung out, with a lack of appetite and difficulty settling down. Faint visuals and tingling may linger, and the jaw and teeth may feel sore and sensitive for hours.

Mood often dips into flatness, irritability or low feelings in the following days, as serotonin stores recover. Some describe a sense of emptiness or anxiety that can be more pronounced than after MDMA. Hydration and rest help.

Sleep is often difficult. Spacing use widely, avoiding mixing, and seeking help for confusion, high temperature or chest pain reduce harm.

Half-life is long (about 11 hours).

Comedown and low mood follow.

### After-effects (8-24 h)

Insomnia.

### Next day (2-4 days)

Low mood for days.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses empathogens bring warmth, openness and mild stimulation. At higher doses, stimulation, jaw clenching, sweating and overheating rise while the pleasant effects do not increase much. At very high levels, serotonin toxicity, overheating, seizures and collapse can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
