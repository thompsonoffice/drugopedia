# Illicit fentanyl

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Illegally manufactured fentanyl and fentanyl analogs (such as carfentanil, acetylfentanyl, and others), sold as powder or pressed into counterfeit pills.

**Brand and street names:** No brands. Street names include "fent", "fetty", "blues", "M30s", "dance fever", "China white", "tango and cash".

## History and origins

Isolated outbreaks of street fentanyl appeared in the US from the late 1970s ('China white'). From around 2013, illegally made fentanyl, often from chemicals produced overseas, replaced much of the heroin supply in North America. By 2016 it was the leading cause of overdose death, and synthetic opioids caused around 70,000 US deaths a year at the peak around 2022 to 2023. Newer adulterants include xylazine ('tranq'), an animal sedative that causes severe wounds, and nitazenes, a separate family of synthetic opioids.

## Medical use, then and now

None. Illicit fentanyl is not made to any medical standard.

## How it entered recreational use

Most people who die from fentanyl did not know they were taking it: it is mixed into heroin, counterfeit oxycodone and Xanax pills, cocaine, and methamphetamine. Many people with opioid use disorder now use it knowingly because it has displaced heroin.

## Legal status

US Schedule II for fentanyl; analogs Schedule I as a class.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

People describe illicit fentanyl as a fast, heavy, short high, with less of heroin's warm, dreamy euphoria and more blank sedation, followed by quick, harsh withdrawal a few hours later. Many describe it as 'chasing', needing to use again and again through the day.

Because pressed pills and powders are mixed unevenly ('chocolate chip cookie effect'), one part may contain little and another a deadly amount. Xylazine adds very long blackouts and skin wounds.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (seconds)

Smoked or injected, street fentanyl hits within seconds. Many describe an immediate heavy rush in the head and body, less warm and more 'blank' than heroin.

### Come-up (seconds)

The come-up is almost nonexistent. Some describe a strong sinking feeling, like falling into a soft hole.

### Peak (1-3 min)

The peak lasts only minutes: heavy sedation, stillness, and a numb, faraway feeling. Many survivors report no warning before losing consciousness, which is why fentanyl overdose kills so quickly.

### Plateau (5-20 min)

The plateau is short and nod-heavy. With xylazine mixed in, people may stay unconscious for hours.

### Comedown (20-60 min)

Fentanyl wears off fast, often in 1 to 2 hours, and the comedown comes harshly with restlessness, sweats, and anxiety.

### After-effects (1-4 h)

Withdrawal begins within hours in dependent people. With xylazine, wounds and long sedation are common.

### Next day (withdrawal may begin)

People who use daily describe needing to use many times a day to stay out of withdrawal, which is intense.

## How the route changes the experience

- [Inhaled and smoked](../routes/inhaled-smoked/fentanyl-illicit.md): Smoking on foil is now the most common way illicit fentanyl is used in many areas.
- [Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)](../routes/injected/fentanyl-illicit.md): Injected, often mixed with other drugs.
- [Intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting)](../routes/intranasal/fentanyl-illicit.md): Snorted, often unknowingly in powders sold as other drugs.
- [Oral (swallowed)](../routes/oral/fentanyl-illicit.md): Swallowed as counterfeit pills.

Not covered: No rectal, transdermal, or sublingual illicit use is common enough to cover.

## Overdose note for Illicit fentanyl

Overdose often comes within seconds to minutes, sometimes before the person can put the drug down. Chest-wall rigidity ('wooden chest') can make breathing impossible. Multiple naloxone doses may be needed. Fentanyl test strips can detect it in drugs before use, and are legal in most US states. Never use alone; services like the Never Use Alone hotline (1-877-696-1996) send help if a caller stops responding.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). Also see [naloxone (Narcan)](../narcan/naloxone.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
