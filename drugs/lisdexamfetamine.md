# Lisdexamfetamine (Vyvanse)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Prodrug converted to dextroamphetamine in the blood.

**Brand and street names:** Vyvanse (capsules and chewables).

## History and origins

Developed by New River Pharmaceuticals and approved in 2007 for ADHD, and in 2015 for binge-eating disorder.

## Medical use, then and now

ADHD and moderate-to-severe binge-eating disorder.

## How it entered recreational use

Less misused than IR amphetamine because the prodrug design slows onset by any route, but still misused for focus.

## Legal status

US Schedule II.

## Immediate-release vs extended-release

Vyvanse is long-acting by its chemistry (a prodrug), not by a coating, so it releases steadily however it is taken; higher amounts still cause harm.

## What the pills feel like

Per DailyMed, Vyvanse capsules have colored caps and bodies printed 'S489' with the strength; chewables are round and imprinted 'S489'.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Vyvanse feels smoother and longer than Adderall: a gradual rise into steady focus and energy, with a gentler comedown.

Side effects include low appetite, insomnia, dry mouth, anxiety and raised heart rate.

Because red blood cells must convert it, other routes do not make it faster, but higher amounts still cause stimulant toxicity.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-2 h)

Lisdexamfetamine (Vyvanse, Elvanse) is a prodrug: it is inactive until enzymes in the blood slowly cut it into dextroamphetamine. Swallowed, it comes on gradually, with no sharp rush. Over the first hour or two the body warms slightly, the heart picks up a little, the mouth dries and appetite quietly fades.

Mentally the onset feels like a fog lifting: background restlessness settles for people with ADHD, tasks look more manageable and the mind locks onto what is in front of it. People without ADHD may feel a smooth lift in mood, energy and talkativeness.

Its slow conversion was designed to make other routes no faster and to reduce misuse, though it is still an amphetamine with the same risks at high amounts.

Lisdexamfetamine is absorbed intact and cleaved in red blood cells to release dextroamphetamine, so the rate is limited by enzyme activity.

This makes onset slower and peak levels lower than the same amphetamine in IR form.

### Come-up (2-3 h)

A gradual lift in focus.

### Peak (3-4 h)

At its peak the body is alert and slightly tense: faster pulse, raised blood pressure, dry mouth, clenched jaw for some, and little interest in food. Sweating and a mild tremor can appear. Some notice a gentle warmth in the face and an urge to keep moving, pacing or tidying.

Mentally the peak is steady focus, motivation and confidence. Tasks feel rewarding, and people may work for hours without noticing time. At higher amounts this becomes edgy, overly talkative and anxious, with racing thoughts. Conversation can become fast and one-sided, and people may hyperfocus on small details while losing track of bigger priorities.

Risks include high blood pressure, heart rhythm problems, anxiety, and, rarely, psychosis or mania, especially with high amounts or sleep loss.

At peak, effects are those of dextroamphetamine: alertness, raised heart rate and blood pressure.

The FDA label includes a boxed warning for misuse and dependence.

### Plateau (10-14 h)

The plateau is long, often most of the day, which is part of why it is popular for ADHD. Physically people stay mildly wired, with low appetite, dry mouth and sometimes headaches or a tight jaw. Hands may feel slightly shaky, and people often forget to drink water or eat until the afternoon.

Mentally attention stays steady, though some feel flat, overly serious or emotionally muted ('zombie' feeling), and irritability can build late in the day. Late in the plateau the sense of drive often thins into a tense, wired tiredness, especially after a poor night's sleep.

Taking it late or in extra amounts causes insomnia, and skipping meals adds to crash and mood problems.

### Comedown (12-14 h)

As it wears off, often in the evening, people feel tired, hungry, headachy and sometimes achy. Hunger can arrive suddenly and intensely, and some people feel a heavy, sleepy drag in the early evening.

Emotionally a 'rebound' of irritability, low mood, tearfulness or returning ADHD restlessness is common, usually milder than with short-acting amphetamine. Small frustrations can feel bigger than usual, and family members often notice the change before the person does.

Misuse or high amounts can lead to dependence and a heavier crash with depression and sleepiness; any change in use should be discussed with the prescriber. Never stop suddenly after long high use without advice, because the low can be severe.

Dextroamphetamine's half-life extends effects into the evening.

Insomnia and appetite loss are the most common lingering effects.

### After-effects (14-24 h)

Insomnia if taken late.

### Next day

Next-day fatigue after heavy use.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.
