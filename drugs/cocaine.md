# Cocaine

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin reuptake inhibitor; also a local anesthetic.

**Brand and street names:** Medical: cocaine hydrochloride nasal solution (Goprelto, Numbrino). Street: coke, blow, snow, yayo.

## History and origins

Coca leaf has been chewed in the Andes for thousands of years. Albert Niemann isolated cocaine in 1860; Carl Koller used it as an eye anesthetic in 1884, and Freud promoted it. It was in Coca-Cola until about 1903 and restricted by the Harrison Act of 1914.

## Medical use, then and now

Still used as a topical anesthetic that also shrinks blood vessels in nose and throat procedures.

## How it entered recreational use

Popular since the 1970s and 1980s; today many cocaine-involved deaths also involve fentanyl.

## Legal status

US Schedule II; UN 1961 Single Convention.

## What the pills feel like

Powder cocaine is white and crystalline and is often cut with other substances, including levamisole (which can destroy white blood cells) and fentanyl. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Cocaine's character is a short, bright burst of confidence, talkativeness and euphoria, followed quickly by craving for more.

The heart races, blood vessels tighten and body temperature rises; even young, healthy people can have heart attacks or strokes.

With alcohol, the liver makes cocaethylene, which is more toxic to the heart. Repeated use brings anxiety, paranoia and nasal damage.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-5 min)

Snorted, effects begin within a few minutes: numbness, alertness and euphoria.

Snorted cocaine also numbs the nasal lining and narrows its blood vessels, slowing its own absorption slightly.

It blocks dopamine, noradrenaline and serotonin reuptake and also blocks sodium channels, which affects heart rhythm.

### Come-up (5-15 min)

Confidence and energy surge.

### Peak (15-30 min)

The peak comes within 15 to 30 minutes.

At peak, coronary arteries narrow, heart oxygen demand rises and platelets clump, so heart attack can occur even in young people; stroke and aortic dissection are also linked.

With alcohol, the liver forms cocaethylene, which lasts longer and is more toxic to the heart.

### Plateau (30-60 min)

Effects last about 30 to 60 minutes.

### Comedown (1-2 h)

The comedown brings anxiety and craving.

Cocaine's half-life is under an hour, which drives repeated use.

Chronic snorting can perforate the nasal septum; levamisole adulteration causes skin necrosis and low white cell counts.

### After-effects (2-6 h)

Restlessness and low mood.

### Next day

Next-day fatigue and depression after binges.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Cocaine

Cocaine overdose causes chest pain, heart attack, stroke, seizures and overheating; fentanyl contamination is now a leading cause of death.

Read the shared page: [Stimulant safety: overheating, heart risks, psychosis, fentanyl contamination](../safety/stimulant-safety.md).
