# Clonazepam (Klonopin)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Never stop a daily benzodiazepine suddenly: withdrawal can cause seizures. Counterfeit pills often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines.

## At a glance

Long-acting, high-potency benzodiazepine.

**Brand and street names:** Klonopin, Rivotril, Klonopin wafers (discontinued). Street: 'K-pins', 'pins'.

## History and origins

Patented by Roche in 1960s, approved for seizures in 1975, and later for panic disorder in 1997.

## Medical use, then and now

Seizure disorders, panic disorder, and off-label for REM sleep behavior disorder and restless legs.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused for calm and to soften stimulant or opioid withdrawal.

## Legal status

US Schedule IV. UK Class C.

## Immediate-release vs extended-release

Clonazepam has no extended-release form; it is naturally long-acting.

## What the pills feel like

Per the DailyMed Klonopin label, the brand tablets have a K-shaped perforation (a cut-out you can feel), with the lowest strength also scored; one generic maker's label lists a round, flat-faced tablet with beveled edges and a score line; generics are small, round, and scored. Orally disintegrating tablets are round and dissolve quickly.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Klonopin is described as a long, steady, flat calm, less euphoric than Xanax, with a slow onset and a gradual fade. People say it 'takes the edge off' all day.

Memory loss and dulled emotions can occur; some describe feeling 'flattened'.

Tolerance to the calming and sleep effects of benzodiazepines builds within weeks, while memory loss and the danger of mixing with alcohol or opioids remain. People taking them daily often feel little except relief from anxiety, and stopping suddenly can cause seizures.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (20-60 min)

Clonazepam (Klonopin) comes on slowly and quietly. There is rarely a moment people can point to; instead, over a while they notice the jaw is no longer clenched, the shoulders have dropped, and the breath has deepened. The limbs take on a mild, even heaviness, and the eyelids feel slightly weighted.

Mentally the onset is a gradual lowering of the emotional volume. Anxious thoughts are still present but lose their urgency, as if they are happening behind glass. People with panic disorder describe it as a floor being put under them rather than a sudden rescue, and the calm builds without the rush that can make alprazolam feel 'druggy'.

Early physical changes include mild clumsiness, slower reaction times and a slight dryness or thickness in speech. Because clonazepam is long-acting and accumulates with repeated use, effects from earlier use can still be present, and combining with alcohol or opioids is dangerous from the start.

From a medical point of view, the onset reflects how quickly the drug is absorbed and enters the brain. Clonazepam is metabolized by CYP3A4; half-life 30 to 40 hours. As it binds to GABA-A receptors, brain activity slows: anxiety eases, muscles relax, and reaction time lengthens.

Clinicians notice early signs such as slower speech, drooping eyelids, and reduced coordination. People often underestimate how impaired they already are at this stage, which is why driving is dangerous.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

A quiet, gradual calm.

### Peak (1-4 h)

At the peak, the body feels steady, loose and heavy. Muscles are relaxed, movement is unhurried, and balance can be unreliable, especially on stairs or in the dark. Some people notice blurred or slow-focusing vision and a mild dizziness on standing.

The emotional tone is better described as blunting than euphoria. People report a flat, even, unbothered state in which neither worry nor excitement rises very far. Some value this deeply; others feel emotionally numb, detached or 'muted', and report that music or relationships feel less vivid. Short-term memory is impaired, and people may repeat questions or forget conversations.

Clonazepam rarely stops breathing on its own in healthy adults, but with opioids, alcohol, gabapentinoids or other sedatives it contributes to fatal overdoses. Counterfeit pills sold as clonazepam can contain designer benzodiazepines or fentanyl. Older adults face high risks of confusion and falls.

Medically, the peak is when sedation, muscle relaxation, and memory impairment are strongest. Benzodiazepines can block the formation of new memories (anterograde amnesia), so people may act normally yet remember nothing later.

On their own, benzodiazepines rarely stop breathing in healthy adults, but combined with opioids or alcohol they greatly deepen respiratory depression. The FDA added its strongest boxed warning about this combination in 2016.

### Plateau (6-12 h)

The plateau is long, often lasting much of a day. The body remains relaxed and drowsy, and people may nap without meaning to. Muscle tone stays low and fine coordination remains clumsy.

Mentally the plateau is a quiet, slowed steadiness. Concentration is reduced, thinking feels a step behind, and motivation is often low. Time can pass without much being noticed, and people sometimes describe the day as 'blurred'.

Driving and machinery remain impaired well into this phase, often longer than people think, and next-morning drowsiness is common because levels fall slowly.

### Comedown (12-24 h)

Because clonazepam leaves the body slowly, the comedown is gentle and spread out. Muscle tension creeps back, sleep can be lighter, and the body feels ordinary rather than crashing.

Emotionally, rebound anxiety is usually milder than after short-acting benzodiazepines, but some people notice irritability, low mood or a return of underlying anxiety as the blunting lifts.

With daily use, dependence develops within weeks. Stopping suddenly can cause severe anxiety, insomnia, tremor, perceptual disturbances and seizures, sometimes days after the last use because of the long half-life, so stopping should follow a slow medical taper.

As levels fall, the calming effect wears off. With short-acting benzodiazepines, rebound anxiety and insomnia can be sharp. Long-acting ones and their active metabolites can linger for days, causing next-day drowsiness and impaired driving.

In people who take them daily, falling levels can trigger withdrawal, which in severe cases includes seizures and delirium. This is why stopping should be a slow medical taper, not a sudden stop.

### After-effects (1-2 days)

Next-day drowsiness possible.

### Next day (still active)

Daily use leads to dependence; withdrawal is slow-onset and can include seizures.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, benzodiazepines mainly ease anxiety and muscle tension, with mild calm and slight drowsiness. At higher doses, sedation, clumsiness, slurred speech, disinhibition, and memory loss grow, and blackouts become likely. As effects keep rising, people become very difficult to wake, confused, and unable to stand, and when opioids or alcohol are involved, breathing slows and can stop: these are overdose warning signs.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance builds quickly, liver function and age change how fast benzodiazepines are cleared (older adults clear many much more slowly), and many medicines block the CYP3A4 enzyme that breaks down alprazolam, midazolam, and triazolam. Alcohol and opioids multiply the sedation and breathing risk. Stronger effects shade into blackouts, falls, and, in combinations, stopped breathing.

## Overdose note for Clonazepam (Klonopin)

Long half-life means accumulation; dangerous with opioids and alcohol. Withdrawal seizures if stopped suddenly.
