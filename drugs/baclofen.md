# Baclofen (Lioresal)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## At a glance

GABA-B receptor agonist muscle relaxant.

**Brand and street names:** Lioresal, Ozobax and Lyvispah (oral liquid and granules), Fleqsuvy, Gablofen and Lioresal Intrathecal (pump).

## History and origins

Synthesized in 1962 by Heinrich Keberle at Ciba as a possible epilepsy drug; approved for spasticity in the US in 1977.

## Medical use, then and now

Spasticity in multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury and cerebral palsy; intrathecal pumps for severe cases. Used off-label for alcohol use disorder, especially in France.

## How it entered recreational use

Some misuse for relaxation and a mild euphoria; overdoses are increasing.

## Legal status

Prescription-only, not controlled.

## What the pills feel like

Generic baclofen tablets are small, white, round, and scored.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Baclofen's character is relaxed, slightly drunk-feeling muscle looseness with drowsiness; some people feel mild euphoria or sociability, others feel weak and confused.

Because it works on GABA-B receptors, it is related in action to GHB; at high levels it can cause confusion, hallucinations and coma.

Stopping suddenly, especially with an intrathecal pump, can cause high fever, hallucinations, seizures, and death.

Tolerance to the pleasant and sleep effects builds with regular use, but tolerance to slowed breathing builds more slowly, so the danger stays. Daily use can lead to dependence, and stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Baclofen acts on GABA-B receptors, the same receptor family GHB touches, and its onset is a slow, creeping relaxation over an hour or so. Muscles feel softer and heavier, especially in the legs, the body warms slightly, and a light, sleepy dizziness sets in that worsens on standing. Some people notice a faint tingling in the hands or face.

Mentally the onset brings a mild calm and a loosening of anxiety. Some describe a faint alcohol-like ease, which is why baclofen is studied for alcohol use disorder; others feel only drowsy, slightly muddled and slow to find words.

Early side effects include nausea, a weak or rubbery feeling in the legs, and a slight drunk-like unsteadiness.

Medically, the onset reflects absorption from the gut and entry into the brain. Baclofen is mostly cleared unchanged by the kidneys with a half-life of about 2 to 6 hours. Muscle relaxants work mostly through central sedation rather than acting on the muscles themselves (dantrolene is the exception), so drowsiness usually arrives together with the loosening of tension.

Clinicians warn that reaction time slows early, often before the person notices, and that dizziness on standing is common, especially in older adults.

### Come-up (1 h)

Muscles loosen and drowsiness grows.

### Peak (1-3 h)

At the peak muscles are clearly slack, coordination poor, and the body heavy and weak. Some report a pleasant, warm body sensation; many notice weakness, slurred speech, confusion and nausea instead. Climbing stairs or getting out of a chair takes effort.

Emotionally the peak ranges from relaxed and content to foggy and disoriented. Higher amounts can bring an intoxicated, GHB-like state with lowered inhibitions, followed by sudden deep sleepiness that is hard to fight.

Overdose can cause severe drowsiness, coma, slowed breathing, low body temperature, slow heart rate and seizures. Combining with alcohol, opioids or other sedatives multiplies the danger, and kidney problems allow it to build up.

Medically, the peak is when sedation and dizziness are strongest. Several muscle relaxants have anticholinergic or blood-pressure effects as well, which can cause dry mouth, blurred vision, a fast or slow heartbeat, or fainting.

Alone they rarely stop breathing in healthy adults, but combined with opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol, or gabapentinoids they add to respiratory depression; carisoprodol in particular was a common part of the deadly 'holy trinity' with opioids and benzodiazepines.

### Plateau (2-5 h)

The baclofen plateau is a long, heavy drowsiness. Muscles feel slack and weak, the legs especially rubbery, and walking or climbing stairs takes concentration. Some people notice a warm, slightly drunk body feeling; others feel mostly weak, nauseated and unsteady. Speech may be a little slurred.

Mentally the plateau is foggy. Concentration is poor, words come slowly, and memory for the period can be patchy. At higher amounts, people can drift between confusion and sleep, and some report odd perceptions, vivid dreams or hallucinations.

Falls, accidents and choking while sedated are real dangers during this phase. With kidney problems, baclofen builds up and can cause severe drowsiness, confusion and coma even at ordinary prescribed amounts.

### Comedown (4-8 h total)

As baclofen wears off, spasticity and tightness return, and many people feel tired, heavy and a little shaky. Headache, nausea, dry mouth and a lingering wobbliness are common, and sleep may be light or broken.

Emotionally there can be a mild dip in mood, irritability or anxiety. People taking it for alcohol use disorder sometimes notice cravings returning as it fades.

After regular use, abrupt stopping is dangerous: withdrawal can cause hallucinations, confusion, high fever, rebound muscle rigidity and seizures, and with pump (intrathecal) baclofen it can be life-threatening. Any change should be made with a doctor.

As levels fall, sedation lifts and muscle tension may return. Short-acting drugs wear off within hours, while drugs with active metabolites (carisoprodol to meprobamate) linger.

People who use carisoprodol, meprobamate, or baclofen daily can develop dependence; stopping suddenly can cause anxiety, insomnia, hallucinations, and seizures, and abrupt baclofen withdrawal can be life-threatening.

### After-effects (8-24 h)

Weakness, drowsiness.

### Next day (little)

Usually little next day unless kidneys are impaired.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, muscle relaxants mainly cause drowsiness and eased tension. At higher doses, dizziness, heavy sedation, poor coordination, blurred vision, and confusion grow, and some (carisoprodol, meprobamate, orphenadrine, baclofen, tizanidine) bring additional dangers: seizures, heart rhythm problems, very low blood pressure, or coma. With opioids, alcohol, or benzodiazepines, breathing can slow and stop.

Higher amounts bring confusion, hallucinations, seizures and deep coma; withdrawal after daily use can be life-threatening.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, age, liver and kidney function, genetics, and especially other depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines) change the effect. Older adults and people with sleep apnea or lung disease are at higher risk. Stronger effects do not stop at more pleasure: they shade into blackouts, falls, and slowed breathing.

Not covered: Other routes are not used medically and are not covered.

## Overdose note for Baclofen (Lioresal)

Overdose causes deep coma, slowed breathing, seizures, slow heartbeat, and low body temperature; people with kidney disease are especially at risk because baclofen is cleared by the kidneys.
