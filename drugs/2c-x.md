# Other 2C drugs (2C-I, 2C-E, 2C-T-7, 2C-P and others)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic phenethylamine psychedelics.

**Brand and street names:** 2C-I, 2C-E, 2C-T-2, 2C-T-7 (blue mystic), 2C-P, 2C-C, 2C-D.

## History and origins

Most were described by Shulgin in PiHKAL. Several were sold online in the 2000s; 2C-T-7 was linked to deaths and scheduled in 2002, and others in 2012.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Sold as research chemicals or as LSD or ecstasy.

## Legal status

Most are US Schedule I.

## What the products look and feel like

Powders or pills. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

2C drugs differ a lot: 2C-E is intense and long, 2C-I more stimulating, 2C-P very long and strong, 2C-T-7 linked to deaths when snorted or mixed.

Many cause body discomfort, nausea and strong visuals.

Potency differences mean an amount that is mild with one can be overwhelming with another.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-90 min)

The 2C family (2C-I, 2C-E, 2C-T-2, 2C-T-7, 2C-C and others) varies widely, but many share a heavy 'body load' at onset: nausea, muscle tension, a tight jaw, goosebumps and a jittery, buzzing energy. The 2C family (2C-E, 2C-I, 2C-T-7, 2C-P and others) varies widely: some come on within an hour, others creep in over two or three, which tempts people to take more too early. Nausea, chills, yawning and a heavy, tense stomach are common as the body adjusts, and pupils widen.

Mentally the onset brings anticipation and a slowly building intensity, sometimes with anxiety before the visuals arrive. Mentally the onset may feel anxious and uncertain before it settles.

Effects, strength and duration differ greatly between compounds, and amounts that are mild for one can be overwhelming for another.

Absorption and onset vary by compound; 2C-P and 2C-E are slow.

Heart rate and blood pressure rise.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Visuals and body load build.

### Peak (2-4 h)

At the peak strong visuals and altered thinking take over. 2C-E is known for heavy, intense, sometimes grueling experiences; 2C-I for energetic, colorful visuals; 2C-T-7 for a long, bodily trip.

Emotionally the peak can be euphoric, insightful or difficult and confusing.

Some 2C compounds, especially 2C-T-7 and 2C-T-21, have been linked to deaths. Psychedelics rarely threaten the body directly, but they impair judgment and coordination, so falls, traffic, water and heights are real dangers. Panic, paranoia and terrifying experiences can happen to anyone, and people with a personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder face a higher risk of lasting harm.

At peak, strong 5-HT2A effects; seizures and overheating are reported with some compounds.

2C-T compounds inhibit MAO weakly, raising interaction risk.

### Plateau (3-6 h)

The plateau of 2C compounds lasts many hours depending on the specific drug, with strong visuals, a heavy or buzzing body and a sense that effects are firmly 'locked in'. 2C-E and 2C-P are known for a long, intense, sometimes grinding plateau with body load; 2C-I is more stimulating; 2C-T-7 carries more body discomfort and has been linked to deaths.

Mood can be euphoric, introspective or overwhelmed, and thinking may loop, with difficult themes surfacing. Communication can become hard, and people may feel trapped in the experience because the long duration is underestimated.

Overheating, rising blood pressure and agitation are risks at higher amounts, and combining with stimulants, MDMA or MAO inhibitors (with 2C-T compounds especially) is dangerous.

### Comedown (6-12 h)

The comedown is long and gradual, with residual visuals and stimulation lingering for hours. The body feels drained, achy and tense, sometimes with a headache, sore jaw or upset stomach. Faint color shifts and trails may still flicker when the eyes close, and the skin can feel prickly or oversensitive.

Mood may be calm and reflective or flat, anxious and irritable, especially after a grinding plateau. Many feel mentally worn out and want quiet and darkness, and some describe feeling emotionally raw for a day.

Sleep is often delayed into the night. Because doses differ greatly between 2C compounds and product identity is uncertain, misjudged amounts are a common cause of emergencies.

Durations vary widely, up to many hours.

Fatigue and insomnia follow.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Insomnia.

### Next day

Next-day fatigue.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
