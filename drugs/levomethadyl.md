# Levomethadyl acetate (LAAM)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic opioid related to methadone, with even longer action.

**Brand and street names:** ORLAAM.

## History and origins

Approved by the FDA in 1993 for opioid addiction treatment, allowing dosing three times a week. Withdrawn in the US in 2003 and Europe in 2001 after reports of fatal heart rhythm problems.

## Medical use, then and now

Formerly for opioid use disorder; no longer available.

## How it entered recreational use

Not commonly misused.

## Legal status

US Schedule II (not marketed).

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Like methadone, LAAM produced little euphoria at stable treatment doses, instead a steady, normal feeling lasting 2 to 3 days.

People who tried it described it as smooth and long, with slow onset.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (1-4 h)

Slow onset over hours.

### Come-up (4-8 h)

Gradual relief of withdrawal.

### Peak (8-12 h)

Mild, flat peak.

### Plateau (1-3 days)

Plateau of 48 to 72 hours.

### Comedown (2-3 days)

Very gradual fade.

### After-effects (3+ days)

Sweating, constipation.

### Next day (still active)

Still active for days.

Not covered: Oral only.

## Overdose note for Levomethadyl acetate (LAAM)

Fatal heart rhythm disturbances (QT prolongation); very long, delayed effect.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md).
