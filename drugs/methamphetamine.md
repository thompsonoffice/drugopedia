# Methamphetamine

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Potent dopamine releaser.

**Brand and street names:** Desoxyn (prescription); street: meth, crystal, ice, glass, tina, crank.

## History and origins

First made from ephedrine by Nagai Nagayoshi in 1893 and crystallized by Akira Ogata in 1919. It was given to soldiers in World War II (Pervitin in Germany, Philopon in Japan). Illicit meth spread in the US from the 1960s; today most comes from large-scale cartel labs and is very pure.

## Medical use, then and now

Desoxyn is approved for ADHD and formerly for obesity, but is rarely prescribed.

## How it entered recreational use

Smoked, snorted, swallowed or injected for euphoria and energy; a major cause of overdose deaths, often with fentanyl.

## Legal status

US Schedule II; UN 1971 Convention Schedule II.

## What the pills feel like

Crystal meth looks like clear or white glassy shards; it also appears pressed into counterfeit Adderall or 'ecstasy' pills. Stimulant drugs sold on the street, including counterfeit Adderall, are often contaminated with methamphetamine or fentanyl.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Methamphetamine's character is a powerful, long rush of euphoria, confidence, energy and sexual drive lasting many hours, far longer than cocaine.

People stay awake for days, stop eating and develop repetitive behaviors; paranoia and hallucinations, such as feeling bugs under the skin, are common with heavy use.

Long-term use damages teeth ('meth mouth'), heart, skin and brain dopamine systems.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (seconds)

Methamphetamine's onset brings a strong rush of energy: the heart pounds, the body warms, and a jittery, tingling alertness spreads through the limbs.

Mentally there is surging euphoria and confidence, with a sense of limitless energy and focus. People feel talkative, sexual and invincible.

Pupils widen, the mouth dries, the jaw clenches and appetite disappears.

Methamphetamine crosses into the brain more readily than amphetamine and releases dopamine powerfully while also blocking its breakdown.

Early signs include high blood pressure, fast heart rate, high temperature and dilated pupils.

### Come-up (minutes)

Intense euphoria and energy.

### Peak (15-60 min)

The peak is long and intense: the body feels hot, wired and restless, with sweating, fast breathing and a racing heart. Teeth grinding and repetitive movements are common.

The emotional tone is driven, euphoric focus that can become compulsive, such as cleaning or taking things apart for hours. Irritability and suspicion grow with longer use.

Overheating, heart rhythm problems, stroke and psychosis are serious risks. Street methamphetamine may contain fentanyl.

At peak, very high dopamine causes euphoria and, in heavy use, paranoia and hallucinations; severe toxicity causes hyperthermia, rhabdomyolysis, stroke and heart attack.

Methamphetamine-related heart failure (cardiomyopathy) and pulmonary hypertension are increasingly common in long-term users.

### Plateau (4-8 h)

The plateau lasts many hours, with sustained energy and no sleep. The body stays hot and dehydrated.

Mentally focus remains strong but judgment worsens; paranoia, hearing things, or feeling bugs on the skin can develop over long binges.

Skin picking and dental damage are linked to this phase.

### Comedown (8-12 h)

The comedown is a long crash: exhaustion, ravenous hunger and heavy sleep.

Emotionally depression, irritability and anxiety follow, sometimes for days.

Repeated use leads to dependence and lasting psychosis risk.

Half-life of about 10 to 12 hours keeps effects going long after the high.

Chronic use reduces dopamine transporters in the brain; partial recovery takes months to years of abstinence.

### After-effects (1-3 days)

Long sleep and extreme hunger.

### Next day (days)

Days of low mood and craving.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Methamphetamine

Meth overdose causes overheating, heart attack, stroke, seizures and psychosis; fentanyl contamination adds breathing failure.

Read the shared page: [Stimulant safety: overheating, heart risks, psychosis, fentanyl contamination](../safety/stimulant-safety.md).
