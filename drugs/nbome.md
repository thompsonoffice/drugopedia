# NBOMe drugs (25I-NBOMe, 25C-NBOMe, 25B-NBOMe)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Extremely potent synthetic phenethylamines.

**Brand and street names:** N-bomb, smiles, 25I; often sold as LSD.

## History and origins

Created by Ralf Heim in 2003 and studied by David Nichols' lab as research tools. Sold from about 2010 on blotters as 'legal acid'; linked to many deaths and controlled in 2013.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Usually taken by people who think they have LSD.

## Legal status

US Schedule I (2013); UN 1971 Schedule I (2015).

## What the products look and feel like

Usually blotter paper identical to LSD; a bitter or numbing taste suggests an NBOMe. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

NBOMes produce LSD-like visuals but with much more body stress: racing heart, vasoconstriction, agitation.

They are active at tiny amounts, so a blotter can contain a toxic quantity; unlike LSD, they can kill.

A bitter or numbing taste on blotter is a warning sign: 'If it's bitter, it's a spitter.'

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the sublingual and buccal (under the tongue or in the cheek) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-60 min)

NBOMe compounds (like 25I-NBOMe, often sold as fake LSD on blotter) often start with a bitter or numbing taste, which is a warning sign. The onset brings strong stimulation, tension and a racing heart.

Mentally an edgy, intense rise.

Body temperature may climb.

NBOMes are destroyed by first-pass metabolism if swallowed, so blotters are held in the mouth.

Vasoconstriction, fast heart rate and high blood pressure appear early.

### Come-up (1 h)

Visuals and stimulation.

### Peak (2-4 h)

At the peak intense visuals with a harsh, stimulating body load.

Emotionally can tip into confusion, paranoia and agitation.

NBOMes have caused many deaths from seizures, overheating, heart problems and violent agitation, even at tiny amounts. Any seizure, very high temperature or extreme agitation is an emergency.

At peak, seizures, overheating, agitation and arrhythmias can occur; some deaths involved violent trauma.

Rhabdomyolysis and kidney failure follow severe cases.

### Plateau (4-6 h)

The plateau is long and uncomfortable for many.

Thinking agitated.

Medical help may be needed.

### Comedown (6-10 h)

The comedown is draining.

Mood anxious.

Fentanyl test strips do not detect NBOMes.

Effects last several hours.

Survivors of severe poisoning may have lasting kidney or brain injury.

### After-effects (12-24 h)

Exhaustion.

### Next day

Next-day fatigue.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for NBOMe drugs (25I-NBOMe, 25C-NBOMe, 25B-NBOMe)

NBOMes cause seizures, overheating, heart attacks, kidney failure and death.

Read the shared page: [Psychedelic safety](../safety/psychedelic-safety.md).
