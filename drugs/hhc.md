# HHC (hexahydrocannabinol)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Hydrogenated cannabinoid made from hemp CBD.

**Brand and street names:** HHC, HHC-P, HHC-O.

## History and origins

HHC was first made by Roger Adams in 1944 by adding hydrogen to THC. It appeared in US and European markets in 2021 as a hemp product.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Marketed as a shelf-stable 'legal' THC alternative; it spread rapidly in Europe in 2022.

## Legal status

Banned in many EU countries from 2023, and controlled under the UN 1971 Convention (Schedule II) in 2025; status varies by US state.

## What the products look and feel like

Vapes and gummies. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

HHC feels close to ordinary THC, somewhat milder: relaxation, euphoria, appetite and time distortion.

Products contain a mix of two forms, only one strongly active, so strength varies.

Like delta-8, it is made by chemical reactions in unregulated labs, and newer variants (HHC-P) are much stronger.

Tolerance to the high builds quickly with daily use and fades after a break of a few weeks; people returning after a break, or switching to concentrates or edibles, are often surprised by how strong the effect is.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (minutes)

HHC (hexahydrocannabinol) is a hydrogenated form of THC made from hemp-derived chemicals and sold in vapes, gummies and flower in many countries. Inhaled it comes on within minutes; eaten, over an hour or more. Early effects include warmth, relaxation, dry mouth and red eyes. Some feel a light pressure behind the eyes and a gently racing pulse.

Mentally many describe it as similar to regular THC, somewhere between delta-8 and delta-9 in strength: gently euphoric, relaxed and slightly spacey.

Products vary a lot in strength and purity. Several European countries banned HHC after it spread quickly. Some products also contain other semi-synthetic cannabinoids.

HHC is made by adding hydrogen to THC-like molecules; one form (9R) binds CB1 strongly, the other (9S) weakly.

Body effects match THC: faster heart rate, dry mouth and red eyes.

### Come-up (5-15 min)

Relaxation builds.

### Peak (15-45 min)

At the peak the body is relaxed and heavy, with a faster heart and impaired coordination. Eyes become red and dry, the mouth sticky, and some people feel lightheaded on standing. Muscles loosen and the body feels warm and heavy; some notice a strong pulse.

Mentally there is euphoria, slowed time and heightened senses; anxiety and paranoia can occur at higher amounts. Some find it pleasantly mellow and social; others feel distracted and lose the thread of conversations. Time can seem to stretch.

Driving is impaired, and drug tests may detect it. Driving is impaired, and drug tests may detect it. Higher amounts or strong vapes can cause anxiety, dizziness and nausea, especially in new users.

Peak effects depend on the 9R-to-9S ratio, which varies by batch.

Newer variants like HHC-P are far more potent and have caused emergency visits in Europe.

### Plateau (about 1 h)

The plateau lasts a few hours. Appetite may increase and drowsiness grows. The body stays relaxed and slowed, with dry eyes and mouth and a mild pulse increase. Some people feel sleepy and lie down; edibles make the plateau longer and heavier. Red eyes, a sticky mouth and slight lightheadedness on standing often continue, and hunger may suddenly feel urgent.

Mentally thoughts drift and focus is poor. Thoughts drift, short-term memory is poor, and some people enjoy music or food more. Others feel foggy and want to rest.

Tolerance builds with frequent use. Tolerance builds with frequent use, and the same amount feels weaker over time. Mixing with alcohol often worsens dizziness and nausea.

### Comedown (2-3 h)

The comedown brings tiredness and dry mouth. The comedown brings tiredness, dry mouth and sometimes a mild headache. After edibles, grogginess can last into the next day. Eyes may feel dry and heavy, and sleep may be deep but less refreshing than usual, with few dreams.

Mentally mild fogginess may linger. Mild fogginess, low motivation and forgetfulness may linger for a few hours. Some feel calm, others a little flat. Food, water and a good night's sleep usually clear the remaining haze.

Regular users who stop may feel irritable and sleep poorly for a while. Regular users who stop may feel irritable, sleep poorly and have vivid dreams for a week or two. Cutting down gradually helps.

Effects fade like THC.

HHC metabolites may or may not appear on standard drug tests, but many tests detect them.

### After-effects (3-5 h)

Dry mouth and sleepiness.

### Next day (next morning)

Possible next-day fog.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cannabis brings relaxation, mild euphoria, giggliness, and heightened senses. At higher doses, time distortion, anxiety, paranoia, a racing heart, nausea, dizziness and, for some, hallucinations or panic appear. Very high doses, especially edibles, can cause hours of confusion, vomiting and frightening psychotic symptoms.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for HHC (hexahydrocannabinol)

Too much causes anxiety, vomiting, confusion and fast heart rate.
