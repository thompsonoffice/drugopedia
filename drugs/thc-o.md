# THC-O acetate

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Acetylated THC, a synthetic cannabinoid made from hemp-derived THC.

**Brand and street names:** THC-O, THCO, 'the psychedelic cannabinoid'.

## History and origins

THC acetate was studied by the US military in the 1950s and turned up in a few clandestine labs. It was marketed widely after 2020 as a 'legal' hemp product.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Marketed as stronger and 'more psychedelic' than THC.

## Legal status

The DEA stated in 2023 that THC-O is a Schedule I controlled substance because it does not occur naturally in hemp.

## What the products look and feel like

Usually vape cartridges or gummies. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

THC-O is a prodrug: the body removes the acetate group to release THC, so effects start late and feel heavy, sedating and longer-lasting.

Reports of 'psychedelic' effects are mostly anecdotal and probably reflect strength.

Vaping acetate compounds is a concern: heated vitamin E acetate caused EVALI lung injuries, and THC-O acetate can form ketene, a lung toxin, when heated.

Tolerance to the high builds quickly with daily use and fades after a break of a few weeks; people returning after a break, or switching to concentrates or edibles, are often surprised by how strong the effect is.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (20-60 min)

THC-O acetate is a semi-synthetic THC compound made from hemp-derived chemicals and sold online and in shops as vapes and gummies. It is marketed as stronger and more 'psychedelic' than THC. Eaten, it can take a long time to begin; inhaled, it comes on faster. Early effects include heaviness, dry mouth, red eyes and a warm tingle.

Mentally the onset can be slow to recognize and then strong, with euphoria, dreaminess and distorted time; some describe mild visual changes.

Its strength and slow onset make accidental overconsumption likely. Heating acetate compounds in vapes may create a harmful gas (ketene) that can damage the lungs, a concern also linked to vitamin E acetate in vaping lung injury.

THC-O itself is thought to be inactive until liver and blood enzymes remove the acetate group to release delta-8 or delta-9 THC, which explains the delay.

The delay leads people to take more before effects begin.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

A gradual, heavy relaxation.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At the peak the body feels very heavy and sedated, with a fast heart, dizziness and sometimes nausea or vomiting. Some feel chills, a racing pulse and an urge to lie still, and coordination becomes very poor. Some people feel numb or tingly.

Mentally there can be intense euphoria, deep relaxation or strong anxiety, paranoia and confusion, similar to a very strong THC edible. Some report a dreamlike, slightly psychedelic feel, with vivid inner imagery and altered sense of space, but many simply feel very high and sleepy.

Regulation is weak and products are often untested; the US DEA has stated THC-O is not legal hemp. Calm surroundings, water and time help; chest pain, fainting or severe vomiting need medical care.

At the peak, effects are those of the released THC, which can be strong because acetylated products are often concentrated.

When vaped, acetate compounds can break down into ketene, a gas toxic to the lungs.

### Plateau (2-3 h)

The plateau is long. The body stays sedated and appetite may increase. Heaviness and sleepiness continue, with a dry mouth and red eyes. Some people feel queasy and dizzy, especially if they took more while waiting for the onset. Lying down helps. Some feel their heart pounding when they move, and limbs feel numb or rubbery.

Mentally thinking stays slow and memory poor. Concentration is very limited, and some people feel stuck in an extended dreamy state that seems to last forever. Anxiety may rise and fall in waves.

Driving is unsafe for many hours. Products are often untested, and contents may differ from labels. Waiting fully before taking more is the key safeguard, since the slow onset fools many people.

### Comedown (3-5 h)

The comedown can include grogginess and a heavy head into the next day. Many people wake feeling slow, thirsty and heavy-limbed, with a mild headache, especially after gummies. Appetite may stay high. Eyes may stay dry and puffy.

Mentally fogginess and low motivation may linger. Thinking can remain sluggish for much of the next day, and some feel a little low or anxious after an overly strong experience. Rest and food help. Most people feel normal by the following evening.

Regular use builds tolerance and dependence like other THC products. Lung symptoms such as cough or shortness of breath after vaping should be checked by a doctor. Children must never have access to these products.

Effects last longer than ordinary THC because conversion continues over time.

Lung symptoms (cough, chest pain, shortness of breath) after vaping need medical attention.

### After-effects (5-8 h)

Grogginess.

### Next day

Next-day fog.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cannabis brings relaxation, mild euphoria, giggliness, and heightened senses. At higher doses, time distortion, anxiety, paranoia, a racing heart, nausea, dizziness and, for some, hallucinations or panic appear. Very high doses, especially edibles, can cause hours of confusion, vomiting and frightening psychotic symptoms.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for THC-O acetate

Too much causes long-lasting confusion, vomiting and anxiety; vaping may injure the lungs.
