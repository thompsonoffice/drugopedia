# Ayahuasca

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Amazonian brew combining DMT-containing plants with MAO-inhibiting Banisteriopsis caapi vine.

**Brand and street names:** Ayahuasca, yagé, hoasca, la medicina; 'pharmahuasca' (pharmaceutical analogues).

## History and origins

Used for centuries by Indigenous peoples of the Amazon basin. Brazilian churches (Santo Daime, União do Vegetal) use it as a sacrament; in 2006 the US Supreme Court protected UDV's religious use (Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita). 'Ayahuasca tourism' grew in Peru from the 2000s.

## Medical use, then and now

No approved use; studied for depression and addiction.

## How it entered recreational use

Taken in ceremonial retreats, increasingly by tourists.

## Legal status

DMT is US Schedule I; specific churches have religious exemptions. Legal or tolerated in Peru, Brazil and some other countries.

## What the products look and feel like

A thick brown liquid with a bitter taste.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Ayahuasca's character is long, emotional and visionary: vivid inner imagery, strong emotional release, and often vomiting, which participants call 'purging'.

The MAO inhibitors in the vine make swallowed DMT active and also block breakdown of other substances, so it can cause dangerous interactions with antidepressants, stimulants, and some foods.

Deaths at retreats have involved heart problems, drug interactions, other plants added to the brew (such as toé/brugmansia), and violence or neglect.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Ayahuasca, a brew of a DMT-containing leaf (usually chacruna) and the MAO-inhibiting Banisteriopsis caapi vine, comes on slowly over the first hour. The brew itself is thick, bitter and earthy. As it takes hold, the stomach grows uneasy and heavy, the body warms or chills, and a buzzing vibration may rise through the limbs. In the dark, faint colors and shifting patterns begin to appear behind the eyelids.

Mentally the onset often brings anticipation and anxiety, then a feeling of being drawn inward and downward. Memories, faces and emotions surface with unusual vividness, sometimes unbidden, and sounds such as singing (icaros) or rain can seem to shape the visions.

Nausea builds steadily and vomiting ('the purge') is common; in traditional settings it is considered cleansing and part of the ceremony. Diarrhea, sweating, yawning, shivering and crying can come in waves.

Harmala alkaloids inhibit MAO-A in the gut and liver within minutes, allowing DMT to reach the bloodstream.

Nausea, vomiting and diarrhea are common as both serotonin and harmala alkaloids act on the gut.

### Come-up (1 h)

Visions and emotional waves build; vomiting is common.

### Peak (1.5-2.5 h)

At the peak, closed-eye visions can be cinematic: jungles, serpents, animals, ancestors, and scenes from the person's own life. Body sensations surge in waves of heat, cold, tingling or heaviness.

Emotionally the peak can be cathartic and confronting: crying, grief, fear, forgiveness and deep love are all common. Many describe it as a long, intense psychological journey rather than a pleasurable high.

The MAO inhibitor in the brew makes interactions dangerous: with SSRIs and other serotonergic drugs it can cause serotonin syndrome, and with stimulants or certain foods it can spike blood pressure. Psychedelics rarely threaten the body directly, but they impair judgment and coordination, so falls, traffic, water and heights are real dangers. Panic, paranoia and terrifying experiences can happen to anyone, and people with a personal or family history of psychosis or bipolar disorder face a higher risk of lasting harm.

At peak, DMT's 5-HT2A effects combine with harmaline's own effects, producing long visionary states.

MAO inhibition can cause serotonin syndrome with SSRIs, SNRIs, MDMA, tramadol, DXM and others, and hypertensive crisis with stimulants or tyramine-rich foods.

### Plateau (2-3 h)

The plateau is a long stretch, often hours, of rolling visions and emotional processing. Visions may come in waves, separated by calmer periods, and nausea or purging may return intermittently. The body can feel heavy and unable to move or charged with energy.

Thinking moves between moments of striking insight and confusion. People often revisit relationships, trauma or regrets, sometimes experiencing them as if from the outside, and may feel overwhelming forgiveness or grief.

People are vulnerable in this state and need a safe setting and trustworthy, experienced people around them. Sexual abuse by so-called shamans in unregulated retreats has been widely reported.

### Comedown (3-6 h)

The comedown is gradual, leaving deep tiredness, an empty stomach, aching muscles from purging, and sometimes a lingering shimmer when the eyes close. Sleep usually comes eventually.

Emotionally people often feel cleansed, tender and reflective, sometimes euphoric for days. Others feel shaken, raw or disturbed by what surfaced, and integration support helps.

Deaths have been reported, usually involving drug interactions (especially SSRIs and stimulants), extra plants or substances added to the brew, or pre-existing heart problems. People are often advised to avoid certain foods and medicines before and after.

Effects fade as DMT is cleared, but MAO inhibition lasts longer.

People on antidepressants are at the highest risk; medical advice is essential.

### After-effects (6-12 h)

Tiredness and introspection.

### Next day

Next-day fatigue.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Ayahuasca

Serotonin syndrome with SSRIs or other drugs, high blood pressure, and dehydration from vomiting are the main dangers.
