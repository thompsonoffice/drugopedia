# Ayahuasca

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Amazonian brew combining DMT-containing plants with MAO-inhibiting Banisteriopsis caapi vine.

**Brand and street names:** Ayahuasca, yagé, hoasca, la medicina; 'pharmahuasca' (pharmaceutical analogues).

## History and origins

Used for centuries by Indigenous peoples of the Amazon basin. Brazilian churches (Santo Daime, União do Vegetal) use it as a sacrament; in 2006 the US Supreme Court protected UDV's religious use (Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita). 'Ayahuasca tourism' grew in Peru from the 2000s.

## Medical use, then and now

No approved use; studied for depression and addiction.

## How it entered recreational use

Taken in ceremonial retreats, increasingly by tourists.

## Legal status

DMT is US Schedule I; specific churches have religious exemptions. Legal or tolerated in Peru, Brazil and some other countries.

## What the products look and feel like

A thick brown liquid with a bitter taste.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Ayahuasca's character is long, emotional and visionary: vivid inner imagery, strong emotional release, and often vomiting, which participants call 'purging'.

The MAO inhibitors in the vine make swallowed DMT active and also block breakdown of other substances, so it can cause dangerous interactions with antidepressants, stimulants, and some foods.

Deaths at retreats have involved heart problems, drug interactions, other plants added to the brew (such as toé/brugmansia), and violence or neglect.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Effects begin 30 to 60 minutes after drinking, with nausea.

Harmala alkaloids inhibit MAO-A in the gut and liver within minutes, allowing DMT to reach the bloodstream.

Nausea, vomiting and diarrhea are common as both serotonin and harmala alkaloids act on the gut.

### Come-up (1 h)

Visions and emotional waves build; vomiting is common.

### Peak (1.5-2.5 h)

The peak at 1.5 to 2.5 hours brings intense imagery.

At peak, DMT's 5-HT2A effects combine with harmaline's own effects, producing long visionary states.

MAO inhibition can cause serotonin syndrome with SSRIs, SNRIs, MDMA, tramadol, DXM and others, and hypertensive crisis with stimulants or tyramine-rich foods.

### Plateau (2-3 h)

Plateau about 1 to 2 hours.

### Comedown (3-6 h)

Taper over 1 to 2 hours.

Effects fade as DMT is cleared, but MAO inhibition lasts longer.

People on antidepressants are at the highest risk; medical advice is essential.

### After-effects (6-12 h)

Tiredness and introspection.

### Next day

Next-day fatigue.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Ayahuasca

Serotonin syndrome with SSRIs or other drugs, high blood pressure, and dehydration from vomiting are the main dangers.
