# Dihydrocodeine

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Semi-synthetic opioid close to codeine, somewhat stronger.

**Brand and street names:** DF118, DHC Continus (UK), Synalgos-DC and Trezix (US, with caffeine and acetaminophen), Paracodin.

## History and origins

Developed in Germany in 1908 and marketed from 1911. Widely used in the UK and Europe.

## Medical use, then and now

Moderate pain and cough. In Germany and Austria it was used in the 1990s as an informal substitution treatment for heroin addiction.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused like codeine, especially in Europe.

## Legal status

US Schedule II (Schedule III in combinations). UK Class B.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Dihydrocodeine feels much like codeine but a little stronger and more consistent, with gentle warmth, mild euphoria, sleepiness, and notable itch.

Some describe a slightly more 'pins and needles' itchy body feeling than codeine.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Effects start 30 to 45 minutes after swallowing.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Warmth and relaxation build gently.

### Peak (1-2 h)

Mild to moderate euphoria peaks around 1 to 2 hours.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

Drowsy calm for a few hours.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

Fades over 4 to 6 hours.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Constipation and itch.

### Next day

Mostly normal next day.

## Overdose note for Dihydrocodeine

Similar to codeine; combination products add acetaminophen risk.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). Also see [naloxone (Narcan)](../narcan/naloxone.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
