# Mephedrone (4-MMC)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic cathinone releasing dopamine and serotonin.

**Brand and street names:** Meow meow, M-CAT, drone, bubble.

## History and origins

First described in 1929, it became popular in the UK from about 2007 as a legal high and was banned there in 2010.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Popular in clubs as an MDMA-like stimulant; injected in some chemsex scenes.

## Legal status

US Schedule I (2012); UN 1971 Schedule II (2015).

## What the products look and feel like

White or off-white powder or crystals, sometimes with a fishy smell. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Mephedrone feels like a mix of MDMA and cocaine: euphoria, empathy, talkativeness and energy, but short-lived, with a strong urge to redose.

Users report sweating, a pounding heart, jaw clenching and cold or bluish fingers from narrowed blood vessels.

Heavy use causes paranoia and insomnia, and injecting causes abscesses and infections.

Tolerance and compulsive redosing develop quickly with cathinones, and binges are common.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (10-20 min)

Mephedrone (4-MMC, 'meow meow') is a synthetic cathinone that was legally sold in the UK until 2010. Swallowed it builds over a short while; snorted it hits within minutes. The body warms, the heart speeds up, pupils widen and a tingling rush runs up the spine and scalp, often with jaw tension and teeth grinding.

Mentally the onset is a surging euphoria that people compare to a blend of MDMA and cocaine: an upwelling of affection, talkativeness and confidence, music sounding richer and touch feeling good.

The rush fades fast, and the urge to redose arrives early and strongly, which is the defining trap of mephedrone. Cold or bluish fingers and toes can appear because it tightens blood vessels.

Mephedrone is absorbed fast and releases both serotonin and dopamine, like a mix of MDMA and cocaine.

Vasoconstriction causes cold or bluish fingers; heart rate rises.

### Come-up (15-30 min)

A fast rise of euphoria.

### Peak (30-60 min)

At its peak the body feels energetic and hot, with sweating, a pounding pulse, clenched jaw, restlessness and loss of appetite. Some notice a distinctive body odor and blotchy skin. Fingers and toes may feel cold or look bluish, and the heartbeat can be felt in the throat.

Mentally the peak is warm and sociable: people feel open, close to others and deeply interested in conversation, with a stimulating clarity. It is shallower and shorter than MDMA, and there is often a background nervousness.

Overheating, a racing or irregular heartbeat, agitation and seizures are the main dangers, and they rise with redosing and with alcohol or other stimulants.

At peak, overheating, agitation and arrhythmias are the main dangers.

Rapid redosing raises toxicity.

### Plateau (1-2 h)

The plateau is short and choppy, usually an hour or less per dose, so a session becomes a cycle of redosing. The body stays wired, dehydrated and tense, with tightened blood vessels, nosebleeds if snorted and difficulty urinating. Many describe a 'wired but empty' body: buzzing nerves, a dry, sore nose and tight muscles in the neck and back.

Mentally each wave brings less euphoria and more anxiety, restlessness and fixation on taking more. Some people become paranoid, agitated or confused, especially after a long night. Conversation that felt profound an hour ago starts to loop, and people may stay up long past the point of enjoying it.

Large or repeated amounts have caused deaths from overheating and heart problems; anyone hot, confused or with chest pain needs emergency care.

### Comedown (2-4 h)

The comedown brings exhaustion, aching muscles, a sore jaw, poor sleep and sometimes chest tightness or a racing heart that keeps people awake. Appetite returns slowly, and the nose and throat may be raw for days if it was snorted.

Emotionally it is often harsh: low mood, anxiety, irritability and intense craving, sometimes for days. Memory and concentration feel fuzzy. People often describe feeling emotionally hollowed out, as if the warmth of the night has been repaid with interest.

Frequent use produces strong compulsive use and dependence; support services and talking therapies can help, and people should avoid mixing it with other stimulants or alcohol.

Half-life is short (about 2 hours).

Comedown includes depression and craving.

### After-effects (4-12 h)

Anxiety and insomnia.

### Next day

Low mood for a day or two.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses cathinones bring energy, euphoria and sociability. At higher doses, agitation, a racing heart, sweating, paranoia and a strong urge to redose dominate. At very high levels, psychosis, overheating, seizures, muscle breakdown and heart failure can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.
