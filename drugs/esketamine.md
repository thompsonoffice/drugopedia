# Esketamine (Spravato)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

The S-enantiomer of ketamine, given as a nasal spray for depression.

**Brand and street names:** Spravato.

## History and origins

Approved by the FDA in 2019 for treatment-resistant depression and in 2020 for depression with suicidal thoughts.

## Medical use, then and now

Given only in certified clinics under a restricted program (REMS), with monitoring for at least two hours afterward.

## How it entered recreational use

Not dispensed for home use, which limits misuse.

## Legal status

US Schedule III.

## What the products look and feel like

Spravato is a single-use nasal spray device kept at the clinic.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Patients describe mild-to-moderate dissociation: floaty, distant, with altered colors and sounds, starting soon after the spray.

Blood pressure rises, and nausea and dizziness are common, which is why patients are observed.

Patients must not drive until the next day after a restful sleep.

Tolerance to dissociatives builds quickly, and ketamine tolerance leads people to use more, raising the risk of bladder and memory damage.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the intranasal (nasal sprays and snorting) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (minutes)

Esketamine (Spravato) is a nasal spray made of one mirror-image half of ketamine, approved for treatment-resistant depression and depression with suicidal thoughts. It is given only in a certified clinic, under observation. Within minutes, many people feel a light, floating tingle, numbness in the mouth and a mild heaviness in the arms and legs.

Mentally the onset can feel dreamy and detached: sounds may seem distant or echoing, time stretches, and some people feel slightly unreal or separated from their body. For others it is barely noticeable beyond drowsiness.

Blood pressure often rises for an hour or two, which is checked in the clinic. Nausea, dizziness and an odd taste are common.

Esketamine is absorbed through the nasal lining, with peak levels within about 20 to 40 minutes.

Blood pressure rises in many patients, peaking around 40 minutes, so it is checked before and after.

### Come-up (10-20 min)

Dissociation builds over 15 to 30 minutes.

### Peak (30-40 min)

At its peak, roughly within the first hour, the body may feel heavy or floaty, coordination is impaired, and some people feel numb, sleepy or nauseated. Patients are kept seated or reclining and are not allowed to drive afterward. Some feel their speech slow and their mouth or tongue go numb.

Mentally there can be marked dissociation: feeling outside one's body, vivid daydreams, distorted sense of time or space, and sometimes anxiety. Many people find it strange but tolerable; some find it pleasant or emotionally meaningful.

Sedation, raised blood pressure and dissociation are why it is restricted. Misuse potential exists, which is why take-home use is not allowed.

At peak, dissociation, dizziness and nausea are common; sedation is monitored.

Antidepressant effects are thought to come from glutamate surges and new synaptic connections after the drug wears off.

### Plateau (40-60 min)

The effects wind down over an hour or two in the clinic. Dizziness, drowsiness and mild nausea may linger, and the body slowly regains steadiness. Some still feel slightly drunk or wobbly when standing, and nausea or a mild headache may appear as the dissociation lifts.

Mentally the dissociation fades into a quiet, tired calm. Some people notice mood relief within hours to days, part of what makes esketamine valuable in severe depression. Thoughts may feel softer and less heavy, and some patients describe emotional memories surfacing gently. Others simply feel sleepy.

Staff check blood pressure and alertness before release. A ride home is required. Patients usually rest, listen to music or sit quietly while staff monitor them.

### Comedown (1-2 h)

Afterward many people feel tired and may want to sleep, with occasional headache. Some feel slightly foggy or tired into the evening, and a good night's sleep usually clears it.

Mentally there may be a lifting of depression over the following days, though benefits usually need repeated sessions and continuing other treatment. Some feel more hopeful or less weighed down by thoughts; others notice little at first. Suicidal thoughts should always be reported to the care team.

Bladder problems and dependence, seen in heavy recreational ketamine use, are a concern but appear uncommon with supervised medical dosing. Doctors also monitor for misuse and for rises in blood pressure over a treatment course.

Most acute effects resolve within two hours.

Bladder symptoms and misuse potential are monitored under the REMS program.

### After-effects (2-4 h)

Tiredness.

### Next day

No driving until the next day.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses dissociatives bring numbness, lightness, mild euphoria and dreamy detachment. At higher doses, the body feels far away, movement becomes difficult, and vision and sound distort. At still higher levels, people lose contact with surroundings entirely, cannot move or respond, and are at risk of injury, vomiting and choking, with breathing danger when combined with depressants.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Esketamine (Spravato)

Under monitoring, serious harm is rare; risks include sedation, high blood pressure and dissociation.
