# Non-beverage alcohols (methanol, isopropyl, hand sanitizer, mouthwash)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Toxic alcohols and alcohol-containing products not meant for drinking.

**Brand and street names:** Methanol (wood alcohol), isopropyl alcohol (rubbing alcohol), hand sanitizer, mouthwash, 'moonshine', counterfeit spirits.

## History and origins

Methanol poisoning outbreaks from bootleg or counterfeit spirits have killed thousands worldwide, including during US Prohibition and in recent outbreaks in Asia, Africa and Latin America. In 2020 the FDA recalled hand sanitizers contaminated with methanol.

## Medical use, then and now

Fomepizole and dialysis treat toxic alcohol poisoning.

## How it entered recreational use

Swallowed by people with alcohol dependence without access to drinks, by children accidentally, and in counterfeit spirits.

## Legal status

Legal products; methanol-contaminated sanitizers were recalled.

## What the products look and feel like

Hand sanitizers, rubbing alcohol bottles, and unlabeled spirits. Check FDA recall lists.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Methanol at first feels like ordinary drunkenness, but hours later its breakdown products cause severe acid build-up, blindness and death.

Isopropyl alcohol is about twice as intoxicating as ethanol and causes severe stomach irritation and vomiting.

Children who swallow hand sanitizer or mouthwash can develop dangerously low blood sugar and unconsciousness.

Tolerance hides impairment but not organ damage; daily drinking leads to dependence, and stopping suddenly can cause seizures and delirium tremens.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Non-beverage alcohol means alcohol-containing products not meant to be drunk: mouthwash, hand sanitizer, rubbing alcohol (isopropyl), cooking extracts, cologne, and methylated or denatured spirits. They are usually drunk when beverage alcohol is unaffordable or unavailable. Ethanol-based products hit like strong liquor, with a burning throat, warm stomach and spreading flush; isopropyl causes a harsher burn, nausea and stomach pain.

Mentally ethanol products bring the familiar loosening of drunkenness, but often quickly and heavily because of their high strength. Isopropyl brings a heavier, more sedating intoxication, roughly twice as intoxicating as ethanol, with confusion and dizziness.

Many products contain other toxins. Methanol, found in some windshield fluid, fuel and illegally made spirits, can cause blindness and death, often after a delay of many hours in which the person seems only drunk. Mass methanol poisonings happen worldwide.

Methanol and isopropyl alcohol are absorbed quickly from the gut.

Early intoxication resembles ethanol, giving false reassurance.

### Come-up (1 h)

Drunkenness similar to ethanol.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At the peak the body is clumsy and heavy, with slurred speech, poor balance, nausea and vomiting. Isopropyl causes stomach bleeding, a fruity smell on the breath, and low blood pressure. Methanol poisoning brings headache, abdominal pain and rapid breathing hours later.

Mentally there is heavy drunkenness: confusion, poor judgment, memory blackouts and mood swings. Methanol poisoning adds blurred or 'snowstorm' vision and confusion that worsens rather than improves.

Breathing can slow and stop, people can choke on vomit, and methanol or ethylene glycol cause severe acid buildup, blindness, kidney failure and death. Emergency treatment, including antidotes like fomepizole and dialysis, can save sight and life if started early.

Methanol's metabolite formic acid causes severe metabolic acidosis and damages the optic nerve, causing blurry 'snowfield' vision and blindness.

Isopropyl alcohol causes deep sedation, low blood pressure and stomach bleeding.

### Plateau (2-6 h)

Because these products are so strong, people may stay heavily intoxicated for many hours, with repeated vomiting, falls and stupor. Sanitizer gels and mouthwashes can also irritate the stomach and throat. Breath may smell of mint, perfume or chemicals, and the skin may be cold and clammy.

Mentally the plateau is a fog of confusion, memory loss and fluctuating mood. Some people become withdrawn and drowsy, others agitated. Some people drink continuously to hold off withdrawal shakes and anxiety.

Any vision changes, severe headache, fast breathing or worsening confusion after drinking unknown alcohol or industrial products needs emergency care immediately, even if the person seemed fine at first. Outreach workers and shelters can call for help quickly.

### Comedown (12-24 h)

The comedown is a severe hangover: headache, nausea, vomiting, stomach pain, shakiness and dehydration. After isopropyl, stomach irritation and weakness can persist. Hands may shake and sweat may pour as the alcohol leaves the body.

Mentally people feel foggy, low, anxious and ashamed, often with blackouts of the night before. Craving for more alcohol to relieve withdrawal is common in people with alcohol dependence.

People drinking non-beverage alcohol are often severely dependent and may face dangerous withdrawal, including seizures and delirium tremens, if they stop suddenly. Medically supported withdrawal, housing support and harm-reduction services save lives. Methanol victims may need eye checks.

Fomepizole blocks alcohol dehydrogenase, preventing methanol's toxic breakdown; dialysis removes it.

Survivors of methanol poisoning may have permanent blindness or Parkinson-like brain damage.

### After-effects (days)

Permanent blindness or brain damage possible.

### Next day (permanent harm possible)

Requires hospital care.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower amounts alcohol brings relaxation and talkativeness. At higher amounts, slurred speech, poor balance, poor judgment and memory blackouts follow. At very high levels, vomiting, unconsciousness, slowed breathing and death from alcohol poisoning occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, sex (women reach higher blood alcohol from the same drinks), food, speed of drinking, tolerance, genetics (for example, ALDH2 variants common in East Asian people cause flushing), age, and medicines all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Non-beverage alcohols (methanol, isopropyl, hand sanitizer, mouthwash)

Methanol poisoning causes blindness, brain damage and death; anyone who may have drunk methanol needs emergency care even if they feel fine.
