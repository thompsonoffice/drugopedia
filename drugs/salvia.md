# Salvia divinorum

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Mint-family plant with salvinorin A, a kappa-opioid agonist.

**Brand and street names:** Salvia, diviner's sage, Sally-D, ska María pastora.

## History and origins

Used by Mazatec healers in Oaxaca. Salvinorin A was identified in 1982 and its kappa-opioid action in 2002. It became popular on YouTube in the 2000s.

## Medical use, then and now

None.

## How it entered recreational use

Smoked for brief, intense dissociation.

## Legal status

Not federally controlled; banned in many US states and countries.

## What the products look and feel like

Dried leaves or extracts.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Salvia causes a sudden, short, often disturbing dissociation, with distorted reality and loss of body awareness.

People may laugh uncontrollably or move unknowingly, risking falls.

It is not a classic psychedelic; it acts on kappa-opioid receptors.

Tolerance and dependence vary by substance.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (seconds)

Salvia divinorum hits within seconds when smoked: a pulling or twisting pressure in the body and a sudden shift of reality.

Mentally a bizarre, overwhelming dissociation, often feeling like becoming an object or being pulled into another world.

People may move or speak without awareness.

Salvinorin A reaches the brain within seconds.

Heart rate changes are mild.

### Come-up (under 1 min)

Rapid loss of contact.

### Peak (1-5 min)

The peak is brief and intense.

Emotionally confusing, often frightening.

Accidental injuries are the main danger; a sober sitter is essential.

At peak, kappa-opioid activation causes dissociation and dysphoria.

Accidents are the main risk.

### Plateau (5-10 min)

Effects fade within minutes.

Mind returns slowly.

Laughter or confusion follows.

### Comedown (15-30 min)

The comedown is quick.

Mood unsettled.

It is not addictive.

Rapid metabolism ends effects quickly.

Some feel uneasy afterward.

### After-effects (hours)

Afterglow or unease.

### Next day (none)

Usually none.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses effects are mild; at higher doses side effects and toxicity dominate, as described for each substance.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
