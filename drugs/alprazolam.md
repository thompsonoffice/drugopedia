# Alprazolam (Xanax)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Never stop a daily benzodiazepine suddenly: withdrawal can cause seizures. Counterfeit pills often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines.

## At a glance

Triazolobenzodiazepine; short-acting, high-potency.

**Brand and street names:** Xanax, Xanax XR, Niravam (orally disintegrating), Alprazolam Intensol. Street: 'bars', 'zannies', 'footballs', 'xannies', 'planks'.

## History and origins

Developed by Upjohn (Jackson Hester) in the late 1960s, alprazolam was approved by the FDA in 1981 for anxiety and became the first drug approved for panic disorder. Its fast action made it one of the most prescribed psychiatric drugs in the US. 'Bars' became part of party culture and hip-hop in the 2000s and 2010s. Many street 'Xanax' bars today are counterfeits containing bromazolam, other designer benzos, or fentanyl.

## Medical use, then and now

Anxiety and panic disorder.

## How it entered recreational use

Widely misused for calm and disinhibition, often combined with alcohol or opioids.

## Legal status

US Schedule IV. UK Class C (not prescribable on the NHS).

## What the pills feel like

Xanax and generic alprazolam come as small oval tablets in lower strengths and as long, rectangular 'bars' scored into four sections in the highest immediate-release strength; bars feel like a thin stick with three grooves. Extended-release tablets are triangular or pentagonal. Orally disintegrating tablets crumble on the tongue.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Xanax is described as a fast, clean wave of calm: the 'volume knob' on anxiety turned down, chest tightness gone, racing thoughts slowed. Recreationally, people describe a warm, carefree, disinhibited state, compared to being pleasantly drunk without nausea. It is less sleepy than many benzos at first but more amnesic.

Its hallmark is blackout: people act fairly normal to others but later remember nothing, sometimes doing risky or out-of-character things. Because it is short-acting, the comedown brings rebound anxiety and irritability, which drives repeated use.

Tolerance to the calming and sleep effects of benzodiazepines builds within weeks, while memory loss and the danger of mixing with alcohol or opioids remain. People taking them daily often feel little except relief from anxiety, and stopping suddenly can cause seizures.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-30 min)

Alprazolam usually begins within 15 to 30 minutes of swallowing: a softening of tension, shoulders dropping, and breathing that feels easier.

From a medical point of view, the onset reflects how quickly the drug is absorbed and enters the brain. Alprazolam is metabolized by CYP3A4; half-life about 11 hours, and drugs that block CYP3A4 (such as some antifungals and HIV medicines) raise its levels. As it binds to GABA-A receptors, brain activity slows: anxiety eases, muscles relax, and reaction time lengthens.

Clinicians notice early signs such as slower speech, drooping eyelids, and reduced coordination. People often underestimate how impaired they already are at this stage, which is why driving is dangerous.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Within the next 15 to 30 minutes, calm deepens and worries lose their grip. People feel loose, talkative, and unconcerned. Coordination slips.

### Peak (1-2 h)

The peak, around 1 to 2 hours, brings strong relaxation, mild euphoria, and disinhibition. Speech can slur, balance worsens, and memory begins to drop out.

Medically, the peak is when sedation, muscle relaxation, and memory impairment are strongest. Benzodiazepines can block the formation of new memories (anterograde amnesia), so people may act normally yet remember nothing later.

On their own, benzodiazepines rarely stop breathing in healthy adults, but combined with opioids or alcohol they greatly deepen respiratory depression. The FDA added its strongest boxed warning about this combination in 2016.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The plateau is a few hours of calm, sleepy, and sometimes foggy state.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

Effects fade over 4 to 6 hours for immediate-release. Rebound anxiety, irritability, and low mood can arrive sharply.

As levels fall, the calming effect wears off. With short-acting benzodiazepines, rebound anxiety and insomnia can be sharp. Long-acting ones and their active metabolites can linger for days, causing next-day drowsiness and impaired driving.

In people who take them daily, falling levels can trigger withdrawal, which in severe cases includes seizures and delirium. This is why stopping should be a slow medical taper, not a sudden stop.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

After-effects include grogginess, memory gaps, and sometimes a 'benzo hangover'.

### Next day

After regular use, interdose withdrawal is common: anxiety between doses. Stopping suddenly after daily use can cause seizures.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance builds quickly, liver function and age change how fast benzodiazepines are cleared (older adults clear many much more slowly), and many medicines block the CYP3A4 enzyme that breaks down alprazolam, midazolam, and triazolam. Alcohol and opioids multiply the sedation and breathing risk. Stronger effects shade into blackouts, falls, and, in combinations, stopped breathing.

Not covered: No injected, rectal, transdermal, or smoked medical forms.

## Overdose note for Alprazolam (Xanax)

Alone, alprazolam overdose causes deep sedation and confusion. With opioids or alcohol, it is a leading contributor to overdose deaths. Withdrawal seizures are a particular risk because it is short-acting and potent. Counterfeit 'bars' may contain fentanyl.

Read the shared page: [Benzodiazepine safety: overdose, mixing, blackouts, and withdrawal](../safety/benzodiazepine-safety.md).
