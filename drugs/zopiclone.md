# Zopiclone (Imovane)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## At a glance

Z-drug hypnotic (cyclopyrrolone).

**Brand and street names:** Imovane, Zimovane, Zopiclone generics (not sold in the US).

## History and origins

Introduced by Rhône-Poulenc in 1986; common in the UK, Canada, Australia and Europe.

## Medical use, then and now

Short-term insomnia.

## How it entered recreational use

Misuse occurs, especially combined with alcohol or opioids.

## Legal status

UK Class C; not approved in the US.

## What the pills feel like

Tablets are small, round, and film-coated.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Zopiclone's character is heavy sleepiness and a bitter taste.

It can cause amnesia and sleep behaviors.

It features in many overdose deaths in the UK when combined with other drugs.

Tolerance to the pleasant and sleep effects builds with regular use, but tolerance to slowed breathing builds more slowly, so the danger stays. Daily use can lead to dependence, and stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 30 min)

Zopiclone (Imovane, Zimovane), widely used outside the US, shares eszopiclone's bitter, metallic taste, which often lingers into the morning. The onset is a heavy, warm sleepiness that spreads from the head downward, with loosening muscles, heavy eyelids and a light, drunken unsteadiness when walking.

Mentally a dreamy calm settles in, with thoughts slowing and worries fading. If sleep is resisted, it can turn into a drunken giddiness with loosened inhibitions, silly talk and visual distortions, like patterns sliding on walls or objects seeming to move.

Coordination and memory fade early, sometimes before the person feels very sedated, so the last part of the evening may be lost.

Medically, the onset reflects absorption and entry into the brain. Zopiclone has a half-life of about 5 hours. Most hypnotics are designed to work quickly so the person falls asleep soon after taking them.

Clinicians advise taking hypnotics only right before bed, because people who stay awake can experience confusion, hallucinations, and memory loss.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Drowsiness.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At the peak there is strong sedation, slurring and unsteadiness. Hands fumble with phones or cups, and walking is risky, with a tendency to sway or bump into furniture.

Emotionally it can feel disinhibited, giggly or confused, with memory gaps that can cover hours. Some people feel a pleasant, floating detachment, others anxious and disoriented.

Z-drugs rarely stop breathing on their own, but combined with alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines or gabapentinoids they can cause dangerous sedation and slowed breathing. Complex sleep behaviors, such as sleep-driving, cooking or walking outside with no memory, carry an FDA boxed warning. Anyone who cannot be woken, or is vomiting while drowsy, needs emergency help.

Medically, the peak is when sleep pressure is strongest. People who resist sleep at this point may walk, eat, talk, drive, or send messages with no memory afterward ('complex sleep behaviors'), which led to a 2019 FDA boxed warning for zolpidem, zaleplon, and eszopiclone.

Combined with alcohol, opioids, or other sedatives, breathing can slow dangerously, and amnesia is much more likely.

### Plateau (sleep)

The zopiclone plateau carries sedation through most of the night. Sleep is usually deep and the body heavy and still, though some people wake with a bitter, dry mouth or a sense of strange, vivid dreams. Breathing is slow and regular.

If awake, thinking is foggy and memory poor, and people may do things they do not remember, from eating to sending messages. Judgment and balance are significantly impaired. Some people sleepwalk or wake confused, unsure where they are.

Next-day drowsiness and impaired driving are documented, especially in older adults, who clear the drug more slowly and are more prone to falls during night-time bathroom trips.

### Comedown (6-8 h total)

Waking is often groggy, with a bad, metallic taste, a heavy head and a slight unsteadiness. The first hours of the morning can feel slow and muffled, and some people notice a mild headache or dry mouth.

Mood can be flat or irritable, and some people feel low or anxious in the mornings after regular use. Rebound insomnia is common when stopping. Many describe a 'zopiclone hangover': thick-headed mornings with poor focus.

Dependence and withdrawal similar to benzodiazepines can develop with regular use, including anxiety, insomnia, shakiness and, rarely, seizures; tapering with a doctor is safer than stopping suddenly.

As levels fall, most people sleep through the comedown. Those who wake early can feel groggy, unsteady, and slow, and next-morning impairment can affect driving, especially in women and older adults.

Nightly use can cause tolerance and rebound insomnia on stopping; some hypnotics (GHB, methaqualone, older drugs) cause severe dependence and dangerous withdrawal.

### After-effects (8-12 h)

Bitter taste, grogginess.

### Next day (next morning)

Next-morning impairment possible.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, hypnotics bring sleepiness and calm. At higher doses, or when the person stays awake, confusion, memory loss, hallucinations, and sleep-walking or sleep-driving become likely. With alcohol or opioids, or with drugs like GHB and methaqualone, effects can tip into unconsciousness, vomiting while unconscious, and stopped breathing.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, age, liver and kidney function, genetics, and especially other depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines) change the effect. Older adults and people with sleep apnea or lung disease are at higher risk. Stronger effects do not stop at more pleasure: they shade into blackouts, falls, and slowed breathing.

## Overdose note for Zopiclone (Imovane)

Overdose causes deep sedation; with other depressants, breathing can stop.
