# Alfentanil

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic fentanyl-family opioid; very potent full mu agonist used only in medicine.

**Brand and street names:** Alfenta, Rapifen.

## History and origins

Synthesized by Janssen in 1976.

## Medical use, then and now

Short procedures and anesthesia; acts within about a minute and wears off in minutes.

## How it entered recreational use

Misuse is mostly limited to healthcare workers with access, and is often fatal.

## Legal status

US Schedule II.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Alfentanil is a hospital drug. Patients describe a fast, heavy wave of warmth and sleepiness, sometimes a pleasant floaty detachment before anesthesia, rather than a long euphoria. Its effects are extremely short and very strong.

People who received it rarely remember much; its main 'feeling' is sudden sedation and dulled pain, sometimes with nausea or itch on waking.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (under 1 min)

Given IV, it acts within about a minute.

### Come-up (seconds)

There is little or no come-up.

### Peak (1-5 min)

The peak comes within minutes: heavy sedation and strong pain relief; breathing can stop and chest muscles can stiffen, which is why it is used only with airway support.

### Plateau (5-30 min (remifentanil ends within minutes of stopping))

The effect lasts only as long as the drug is being given (remifentanil) or for minutes to an hour.

### Comedown (under 1 h)

It wears off quickly.

### After-effects (1-4 h)

Patients may feel groggy, nauseous, or have more pain as it ends.

### Next day

Little next-day effect after single medical use.

## How the route changes the experience

- [Injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous)](../routes/injected/alfentanil.md): IV in hospitals, with monitoring.

Not covered: No oral, intranasal (outside research), rectal, transdermal, or smoked medical forms.

## Overdose note for Alfentanil

Extremely potent; breathing can stop and chest-wall rigidity occur within a minute. Used only where airway support is ready.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md).
