# Etomidate

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Intravenous anesthetic acting at GABA-A receptors.

**Brand and street names:** Amidate; in East Asia, etomidate-laced vapes called 'space oil' or 'zombie vapes'.

## History and origins

Introduced by Janssen in 1972 and valued in emergencies because it keeps blood pressure stable. Since about 2021, etomidate has been illegally added to e-cigarettes in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore and elsewhere.

## Medical use, then and now

Emergency intubation and anesthesia in unstable patients.

## How it entered recreational use

Etomidate vapes cause jerking, unsteadiness and 'zombie-like' behavior; several Asian governments urgently controlled it.

## Legal status

Prescription-only in the US; controlled in China (2023), Hong Kong, Taiwan and Singapore.

## What the products look and feel like

Medical etomidate is a clear liquid in vials. Illicit etomidate vapes look like standard e-cigarette pods. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Etomidate produces sudden sleepiness and loss of control, often with involuntary muscle jerks (myoclonus).

Repeated use suppresses the adrenal glands' production of cortisol, which can be life-threatening.

Vapes containing it look like ordinary e-cigarettes, and users often do not know what is in them.

Tolerance to dissociatives builds quickly, and ketamine tolerance leads people to use more, raising the risk of bladder and memory damage.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 s)

Etomidate is an intravenous anesthetic used in emergency rooms to put people to sleep quickly without lowering blood pressure much. In recent years, etomidate added to vape liquids ('space oil', 'zombie' vapes) has spread in parts of East and Southeast Asia. Given into a vein, it acts within seconds, often with a brief burning in the arm and twitching of the muscles.

Mentally the onset is a sudden drop toward sleep. Vaped, people describe a fast, heavy, dreamy dissociation, dizziness, and a feeling of the body becoming far away, followed by confusion.

Muscle jerks (myoclonus) are a known effect and can look like a seizure. In vapes the amount and purity are unknown, and reports describe people collapsing, losing control of movement or behaving like 'zombies.'

Etomidate reaches the brain within a minute; it keeps blood pressure stable, unlike propofol.

Involuntary muscle jerks are common at induction.

### Come-up (about 1 min)

Sedation and muscle jerks.

### Peak (1-5 min)

At its peak in medical use, the person is unconscious with relaxed muscles and fairly stable blood pressure. In vape misuse, people may sway, stagger, twitch and fall, with slurred speech and blank, slow responses. Breathing can slow and the eyes may roll or stare blankly.

Mentally there is little memory of the peak. Those misusing it report a detached, numbing trance with confusion and loss of time. Some describe a heavy, sinking blackness and later remember little but the first rush.

Etomidate suppresses the adrenal glands' ability to make cortisol, the body's stress hormone. With repeated use, this can lead to low blood pressure, low sodium, weakness and even life-threatening adrenal crisis.

At the peak, sedation is deep and breathing can stop.

Etomidate blocks 11-beta-hydroxylase, the adrenal enzyme that makes cortisol; repeated exposure from vapes has caused adrenal insufficiency with weakness, low blood pressure and low sodium.

### Plateau (3-10 min)

Medically the effect is brief, and it is usually given once. Repeated vaping keeps people dizzy, unsteady, twitchy and drowsy, with nausea and vomiting and a risk of falling. Hands may tremble and twitches can continue.

Mentally repeated use keeps a person in a foggy, disconnected state, with poor memory and slowed thinking, and craving can drive frequent use throughout the day. Concentration and school or work performance often suffer.

Long-term misuse has been linked to adrenal failure, involuntary movements, psychiatric symptoms and dependence. Medical teams need to know about etomidate use because it changes how they treat low blood pressure.

### Comedown (under 1 h)

Waking from medical etomidate is fairly quick, sometimes with nausea, vomiting and muscle aches. After vape misuse people may feel weak, faint, nauseated and shaky. Some feel lightheaded on standing, a possible sign of low cortisol, and appetite may be poor.

Mentally there can be confusion, low mood, anxiety and strong cravings, especially after frequent use. Sleep can be disrupted and irritability common.

People who stop after long misuse may need doctors to check and support adrenal function, because sudden adrenal failure can be dangerous. Counseling and addiction services help with the craving. Families can help by watching for fainting, vomiting or extreme weakness.

Its effect ends within minutes after a single dose.

Hong Kong and mainland China report psychiatric symptoms and dependence in young people who vape etomidate.

### After-effects (hours)

Confusion and fatigue.

### Next day (none)

With repeated use: adrenal suppression and weakness.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses dissociatives bring numbness, lightness, mild euphoria and dreamy detachment. At higher doses, the body feels far away, movement becomes difficult, and vision and sound distort. At still higher levels, people lose contact with surroundings entirely, cannot move or respond, and are at risk of injury, vomiting and choking, with breathing danger when combined with depressants.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.
