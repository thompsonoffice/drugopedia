# Etomidate

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Intravenous anesthetic acting at GABA-A receptors.

**Brand and street names:** Amidate; in East Asia, etomidate-laced vapes called 'space oil' or 'zombie vapes'.

## History and origins

Introduced by Janssen in 1972 and valued in emergencies because it keeps blood pressure stable. Since about 2021, etomidate has been illegally added to e-cigarettes in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore and elsewhere.

## Medical use, then and now

Emergency intubation and anesthesia in unstable patients.

## How it entered recreational use

Etomidate vapes cause jerking, unsteadiness and 'zombie-like' behavior; several Asian governments urgently controlled it.

## Legal status

Prescription-only in the US; controlled in China (2023), Hong Kong, Taiwan and Singapore.

## What the products look and feel like

Medical etomidate is a clear liquid in vials. Illicit etomidate vapes look like standard e-cigarette pods. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Etomidate produces sudden sleepiness and loss of control, often with involuntary muscle jerks (myoclonus).

Repeated use suppresses the adrenal glands' production of cortisol, which can be life-threatening.

Vapes containing it look like ordinary e-cigarettes, and users often do not know what is in them.

Tolerance to dissociatives builds quickly, and ketamine tolerance leads people to use more, raising the risk of bladder and memory damage.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the injected (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 s)

IV etomidate causes unconsciousness within about a minute; vaped, effects come within seconds to minutes.

Etomidate reaches the brain within a minute; it keeps blood pressure stable, unlike propofol.

Involuntary muscle jerks are common at induction.

### Come-up (about 1 min)

Sedation and muscle jerks.

### Peak (1-5 min)

The peak lasts minutes.

At the peak, sedation is deep and breathing can stop.

Etomidate blocks 11-beta-hydroxylase, the adrenal enzyme that makes cortisol; repeated exposure from vapes has caused adrenal insufficiency with weakness, low blood pressure and low sodium.

### Plateau (3-10 min)

Short plateau.

### Comedown (under 1 h)

Recovery within minutes to an hour.

Its effect ends within minutes after a single dose.

Hong Kong and mainland China report psychiatric symptoms and dependence in young people who vape etomidate.

### After-effects (hours)

Confusion and fatigue.

### Next day (none)

With repeated use: adrenal suppression and weakness.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses dissociatives bring numbness, lightness, mild euphoria and dreamy detachment. At higher doses, the body feels far away, movement becomes difficult, and vision and sound distort. At still higher levels, people lose contact with surroundings entirely, cannot move or respond, and are at risk of injury, vomiting and choking, with breathing danger when combined with depressants.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Etomidate

Risks include stopped breathing, falls, and adrenal crisis with repeated use.
