# Phendimetrazine (Bontril)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Amphetamine-like appetite suppressant (anorectic), schedule III.

**Brand and street names:** Bontril PDM, Bontril Slow-Release; generic tablets and extended-release capsules.

## History and origins

Phendimetrazine was approved in 1959. It is a prodrug converted in the body into phenmetrazine (Preludin), a stimulant widely misused in the 1950s and 1960s, including by the Beatles in Hamburg, and withdrawn from the US market.

## Medical use, then and now

Short-term treatment of obesity alongside diet and exercise.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused for weight loss and energy; less common than phentermine.

## Legal status

US Schedule III; its active metabolite phenmetrazine is Schedule II.

## Immediate-release vs extended-release

Immediate-release tablets are taken several times a day; extended-release capsules last most of the day. Capsules should be swallowed whole.

## What the pills feel like

Generic tablets and capsules vary by manufacturer. Stimulant drugs sold on the street, including counterfeit Adderall, are often contaminated with methamphetamine or fentanyl.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Phendimetrazine feels more amphetamine-like than diethylpropion because phenmetrazine releases dopamine and noradrenaline: alert, energetic and not hungry, with some euphoria.

Side effects include insomnia, a racing heart, dry mouth and irritability.

Misuse can cause anxiety, high blood pressure and psychosis, and it carries the anorectic risks of pulmonary hypertension and valve disease.

Tolerance to the euphoria builds quickly, while the heart and blood-pressure effects remain. Regular users often chase the first high and use more often.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (about 1 h)

Phendimetrazine (Bontril) is an appetite suppressant that the body converts into phenmetrazine, the former diet drug Preludin, which was widely misused in the 1950s and 60s. Swallowed, it builds slowly with a dry mouth, faster pulse and a fading appetite. People may notice a light, buzzing energy in the limbs and a quicker pace of walking and talking.

Mentally the onset is an energetic, mildly euphoric lift, with talkativeness and drive. Its conversion into phenmetrazine means effects keep building for a while after the first signs.

Restlessness, a jittery chest and sweating can appear early. Taking more before it has fully arrived is a common mistake.

Phendimetrazine is converted to phenmetrazine, which releases dopamine and noradrenaline.

Heart rate and blood pressure rise.

### Come-up (1-2 h)

Appetite falls and alertness rises.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At its peak the body is stimulated: raised blood pressure and pulse, dry mouth, sweating, constipation and little desire for food. Some feel hot and flushed, and the heartbeat may feel strong in the chest or throat. Pupils widen and the mouth feels parched, with sweating on the palms and forehead.

Mentally there is energy, confidence and focus, sometimes with mild euphoria; higher amounts bring anxiety, irritability and racing thoughts. People may become chatty and confident, then edgy and impatient as the day goes on.

Risks include heart rhythm problems, high blood pressure, and rarely psychosis or lung hypertension. It should not be combined with MAO-inhibitor antidepressants.

At high levels, euphoria, agitation and psychosis can occur.

Phenmetrazine itself was withdrawn because of misuse.

### Plateau (4-12 h)

The plateau lasts several hours, longer with extended-release capsules. The body stays wired and not hungry. The mouth stays dry, headaches and constipation build, and meals are often forgotten entirely. Some notice a slight tremor and a raised pulse even while resting.

Mentally people feel driven and busy, sometimes tense or irritable. Some feel focused and upbeat; others feel tense, wound-up and unable to sit still. Irritability often builds toward evening.

Late doses cause insomnia. Blood pressure and pulse remain raised, and combining with caffeine or other stimulants adds strain on the heart. Regular blood pressure checks are part of safe use.

### Comedown (6-14 h)

As it fades, tiredness, headache and hunger return. A heavy, washed-out fatigue can arrive in the evening along with strong hunger. Muscles may feel tired and achy from hours of restless energy.

Emotionally low mood or irritability may follow. Some feel anxious or teary, and sleep may be poor. Motivation can drop sharply.

Because its active form is phenmetrazine, misuse potential is significant and tolerance develops. Stopping after long or high use can bring days of exhaustion and low mood, so it should be done with a doctor's guidance. Its misuse history is why it is tightly controlled, and taking more than prescribed to restore the lift is a warning sign of dependence.

Effects fade over several hours.

Insomnia is common.

### After-effects (next night)

Insomnia if taken late.

### Next day

Possible next-day fatigue.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses stimulants increase alertness, focus, energy and confidence and reduce appetite. At higher doses euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, jaw clenching, sweating, a pounding heart and anxiety increase. At very high levels, paranoia, hallucinations, overheating, chest pain, seizures, heart attack and stroke can occur.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Phendimetrazine (Bontril)

Overdose causes agitation, high blood pressure, fast heart rate, hallucinations and seizures.
