# Diphenoxylate (Lomotil)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Any opioid can cause fatal overdose, and street drugs are frequently contaminated with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Synthetic opioid related to meperidine that acts mostly in the gut; combined with atropine to discourage misuse.

**Brand and street names:** Lomotil, Lonox; difenoxin (Motofen) is its active metabolite.

## History and origins

Developed by Janssen in 1956.

## Medical use, then and now

Diarrhea.

## How it entered recreational use

Rarely misused because atropine causes unpleasant effects and little reaches the brain at medical amounts.

## Legal status

US Schedule V (with atropine).

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

At medical amounts there is little or no high. Misuse mostly produces atropine effects: dry mouth, flushed hot skin, fast heartbeat, blurry vision, and confusion, with only a faint opioid calm.

It is mainly dangerous for children, who can be poisoned by small numbers of tablets.

Tolerance changes everything here. Someone who has never used opioids feels far stronger effects, more nausea, and much higher overdose risk than someone who uses daily, and people who use every day often say they mainly feel 'normal' rather than high.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (15-45 min)

Swallowed, effects begin in 45 to 60 minutes.

### Come-up (30-60 min)

Mild drowsiness, dry mouth.

### Peak (1-2 h)

Slight calm; atropine effects dominate.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

Several hours of drowsiness.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

Fades over 3 to 4 hours.

### After-effects (6-24 h)

Dry mouth, constipation.

### Next day

Normal next day.

## Overdose note for Diphenoxylate (Lomotil)

Children can have delayed breathing suppression up to 12 hours or more after swallowing; atropine poisoning too.

Read the shared page: [What an opioid overdose feels and looks like, and what to do](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). Also see [naloxone (Narcan)](../narcan/naloxone.md).
