# Datura and brugmansia (jimsonweed, angel's trumpet)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Nightshade plants containing scopolamine, atropine and hyoscyamine.

**Brand and street names:** Jimsonweed, thorn apple, devil's trumpet, moonflower; brugmansia (angel's trumpet), toé.

## History and origins

Datura has been used in rituals worldwide and as a poison. In 1676, British soldiers in Jamestown were poisoned after eating it, giving 'jimsonweed' its name.

## Medical use, then and now

None as a plant; its alkaloids are medicines.

## How it entered recreational use

Occasionally eaten by teenagers or added to brews; widely regarded as dangerous.

## Legal status

Legal to grow in most places.

## What the products look and feel like

A large plant with trumpet-shaped white or purple flowers and spiky seed pods.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Datura causes days of true delirium: people talk to people who are not there, cannot recognize where they are, and do not remember the experience clearly.

The amount of alkaloids varies hugely between plants, parts and seasons, so poisoning is unpredictable.

Hot, dry skin, wide pupils, inability to urinate, very fast heartbeat, seizures and death occur.

Tolerance develops to some effects, but delirium risk remains.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Datura (jimsonweed, angel's trumpet, thorn-apple) is a plant whose seeds, flowers and leaves contain powerful anticholinergic chemicals, scopolamine and atropine. The amount in a plant varies enormously. Effects come on over an hour or more: a very dry mouth, flushed hot skin, dilated pupils, blurred vision and a racing heart.

Mentally the onset is a creeping confusion and restlessness, sometimes mistaken for a 'trip'. There is little euphoria; most people feel disoriented and uneasy.

Datura poisoning is a serious emergency, with high risk of heart rhythm problems, seizures, very high temperature, coma and death. The old medical saying 'hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter' describes it.

Tropane alkaloids are absorbed from the gut over hours.

Classic anticholinergic signs: 'hot as a hare, blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter.'

### Come-up (1-3 h)

Dry mouth, wide pupils, confusion.

### Peak (3-12 h)

At the peak the body is hot, red and dry, with wide pupils that won't focus, a pounding heart, urinary retention and unsteady, jerky movements. Speech may be slurred and nonsensical. Breathing may quicken, muscles may twitch, and the person may be unable to stand.

Mentally there is deep delirium: vivid, realistic hallucinations, conversations with people who aren't there, complete loss of awareness of surroundings, and often terror. People may wander, undress or injure themselves. People often pick at invisible threads or insects.

Emergency care is needed. Medical teams may use physostigmine, cooling and sedatives, and support breathing and heart rhythm.

At peak, delirium with realistic hallucinations; overheating, arrhythmias and seizures can be fatal.

Physostigmine, given in hospitals, can reverse severe anticholinergic delirium.

### Plateau (12-48 h)

The plateau lasts many hours and sometimes days. The body stays hot, dry and racing, with blurred vision. Muscles may jerk, urine may be retained painfully, and the skin stays dry and burning. Breathing may become rapid.

Mentally delirium, confusion and frightening hallucinations persist. People may pick at invisible objects, talk to unseen figures or lose all sense of time and place. Fear and agitation often return in waves. Some talk nonstop in mumbles.

Accidents and deaths from wandering, falls and drowning are common in datura poisonings. Someone should stay with the person and call emergency services; restraint should only be done by medical teams.

### Comedown (1-3 days)

The comedown can last days, with exhaustion, blurred vision, dry mouth and constipation. Pupils may stay wide for days, making reading and bright light difficult, and constipation and urinary problems may persist. Muscles may ache and sleep may be broken by vivid dreams.

Mentally confusion, memory gaps and fear are common, and most people never want to repeat it. Some feel deeply shaken or traumatized, and nightmares or flashbacks of hallucinations may occur. Memory gaps are often permanent.

Lingering vision problems and psychiatric symptoms can last weeks. Mental-health follow-up helps people process the experience. Poison centers can advise on plants found in gardens.

Delirium can last days.

Vision may remain blurred for days because pupils stay dilated.

### After-effects (days)

Blurred vision for days.

### Next day (days)

Memory gaps.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses deliriants cause drowsiness, dry mouth and a fast heartbeat. At higher doses, confusion, blurred vision and hallucinations start. At very high levels, full delirium with realistic hallucinations, overheating, seizures and dangerous heart rhythms occur; no one finds this pleasant.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.
