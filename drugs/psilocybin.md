# Psilocybin mushrooms

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Tryptamine prodrug converted to psilocin, a 5-HT2A agonist.

**Brand and street names:** Magic mushrooms, shrooms, Psilocybe cubensis and other species; synthetic psilocybin (COMP360) in trials.

## History and origins

Mushrooms have been used in Mesoamerican rituals for centuries. R. Gordon Wasson's 1957 Life magazine article introduced them to the West; Albert Hofmann isolated psilocybin in 1958. Oregon (2020) and Colorado (2022) legalized supervised use.

## Medical use, then and now

Clinical trials for depression, end-of-life anxiety and addiction; FDA gave breakthrough designation for treatment-resistant depression (2018) and major depression (2019).

## How it entered recreational use

Widely used recreationally and in religious or 'microdosing' contexts.

## Legal status

US Schedule I (Oregon and Colorado allow licensed use); UN 1971 Schedule I (the mushrooms themselves are not explicitly listed).

## What the products look and feel like

Dried mushrooms with thin stems and brown caps; they may bruise blue. Never eat wild mushrooms without expert identification. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Psilocybin feels earthier and more emotional than LSD, often described as warm, introspective and 'organic', with flowing visuals.

The experience is shorter than LSD and more body-centered, with nausea at first.

The main dangers are psychological: overwhelming fear, confusion and accidents. Poisonous look-alike mushrooms (such as Galerina or Amanita species) can be deadly.

Tolerance to classic psychedelics builds within a day and lasts several days, with cross-tolerance between them.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (20-60 min)

Psilocybin mushrooms usually begin with body sensations: stomach discomfort or nausea, yawning, chills, and a heavy or tingly feeling in the limbs. Pupils widen.

Mentally there is a sense of anticipation or unease as perception begins to change: colors brighten, edges soften, and laughter can come easily.

Some feel a wave of anxiety, which often eases if the setting is calm.

Psilocybin is converted in the gut and liver to psilocin, which crosses into the brain.

Nausea and yawning are common early as psilocin acts on gut serotonin receptors.

### Come-up (1 h)

Visuals and emotional shifts build.

### Peak (1.5-3 h)

At the peak, the body may feel heavy, warm or tingly, and the stomach may still be uneasy. Visuals include breathing surfaces, patterns and color intensification.

The emotional tone is often introspective and emotional, with feelings of connection, awe, sadness or fear. Time distortion is strong.

Risks include panic, frightening experiences and accidents. Some species can be confused with poisonous mushrooms.

At the peak, 5-HT2A activation reduces activity in the brain's default mode network, linked to loss of the usual sense of self.

Heart rate and blood pressure rise modestly; the main risks are psychological.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The plateau continues visual and emotional effects, with a heavier body and slower movement.

Mentally people drift in thought, often feeling connected, vulnerable or tearful.

Coordination is reduced, so walking around unsupervised can be risky.

### Comedown (4-6 h)

The comedown is gentle, with tiredness and relaxation.

Emotionally people feel reflective, calm or emotionally open.

Rarely, lasting anxiety, low mood or perception changes occur.

Psilocin's half-life is about 2 to 3 hours.

Clinical studies report lasting mood benefits in some participants, but also rare lasting anxiety or HPPD.

### After-effects (6-8 h)

Reflective afterglow, tiredness.

### Next day

Usually little next-day effect.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses psychedelics bring brighter colors, mood changes and new ways of thinking. At higher doses, strong visual changes, loss of the usual sense of self, and time distortion occur. At very high levels, people may lose touch with reality entirely, and panic, confusion and risky behavior become more likely.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: body size, tolerance, genetics, mood and setting, health conditions, medicines (especially antidepressants and lithium), and product purity all change the effect.

## Overdose note for Psilocybin mushrooms

Physical toxicity is low; risks are panic, accidents, and poisoning from misidentified mushrooms.

Read the shared page: [Psychedelic safety](../safety/psychedelic-safety.md).
