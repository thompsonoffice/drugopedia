# Tizanidine (Zanaflex)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Mixing depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sleep drugs, muscle relaxants) is the leading cause of deaths with these drugs. Some must never be stopped suddenly after daily use.

## At a glance

Alpha-2 adrenergic agonist muscle relaxant, related to clonidine.

**Brand and street names:** Zanaflex tablets and capsules, Sirdalud (outside US).

## History and origins

Developed by Sandoz and approved in the US in 1996 for spasticity from multiple sclerosis and spinal cord injury.

## Medical use, then and now

Spasticity; off-label for sleep, headaches and opioid withdrawal symptoms.

## How it entered recreational use

Misused for sedation and sometimes to ease opioid withdrawal.

## Legal status

Prescription-only, not controlled.

## What the pills feel like

Per DailyMed, Zanaflex tablets are uncoated, white and scored; Zanaflex capsules are two-piece hard gelatin capsules, in light blue, blue-and-white, or blue with a white stripe depending on strength. Tablets and capsules absorb differently with food.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Tizanidine's character is a heavy, quiet sedation with dry mouth and a noticeable drop in blood pressure; some describe it as a 'clonidine-like' calm, others as simply knocked out.

Standing up quickly can cause fainting; the heart can slow.

Stopping suddenly after daily use can cause rebound high blood pressure and fast heartbeat.

Tolerance to the pleasant and sleep effects builds with regular use, but tolerance to slowed breathing builds more slowly, so the danger stays. Daily use can lead to dependence, and stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Tizanidine (Zanaflex) acts on the same brain receptors as the blood-pressure drug clonidine, and its onset feels like the body's engine being throttled down. Within an hour a heavy, warm drowsiness spreads quickly from the head into the limbs, the mouth goes very dry, and standing up can bring a sudden lightheaded swoon as blood pressure drops.

Mentally the onset is a quiet, sinking calm. Anxiety and racing thoughts fade, and many people feel peaceful, slowed and detached, as if the volume of the world has been turned down. There is little giddy euphoria, more a soft, sleepy stillness.

Muscles, especially those with spasticity, slacken noticeably. Some feel their heart slowing, a heaviness in the chest, cold hands or a faint tingling.

Medically, the onset reflects absorption from the gut and entry into the brain. Tizanidine is processed by CYP1A2 and has a short half-life of about 2.5 hours; tablets and capsules are not interchangeable when taken with food. Muscle relaxants work mostly through central sedation rather than acting on the muscles themselves (dantrolene is the exception), so drowsiness usually arrives together with the loosening of tension.

Clinicians warn that reaction time slows early, often before the person notices, and that dizziness on standing is common, especially in older adults.

### Come-up (about 1 h)

Drowsiness and lightheadedness rise quickly.

### Peak (1-2 h)

At the peak the body feels leaden and deeply relaxed, sometimes almost pinned down by drowsiness. Dizziness, weakness and near-fainting on standing are common, and the dry mouth can be intense enough that the tongue sticks. Vision can blur slightly.

Emotionally the peak is tranquil and foggy, with a dreamy, half-asleep quality. Some find it pleasant and soothing; others find it unpleasantly heavy or dysphoric. Vivid dreams or brief hallucinations at the edge of sleep can occur.

Low blood pressure and a slow heart rate are the main risks, worse with alcohol, other depressants, blood-pressure medicines, or ciprofloxacin and fluvoxamine, which block its breakdown. Fainting, very slow pulse or extreme sedation needs urgent care.

Medically, the peak is when sedation and dizziness are strongest. Several muscle relaxants have anticholinergic or blood-pressure effects as well, which can cause dry mouth, blurred vision, a fast or slow heartbeat, or fainting.

Alone they rarely stop breathing in healthy adults, but combined with opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol, or gabapentinoids they add to respiratory depression; carisoprodol in particular was a common part of the deadly 'holy trinity' with opioids and benzodiazepines.

### Plateau (2-4 h)

The plateau of tizanidine is short but heavy. Many people simply fall asleep, often deeply. Those who stay awake feel leaden and weak, with limbs that seem too heavy to lift, a very dry mouth, and a dizziness that surges whenever they stand. The blood pressure drop can make the edges of vision go gray or sparkly on standing, a warning sign of fainting.

Mentally the plateau feels quiet, slowed and dreamy, with thoughts moving lazily and attention drifting. Some find this tranquil and pleasant; others find it oppressive, like being trapped in a too-heavy body. Brief dreamlike images or mild hallucinations at the edge of sleep can happen.

Because the effect wears off within a few hours, some people take repeated doses through the day, which builds dependence on its blood-pressure effects. Combining with alcohol, other sedatives, other blood-pressure medicines, or drugs that block its breakdown (such as ciprofloxacin or fluvoxamine) can cause dangerously low blood pressure and slow heart rate.

### Comedown (3-6 h total)

As tizanidine fades, the heaviness lifts fairly quickly and can be replaced by a slightly jittery, tired-but-wired feeling. Muscle stiffness and spasm return, sometimes sharply, and the mouth stays dry. Some feel a headache or a pounding pulse as blood pressure rebounds upward.

Emotionally, the calm can give way to irritability, restlessness or anxiety, especially after frequent use through the day. Sleep may be broken and unrefreshing.

After regular daily use, stopping suddenly can cause rebound high blood pressure, fast heartbeat, tremor, sweating and anxiety, sometimes severe. It should be tapered under medical guidance, and long-term use needs liver monitoring.

As levels fall, sedation lifts and muscle tension may return. Short-acting drugs wear off within hours, while drugs with active metabolites (carisoprodol to meprobamate) linger.

People who use carisoprodol, meprobamate, or baclofen daily can develop dependence; stopping suddenly can cause anxiety, insomnia, hallucinations, and seizures, and abrupt baclofen withdrawal can be life-threatening.

### After-effects (6-12 h)

Dizziness, weakness.

### Next day (little)

Usually little next day.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, muscle relaxants mainly cause drowsiness and eased tension. At higher doses, dizziness, heavy sedation, poor coordination, blurred vision, and confusion grow, and some (carisoprodol, meprobamate, orphenadrine, baclofen, tizanidine) bring additional dangers: seizures, heart rhythm problems, very low blood pressure, or coma. With opioids, alcohol, or benzodiazepines, breathing can slow and stop.

Higher doses mainly deepen sedation and drop blood pressure and heart rate, raising the risk of fainting and falls.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, age, liver and kidney function, genetics, and especially other depressants (alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines) change the effect. Older adults and people with sleep apnea or lung disease are at higher risk. Stronger effects do not stop at more pleasure: they shade into blackouts, falls, and slowed breathing.

## Overdose note for Tizanidine (Zanaflex)

Overdose causes severe drowsiness, very low blood pressure, a slow heart, and coma. Ciprofloxacin and fluvoxamine (which block CYP1A2) can raise levels dangerously.
