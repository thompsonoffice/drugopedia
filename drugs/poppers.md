# Poppers (alkyl nitrites)

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Street products are often mislabeled or contaminated, including with fentanyl.

## At a glance

Volatile alkyl nitrites that widen blood vessels.

**Brand and street names:** Amyl nitrite (medical), isobutyl and isopropyl nitrite; sold as 'room odorizer' or 'leather cleaner'; brands such as Rush and Locker Room.

## History and origins

Amyl nitrite was first made by Antoine Balard in 1844 and used from 1867 by Thomas Lauder Brunton for angina; crushable glass ampules that 'popped' gave the name. Recreational use spread in 1970s disco and gay club culture.

## Medical use, then and now

Historically angina; amyl nitrite was part of the old cyanide antidote kit.

## How it entered recreational use

Inhaled for a brief head rush and muscle relaxation, often in sexual settings.

## Legal status

Amyl nitrite is prescription-only in the US; Congress banned butyl nitrite in 1988 and other nitrites for inhaling in 1990, but products are sold as cleaners. The FDA warned about poppers in 2021.

## What the products look and feel like

Small brown or clear glass bottles of strong-smelling liquid. The liquid burns skin and eyes. Street and online products are frequently mislabeled; lab testing services have found products with different drugs or none of the advertised one.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Poppers cause a sudden head rush, warmth, flushing and a pounding heart lasting a couple of minutes, with relaxation of smooth muscle.

Headache and dizziness are common; blood pressure can fall enough to faint.

With erectile dysfunction medicines (sildenafil, tadalafil) blood pressure can drop dangerously, risking heart attack or stroke. Swallowing the liquid can be fatal (methemoglobinemia), and some users develop eye damage (poppers maculopathy).

Tolerance can develop with regular use, but the danger of sudden death does not decrease.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the inhaled and smoked route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (seconds)

Poppers are small bottles of liquid alkyl nitrites (amyl, butyl, isopropyl) whose fumes are sniffed. They act within seconds: blood vessels widen suddenly, causing a hot rush to the head and face, a pounding heart, flushing, a throbbing headache and a warm, loose feeling in the body.

Mentally the onset is a short, intense head-rush with giddiness and a feeling of dizziness or disorientation. Some feel a brief euphoria and heightened sensuality, which is why poppers are often used during sex and on dancefloors.

Blood pressure drops sharply, which can cause fainting. Combined with erection medicines like sildenafil (Viagra) or tadalafil, the drop can be dangerous or fatal.

Alkyl nitrites are absorbed through the lungs within seconds and release nitric oxide, which relaxes smooth muscle in blood vessels.

Blood pressure falls and heart rate rises reflexively; flushing and headache follow.

### Come-up (10-30 s)

A rush of warmth and flushing.

### Peak (30-90 s)

At the peak, which lasts only a minute or two, the body feels flushed and hot, with a racing pulse, throbbing head, relaxed smooth muscles and sometimes a fluttering in the chest or dizziness on standing. Some feel a brief pressure in the eyes and ears.

Mentally the peak is a brief, heady blur, with time seeming to slow and a sense of release or abandon. Thinking is hazy and judgment loosens. Many describe the head-rush as a short wave of warmth flooding through.

Swallowing poppers liquid is a medical emergency and has caused deaths. Spilling it on skin causes burns. Poppers can trigger methemoglobinemia, where blood carries less oxygen, giving the lips and skin a blue-grey color.

At peak, low blood pressure can cause fainting; combined with PDE5 inhibitors (Viagra, Cialis) it can cause collapse, heart attack or stroke.

Nitrites oxidize hemoglobin to methemoglobin, which cannot carry oxygen; swallowing poppers can be fatal and needs methylene blue treatment.

### Plateau (1-2 min)

Effects fade fast, so people often sniff repeatedly. Repeated use brings ongoing headache, flushing, dizziness, and sometimes a crusty rash around the nose and lips. The face may stay red and the heart keep racing, and some feel briefly faint when standing.

Mentally repeated sniffing keeps a person in short bursts of giddiness and sensuality, but the novelty fades quickly and headache grows. Some people find the headache spoils the experience and stop.

Some poppers, especially isopropyl nitrite, have been linked to 'poppers maculopathy', damage to the center of the retina that causes blurred vision or blind spots. This is very important for people with existing sight problems. Keeping the bottle away from children and never swallowing it are key safety rules.

### Comedown (2-5 min)

As the effect fades, people may feel tired, headachy and lightheaded, sometimes with a dull ache behind the eyes or a sore nose. Headaches can linger for hours, and some people feel queasy or slightly short of breath. A crusty yellow rash may develop around the nose.

Mentally there may be mild fogginess or flatness. Some feel slightly dull or irritable as the headaches persist, and sleep may be lighter.

People with heart problems, glaucoma, anemia or low blood pressure face higher risks. Any vision changes after use need an eye check. Blue lips, breathlessness or confusion after use need emergency care, as these can signal methemoglobinemia. Store bottles upright and cool.

Effects end within minutes.

Repeated use is linked to poppers maculopathy (central vision loss), especially with isopropyl nitrite.

### After-effects (minutes)

Headache.

### Next day (none)

Usually none.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower exposures inhalants cause brief dizziness, giggling and lightheadedness. At higher exposures, confusion, hallucinations, loss of coordination and unconsciousness occur, and the risk of suffocation and sudden heart rhythm death rises sharply.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance, body size, genetics, heart health, mental health history, sleep, heat, hydration, and other drugs (including antidepressants and alcohol) all change the effect. Street products vary hugely in strength and content.

## Overdose note for Poppers (alkyl nitrites)

Dangers include fainting, dangerously low blood pressure, methemoglobinemia (blue lips, breathlessness), and death if swallowed.
