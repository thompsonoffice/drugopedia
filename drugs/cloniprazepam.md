# Cloniprazepam

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Never stop a daily benzodiazepine suddenly: withdrawal can cause seizures. Counterfeit pills often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines.

## At a glance

Designer ('research chemical') benzodiazepine; little or no human research.

**Brand and street names:** Street: pellets.

## History and origins

Sold online from about 2016; a prodrug of clonazepam.

## Medical use, then and now

None approved.

## How it entered recreational use

Sold online or in fake tablets.

## Legal status

Controlled in the UK.

## What the pills feel like

Forms vary by country and manufacturer; see the brand list above. Ask a pharmacist to describe the product you have.

**Touch cannot confirm what a pill is.** Different manufacturers make the same medicine in different shapes and sizes, and counterfeit pills, such as fake 'M30' oxycodone 'blues' or fake Xanax 'bars', are pressed to imitate real ones and often contain fentanyl or designer benzodiazepines. Safer ways to check: ask a pharmacist (they can identify a pill from its imprint), use a pill-identifier app or a screen-reader-friendly service such as Be My Eyes or Seeing AI to read the imprint aloud and look it up on DailyMed or Drugs.com, keep medicines in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## What it feels like

### The character of its effects

Cloniprazepam's character is clonazepam-like long calm.

Combined with opioids or alcohol, it can stop breathing, and memory loss is common.

Daily use leads to dependence, and stopping suddenly can cause seizures.

Tolerance to the calming and sleep effects of benzodiazepines builds within weeks, while memory loss and the danger of mixing with alcohol or opioids remain. People taking them daily often feel little except relief from anxiety, and stopping suddenly can cause seizures.

### Timeline

Times below are typical for the oral (swallowed) route and vary a lot between people. Other routes change the timing; see the route pages.

### Onset (30-60 min)

Cloniprazepam is a prodrug that the body converts into clonazepam and related compounds; it appeared as a designer drug around 2015. It comes on gradually as conversion happens, with a heavy clonazepam-like calm. Unlike prescription tablets, the strength of each pill, powder or blotter is a guess.

Mentally a numb, steady quiet. The early effect is often mistaken for 'not working', which is how people end up taking more.

Its delayed onset invites redosing. Reaction time and coordination are already impaired here, even when the person feels only relaxed.

From a medical point of view, the onset reflects how quickly the drug is absorbed and enters the brain. Like other benzodiazepines, it enhances the effect of the calming neurotransmitter GABA at GABA-A receptors and is cleared mostly by the liver. As it binds to GABA-A receptors, brain activity slows: anxiety eases, muscles relax, and reaction time lengthens.

Clinicians notice early signs such as slower speech, drooping eyelids, and reduced coordination. People often underestimate how impaired they already are at this stage, which is why driving is dangerous.

### Come-up (after onset)

As it builds, a clonazepam-like long calm starts to appear.

### Peak (1-3 h)

At its peak sedated and clumsy. Walking becomes swaying, hands fumble, speech thickens and the eyes grow heavy and unfocused.

Mentally forgetful and indifferent. Blackouts are the hallmark of designer benzos: people may talk, text, drive or argue and later remember nothing, and others often cannot tell anything is wrong.

Dangerous with depressants. With opioids, alcohol, gabapentinoids or other sedatives, breathing can slow dangerously; give naloxone in any suspected overdose because fentanyl is so often present, and call emergency services.

Medically, the peak is when sedation, muscle relaxation, and memory impairment are strongest. Benzodiazepines can block the formation of new memories (anterograde amnesia), so people may act normally yet remember nothing later.

On their own, benzodiazepines rarely stop breathing in healthy adults, but combined with opioids or alcohol they greatly deepen respiratory depression. The FDA added its strongest boxed warning about this combination in 2016.

### Plateau (several hours)

The plateau is long, like clonazepam. Balance stays poor and falls, burns and other injuries are common during this stretch.

Mentally dull. People frequently forget they already took a dose and take another, stacking the effect.

Builds up with repeated use. Driving, swimming, cooking or caring for children during this time is unsafe.

### Comedown (long)

The comedown is slow. The body feels heavy and dull, sometimes with a headache, dry mouth and nausea.

Emotionally rebound anxiety. Rebound anxiety, irritability, low mood and worry about what happened during a blackout are common.

Withdrawal resembles clonazepam's. Daily use builds dependence within weeks; stopping suddenly can cause seizures and psychosis, so withdrawal should be managed by a doctor, often by switching to a long-acting prescribed benzodiazepine and tapering slowly.

As levels fall, the calming effect wears off. With short-acting benzodiazepines, rebound anxiety and insomnia can be sharp. Long-acting ones and their active metabolites can linger for days, causing next-day drowsiness and impaired driving.

In people who take them daily, falling levels can trigger withdrawal, which in severe cases includes seizures and delirium. This is why stopping should be a slow medical taper, not a sudden stop.

### After-effects (hours after)

Drowsiness and patchy memory.

### Next day

Daily use causes dependence; stopping suddenly can cause seizures.

### Lower vs higher doses (described without amounts)

At lower doses, benzodiazepines mainly ease anxiety and muscle tension, with mild calm and slight drowsiness. At higher doses, sedation, clumsiness, slurred speech, disinhibition, and memory loss grow, and blackouts become likely. As effects keep rising, people become very difficult to wake, confused, and unable to stand, and when opioids or alcohol are involved, breathing slows and can stop: these are overdose warning signs.

With designer benzodiazepines, nobody can know what dose they are actually taking, so the jump from 'mild' to 'blackout' or overdose can happen without warning.

### Why the same amount affects people differently

Why the same amount affects people differently: tolerance builds quickly, liver function and age change how fast benzodiazepines are cleared (older adults clear many much more slowly), and many medicines block the CYP3A4 enzyme that breaks down alprazolam, midazolam, and triazolam. Alcohol and opioids multiply the sedation and breathing risk. Stronger effects shade into blackouts, falls, and, in combinations, stopped breathing.

## Overdose note for Cloniprazepam

Dangerous with opioids, alcohol, and other sedatives; overdose signs include being unable to wake and slowed breathing.
