# Vicodin

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Counterfeit pills made to look like these brands often contain fentanyl or other drugs.

## What it contains

The opioid or main ingredient is [Hydrocodone](../drugs/hydrocodone.md), combined with: acetaminophen.

## History

Introduced by Knoll in 1978; one of the most prescribed drugs in the US through the 2000s. Hydrocodone combinations were moved from Schedule III to Schedule II in 2014. The brand name was discontinued but the name is still used for generics.

## How the combination changes the feel

The acetaminophen adds pain relief that works differently from the opioid, so many people feel more relief with a milder high than from hydrocodone alone. Some describe a clean, warm, chatty lift with mild itch.

## What it feels like over time

Times vary between people; this describes swallowed tablets.

### Onset

Vicodin, the name most people still use for hydrocodone-acetaminophen, starts with a mild warmth and loosening. Older Vicodin formulas contained more acetaminophen per tablet than Norco, so the non-opioid share of the pain relief was larger.

Mentally the onset is a gentle, relaxed lift, softer and slower than Percocet.

Itch and mild nausea are common early signs, as with all hydrocodone products.

### Peak

At the peak people feel warm, heavy and comfortable, with tingling skin and slowed breathing.

Body sensations at the peak include a pleasant pins-and-needles tingle in the hands and feet, a warm flush across the face and chest, and pinpoint pupils that make light feel softer. Many feel a mild queasiness when they stand or walk.

The emotional tone is calm, content and dreamy. People familiar with both often say Vicodin felt milder and sleepier than Percocet.

Breathing is most suppressed now. Because each tablet also contains acetaminophen, taking more to chase or extend the effect quietly raises the risk of liver injury, which may cause no symptoms for a day or more. Alcohol, fasting, liver disease, and other acetaminophen-containing products (many cold and sleep medicines) make it worse.

### Plateau

The plateau is drowsy and warm. Limbs stay heavy, eyelids droop, and light nodding can occur, especially lying down. Itching continues in patches, and the mouth stays dry.

Mentally people drift into relaxed, unfocused daydreams. Time stretches and motivation fades; the mood is calm and passive rather than euphoric.

Constipation and slow urination build, and breathing stays slowed. Combining with alcohol, benzodiazepines or sleep aids makes this phase dangerous.

### Comedown

The comedown is gradual: heaviness gives way to tiredness, a dull headache, and the return of aches.

Mood is slightly flat and sluggish; regular users feel restless, irritable and craving.

Constipation and nausea can linger. Each repeat dose adds both opioid dependence risk and acetaminophen exposure to the liver.

## What the added ingredients bring, and their risks

**Acetaminophen (paracetamol):** adds pain and fever relief without extra sedation, but it is the most common cause of acute liver failure in the US. Liver damage can happen silently over a day or two, with few symptoms until it is severe, and is more likely with alcohol, fasting, liver disease, or taking more than one acetaminophen product at once (many cold medicines contain it). In 2011 the FDA limited the acetaminophen in prescription combinations and added a boxed warning about liver injury. Overdose needs hospital treatment even if the person feels fine.

## What it feels like to the touch

The Vicodin brand has been discontinued and no current brand label is listed on DailyMed, so its exact original appearance could not be verified there. Most pills people call 'Vicodin' today are generic hydrocodone-acetaminophen, which varies by maker; many are white, oblong, scored tablets that feel firm and chalky, with a score line you can feel. Identify by imprint, not by feel. **Touch, shape and imprint cannot confirm what a pill is.** Counterfeit copies of these brands are pressed to look and feel real and often contain fentanyl, xylazine or designer benzodiazepines. Ask a pharmacist, read the imprint with Be My Eyes or Seeing AI and look it up on DailyMed, keep medicine in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## Overdose and safety

Opioid overdose signs: cannot be woken, slow or stopped breathing, blue lips, pinpoint pupils. Call 911 and give [naloxone](../narcan/naloxone.md). Read [opioid overdose](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). If too much was taken, the acetaminophen can destroy the liver even when the person seems fine; go to an emergency department or call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) right away, since an antidote (acetylcysteine) works best early.

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
