# Fioricet with Codeine

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Counterfeit pills made to look like these brands often contain fentanyl or other drugs.

## What it contains

The opioid or main ingredient is [Codeine](../drugs/codeine.md), combined with: acetaminophen, butalbital (a barbiturate), caffeine.

## History

Fioricet (butalbital, acetaminophen, caffeine) is a long-used tension-headache medicine; the codeine version adds an opioid.

## How the combination changes the feel

A layered effect: barbiturate sedation and muscle looseness, a caffeine lift, and codeine warmth. People describe it as more 'drunk-feeling' than plain codeine.

## What the added ingredients bring, and their risks

**Acetaminophen (paracetamol):** adds pain and fever relief without extra sedation, but it is the most common cause of acute liver failure in the US. Liver damage can happen silently over a day or two, with few symptoms until it is severe, and is more likely with alcohol, fasting, liver disease, or taking more than one acetaminophen product at once (many cold medicines contain it). In 2011 the FDA limited the acetaminophen in prescription combinations and added a boxed warning about liver injury. Overdose needs hospital treatment even if the person feels fine.

**Butalbital:** a barbiturate that adds sedation and muscle relaxation. Barbiturates have a narrow safety margin, cause dependence, and withdrawal can cause seizures. Frequent use causes rebound (medication-overuse) headaches.

**Caffeine:** adds a mild lift and helps headache relief; it can cause jitteriness, fast heartbeat, and trouble sleeping, and can mask how sedated someone is.

## What it feels like to the touch

Capsules. Touch and shape cannot confirm what a pill is: ask a pharmacist, use a pill-imprint app or a fentanyl test strip.

## Overdose and safety

If too much was taken, the acetaminophen can destroy the liver even when the person seems fine; go to an emergency department or call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) right away, since an antidote (acetylcysteine) works best early. Butalbital adds barbiturate sedation; see [depressant mixing](../safety/depressant-mixing.md). 

For the full drug page, see [Codeine](../drugs/codeine.md).

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
