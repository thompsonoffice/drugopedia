# Norco

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Counterfeit pills made to look like these brands often contain fentanyl or other drugs.

## What it contains

The opioid or main ingredient is [Hydrocodone](../drugs/hydrocodone.md), combined with: acetaminophen.

## History

Launched in the 1990s by Watson with less acetaminophen per tablet than older Vicodin. Became a common hydrocodone brand after the 2011 FDA acetaminophen limits.

## How the combination changes the feel

Feels like other hydrocodone-acetaminophen products; the opioid part sets the mood effects.

## What it feels like over time

Times vary between people; this describes swallowed tablets.

### Onset

Norco comes on with hydrocodone's mellow, rounded warmth: a softening in the stomach and chest and a gentle heaviness in the arms. The acetaminophen quietly adds relief of aches and fever-type pain without any feeling of its own.

Mentally the onset is calm and easygoing: tension fades and people describe feeling relaxed and cozy rather than energized.

Itch, often on the face, neck and back, is common and can arrive early, and some people feel queasy, especially on an empty stomach.

### Peak

At the peak the body feels warm, heavy and soothed, with waves of tingling and itch. Breathing slows.

The emotional tone is relaxed, friendly contentment, dreamier and less sparkling than Percocet. Many describe Norco as 'smooth' and 'sleepy-happy'.

Breathing is most suppressed now. Because each tablet also contains acetaminophen, taking more to chase or extend the effect quietly raises the risk of liver injury, which may cause no symptoms for a day or more. Alcohol, fasting, liver disease, and other acetaminophen-containing products (many cold and sleep medicines) make it worse.

### Plateau

During the plateau the body settles into a heavy, warm stillness, as though sinking into the chair. Itch comes in waves across the nose, neck and back, and people scratch without noticing. Eyelids feel weighted, and nodding (drifting off and jerking awake) can appear. Nausea may return with movement.

Mentally the plateau is quiet, dreamy and unhurried. Conversations slow, attention drifts to music or a screen, and brief half-dream images appear when the eyes close. The mood is passive contentment, sleepier than the more alert plateau people describe with Percocet, and an hour can pass unnoticed.

The gut is almost still, so fullness and constipation build and urination can be slow to start. The face may be flushed and slightly sweaty. Breathing remains slowed, and falling asleep in this phase after alcohol or a sleeping pill is a common overdose setting.

### Comedown

Norco's comedown is usually gentle: the warmth recedes from the limbs, itch fades, and the body is left tired, a little stiff and achy. Pain being treated returns gradually rather than all at once.

Emotionally people tend to feel flat, sleepy or slightly foggy rather than sharply irritable, though those who use regularly feel restlessness and a pull to take another tablet.

Headache, dry mouth, nausea and constipation can linger. With dependence, yawning, sneezing, watery eyes and craving start here. Repeating doses to stay comfortable builds tolerance and adds acetaminophen to the liver with each tablet.

## What the added ingredients bring, and their risks

**Acetaminophen (paracetamol):** adds pain and fever relief without extra sedation, but it is the most common cause of acute liver failure in the US. Liver damage can happen silently over a day or two, with few symptoms until it is severe, and is more likely with alcohol, fasting, liver disease, or taking more than one acetaminophen product at once (many cold medicines contain it). In 2011 the FDA limited the acetaminophen in prescription combinations and added a boxed warning about liver injury. Overdose needs hospital treatment even if the person feels fine.

## What it feels like to the touch

Per a DailyMed Norco label (Watson), the yellow Norco tablet is capsule-shaped (oblong with rounded ends), bisected with a score line on one side you can feel with a fingernail, and debossed 'NORCO 539' on the other. Other Norco strengths have been different colors; check the imprint rather than relying on color. The tablets are uncoated and slightly chalky. Generic hydrocodone-acetaminophen tablets vary by maker, and many are white oblong scored tablets that feel very similar. **Touch, shape and imprint cannot confirm what a pill is.** Counterfeit copies of these brands are pressed to look and feel real and often contain fentanyl, xylazine or designer benzodiazepines. Ask a pharmacist, read the imprint with Be My Eyes or Seeing AI and look it up on DailyMed, keep medicine in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## Overdose and safety

Opioid overdose signs: cannot be woken, slow or stopped breathing, blue lips, pinpoint pupils. Call 911 and give [naloxone](../narcan/naloxone.md). Read [opioid overdose](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). If too much was taken, the acetaminophen can destroy the liver even when the person seems fine; go to an emergency department or call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) right away, since an antidote (acetylcysteine) works best early.

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
