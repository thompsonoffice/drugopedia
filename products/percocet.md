# Percocet

> Educational harm-reduction content. Not medical advice, not a guide to use. Nothing here gives amounts, preparation, or sourcing. Counterfeit pills made to look like these brands often contain fentanyl or other drugs.

## What it contains

The opioid or main ingredient is [Oxycodone](../drugs/oxycodone.md), combined with: acetaminophen.

## History

Introduced by Endo in 1976. Its name and its blue imprinted tablets ('perc 30s' in street slang refers to counterfeit oxycodone) are among the most counterfeited in the US.

## How the combination changes the feel

A brighter, more energetic euphoria than hydrocodone combinations for many people, with the acetaminophen adding pain relief.

## What it feels like over time

Times vary between people; this describes swallowed tablets.

### Onset

Percocet starts much like immediate-release oxycodone: within a short time, a light, clean warmth rises in the chest and stomach and the shoulders loosen. The acetaminophen adds a quieter, separate relief: dull aches, headache and inflammation-type pain soften alongside the opioid effect, so many patients feel their pain ease more completely than with oxycodone alone.

Mentally the onset is oxycodone's bright, upbeat lift: anxiety fades, people feel friendlier and more motivated, and the world seems manageable. Acetaminophen adds no buzz or sedation of its own, so the mental character is almost entirely oxycodone's.

Early body signs include a dry mouth, narrowing pupils, a mild itch on the nose or face, and sometimes slight queasiness. Compared with Norco or Vicodin, people often describe Percocet's onset as crisper and less itchy.

### Peak

At the peak, warmth spreads through the whole body, limbs feel pleasantly weighted, and skin may tingle. Breathing slows. Pain relief is at its fullest, and for many this is the most comfortable they have felt in days.

The emotional tone is oxycodone's confident, glowing euphoria: chatty, affectionate and alert before drowsiness arrives. Many people who have taken both say Percocet feels 'sharper' and 'more energetic' than hydrocodone combinations, with a cleaner mental lift.

This is the most dangerous window for breathing, especially with alcohol, benzodiazepines or sleep aids. Because each tablet also contains acetaminophen, taking more to chase or extend the effect quietly raises the risk of liver injury, which may cause no symptoms for a day or more. Alcohol, fasting, liver disease, and other acetaminophen-containing products (many cold and sleep medicines) make it worse.

### Plateau

The plateau settles into warm, drowsy contentment. Nodding can appear, eyelids get heavy, and itching comes and goes. The stomach slows and constipation begins.

Mentally people become quieter and more inward; daydreams come easily and time stretches. The bright lift fades into calm.

Breathing stays depressed, and sleep in this phase, especially with other sedatives, is a common setting for overdose.

### Comedown

The comedown is relatively quick, as with oxycodone: warmth drains away, aches return, and the body feels ordinary or slightly sore.

Emotionally, people describe a flat, irritable letdown that makes taking more tempting.

Taking another tablet to avoid the comedown stacks both opioid and acetaminophen. In dependent people, yawning, runny nose, goosebumps and craving begin here.

## What the added ingredients bring, and their risks

**Acetaminophen (paracetamol):** adds pain and fever relief without extra sedation, but it is the most common cause of acute liver failure in the US. Liver damage can happen silently over a day or two, with few symptoms until it is severe, and is more likely with alcohol, fasting, liver disease, or taking more than one acetaminophen product at once (many cold medicines contain it). In 2011 the FDA limited the acetaminophen in prescription combinations and added a boxed warning about liver injury. Overdose needs hospital treatment even if the person feels fine.

## What it feels like to the touch

Per the current DailyMed Percocet label (SpecGx), the brand comes in four tablets that differ by strength: a pink oval, a blue round tablet (the only one with a bisect score line, on the back), a peach oval, and a yellow capsule-shaped tablet. All are debossed 'PERCOCET' with a strength number on the other side, so the imprint is a raised-in groove you can sometimes trace with a fingertip. They are small, firm and uncoated with a slightly chalky feel. Generic oxycodone-acetaminophen (such as Endocet) varies by maker, often white round or oblong tablets. The street 'perc 30' or 'M30' blue pill is not Percocet at all but a counterfeit of plain oxycodone. **Touch, shape and imprint cannot confirm what a pill is.** Counterfeit copies of these brands are pressed to look and feel real and often contain fentanyl, xylazine or designer benzodiazepines. Ask a pharmacist, read the imprint with Be My Eyes or Seeing AI and look it up on DailyMed, keep medicine in labeled pharmacy bottles, and use fentanyl test strips on anything not from a pharmacy.

## Overdose and safety

Opioid overdose signs: cannot be woken, slow or stopped breathing, blue lips, pinpoint pupils. Call 911 and give [naloxone](../narcan/naloxone.md). Read [opioid overdose](../safety/opioid-overdose.md). If too much was taken, the acetaminophen can destroy the liver even when the person seems fine; go to an emergency department or call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) right away, since an antidote (acetylcysteine) works best early.

## Getting help

SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357** (free, confidential, 24/7, English and Spanish). In an emergency, call **911**.
